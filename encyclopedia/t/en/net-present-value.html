Net Present Value

ENGLISHNet Present Valuereference
فارسیارزش فعلی خالصEstablished equivalent
کوردیبەهای ئێستای پوختProposed equivalent — not standard
Category
Fundamental Analysis
Level
Professional

Simple definition

What a project's future cash is worth today, minus what you must spend to get it. Positive means the project adds value at the rate of return you demanded.

Professional definition

The sum of all cash flows of a project or asset, each discounted to the present at the required rate of return, with the initial outlay entered as a negative flow. The decision rule is to accept positive values and reject negative ones at that rate.

Technical definition

A linear function of the cash flow vector given a discount rate, and the one capital-budgeting criterion that is additive across projects and consistent with maximising current wealth. Its sign depends entirely on the rate used: the same cash flows can be accepted at one hurdle rate and rejected at another, which is why the rate, not the arithmetic, is where appraisal disputes belong.

Academic definition

The present value of an investment's expected cash inflows less the present value of its outflows, discounted at the opportunity cost of capital for equivalent risk.

Mechanism

Each future amount is divided by the compounded discount rate, so distant flows shrink geometrically while the upfront cost does not shrink at all. The criterion therefore compares like with like on today's scale, and its sign answers one question: does this stream beat the return available elsewhere on equally risky money?

In practice

Who uses it
Corporate investment committees, project appraisal and infrastructure teams, lessors and lessees, and any analyst comparing competing uses of capital.
Why it matters
It is additive, scale-aware and gives a single currency answer, so projects of different sizes and lives can be ranked without a reinvestment fudge.
Limitations
It depends completely on the forecast and the chosen rate, and it says nothing about timing flexibility, abandonment options or the credibility of the cash flows.

Common mistakes

  • Using one company-wide hurdle rate for projects of very different risk.
  • Double-counting inflation in the cash flows and the rate.
  • Ignoring the option to delay, which a positive value does not capture.

Examples

numeric

A machine costs 1,000 and returns 400 a year for three years. At a 10 per cent hurdle rate the net present value is minus 5.3, so the project is rejected. At 8 per cent it is plus 30.8 and accepted. Nothing about the machine changed.

Given
  • Initial outlay at t = 0 is 1,000
  • Cash inflow of 400 at the end of years 1, 2 and 3
  • Hurdle rates tested: 10% and 8%
Steps
  1. At 10%: 400/1.10 + 400/1.21 + 400/1.331 = 363.64 + 330.58 + 300.53 = 994.74
  2. NPV = 994.74 - 1,000 = -5.26
  3. At 8%: 370.37 + 342.94 + 317.53 = 1,030.84
  4. NPV = 1,030.84 - 1,000 = +30.84
NPV = -5.26 at 10%; NPV = +30.84 at 8%

The decision flipped on a two-point move in the rate. Any appraisal that reports a single value without the rate is incomplete.

case

Two divisions of one firm submit projects with identical cash flows. Head office applies a single group hurdle rate, so both are approved, although one division sells to governments on long contracts and the other to consumers in a cyclical market.

Mathematical foundation

NPV = \sum_{t=0}^{n} \frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^t} = -C_0 + \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{CF_t}{(1+r)^t}

Variables

CF_tNet cash flow at the end of period t, negative for outflowscurrency
C_0Initial outlay at time zerocurrency
rRequired rate of return, or hurdle rate, for risk of this kindratio per period
nNumber of periods in the project's lifeperiods

Procedure

List every cash flow by period with its sign, choose a discount rate appropriate to the risk of these flows, divide each flow by one plus that rate raised to its period number, and add the results including the negative outlay. Accept if the total is positive at that rate.

Worked example

Given
  • C_0 = 1,000
  • CF_1 = CF_2 = CF_3 = 400
  • r = 0.10
Steps
  1. 400 / 1.10 = 363.64
  2. 400 / 1.10^2 = 330.58
  3. 400 / 1.10^3 = 300.53
  4. Sum of inflows = 994.74
  5. NPV = 994.74 - 1,000
NPV = -5.26

Interpretation

A positive value is the amount of present-day wealth the project adds over and above earning the required rate. Zero means it earns exactly that rate. The number is in currency, so values from different projects can be added.

Assumptions

  • Cash flows are assumed to arrive at period ends; mid-period or continuous conventions give different figures for the same project.
  • One discount rate is applied to every period, which assumes the risk of the stream is constant over its life.
  • Real cash flows must be discounted at a real rate and nominal flows at a nominal rate; mixing the two double-counts or omits inflation.
Limits of the formula
  • The sign of the result is driven by the chosen rate, so appraisal always needs the rate disclosed and a sensitivity range.
  • Ignores managerial flexibility — the option to delay, expand or abandon — which real-options methods were developed to price.

Commonly confused with

Internal Rate of Return

Net present value answers how much value is added at a rate you specify. The internal rate of return answers which rate would make that value zero. One needs a rate as input; the other produces one as output.
