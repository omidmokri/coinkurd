Simple definition

A date when tokens held back for a team, early backers or a treasury become transferable. The amount that could be sold rises, even though no new tokens were created.

Professional definition

A scheduled release of previously non-transferable allocations under a vesting plan, usually a cliff followed by linear release. Unlocks raise circulating supply and therefore reported market capitalisation at an unchanged price, and they narrow the gap between market capitalisation and fully diluted valuation.

Technical definition

What matters is the unlocked amount relative to free float and to average daily traded volume, not the headline percentage of total supply. Enforcement varies: some schedules are locked in contracts, others rest on a promise and can be renegotiated. Recipients' cost basis differs from the market's, so their incentive to sell differs too. Unlocked is not sold — holders can hedge or borrow against an allocation well before the date, which is why price often moves ahead of it.

Academic definition

The scheduled conversion of restricted token allocations into transferable supply under a predetermined vesting schedule, increasing free float and circulating supply without any new issuance.

Mechanism

An unlock changes the supply side of the book, not the demand side. New sellers can appear who paid nothing like the market price, so the same bids now face more potential paper. Because the schedule is published, informed holders can sell exposure forward through perpetuals, options or private deals, which moves the effect away from the date itself.

In practice

Who uses it
Research and listing teams, risk managers sizing positions in young tokens, market makers quoting around the date, and allocation recipients themselves.
Why it matters
It is one of the few supply-side events whose timing and size are knowable in advance, so it can be measured rather than guessed.
Limitations
Schedules can change, enforcement differs, and nothing reveals whether recipients intend to sell. The date marks eligibility to sell, not selling.

Common mistakes

  • Quoting an unlock as a percentage of total supply instead of free float.
  • Assuming the whole unlocked amount reaches the market.
  • Trading the unlock date when the schedule has been public for months.

Examples

numeric

An unlock of 50 million tokens into a float of 400 million raises circulating supply by 12.5 percent. Against average daily volume of 10 million tokens, that is five days of the entire market's turnover.

Given
  • circulating supply before the unlock = 400,000,000 tokens
  • tokens unlocking = 50,000,000
  • average daily traded volume = 10,000,000 tokens
Steps
  1. supply increase = 50,000,000 / 400,000,000 = 12.5%
  2. days of volume = 50,000,000 / 10,000,000 = 5
+12.5% circulating supply, equal to 5 days of average volume

Illustrative figures. Days-of-volume is the more informative ratio, because it compares new potential supply with the market's actual capacity to absorb it.

market

Price frequently drifts lower in the weeks before a large scheduled unlock and stabilises after it. The information was public throughout, so what moved was positioning, not news arriving on the day.

Commonly confused with

Bitcoin Halving

A halving slows the creation of new units on a fixed protocol schedule. An unlock creates nothing; it releases units that already exist into tradable supply. Opposite direction, different mechanism.
