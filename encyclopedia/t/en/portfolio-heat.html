Simple definition

Everything you would lose if every open trade hit its stop at once, expressed as a percentage of your account. It is the one number that answers how exposed am I right now.

Professional definition

The sum across open positions of size times the distance from the current stop to the market, divided by account equity. It is the aggregate counterpart to per-trade risk: a two percent rule on each of eight open trades is a sixteen percent heat figure, not eight separate small risks.

Technical definition

Computed from live stop levels rather than entry risk, so it falls as stops are trailed and rises as positions are added. The figure is an arithmetic sum, which makes it the correct worst case only under two conditions that frequently fail together: that stops fill at their stated level, and that the trader has not implicitly assumed the losses would arrive separately. Under a common shock both assumptions break at once.

Academic definition

The aggregate conditional loss of a portfolio evaluated at its prevailing liquidation thresholds, normalised by equity, used as an ex ante constraint on total exposure.

Mechanism

Per-trade risk limits feel conservative because each one is small, but risk adds. The hidden step is correlation: eight long crypto positions are not eight bets, they are one bet on the same factor at eight times the size, so the losses arrive together rather than spread across months. A heat limit is the constraint that makes that arithmetic visible before the shock, not after.

In practice

Who uses it
Anyone holding more than one position at a time, risk managers setting a book-level limit, and developers coding a pre-trade check that rejects orders over the limit.
Why it matters
It is the only figure that prevents a sequence of individually reasonable trades from adding up to an unreasonable exposure.
Limitations
The sum assumes stops fill at their level and ignores correlation, so it understates a correlated shock with slippage. It also ignores leverage and liquidation thresholds.

Common mistakes

  • Counting entry risk after stops have moved, so the figure is stale.
  • Treating correlated positions as independent contributions.
  • Leaving positions with no stop out of the calculation entirely.

Examples

numeric

Five open positions each risk one percent of a 100,000 account, so nominal heat is five percent. In a weekend gap the stops fill 30 percent beyond their levels on average, and the realised loss is 6,500 rather than 5,000.

Given
  • equity = 100,000
  • 5 open positions, each with 1,000 of risk to its stop
  • all five are long the same sector
  • average slippage beyond the stop = 30 percent of the stop distance
Steps
  1. nominal heat = 5 x 1,000 = 5,000, that is 5.0 percent of equity
  2. all stops trigger in one event because the positions share a factor
  3. realised loss = 5,000 x 1.30 = 6,500
  4. realised heat = 6,500 / 100,000 = 6.5 percent
A 5.0 percent heat limit produced a 6.5 percent loss in one event.

The arithmetic sum is the worst case only if stops fill exactly. Correlation makes the whole sum arrive at once, and slippage makes it larger than the sum.

market

A trader trails stops on three winners until each sits above entry. Heat from those three is now negative in effect, which frees the risk budget for a fourth position without raising total open risk above the limit.

Mathematical foundation

H = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} q_i \, \lvert P_i - S_i \rvert}{E}

Procedure

For each open position, multiply its size by the distance from the current market price to its current stop. Sum those amounts across all positions, including any hedges with their sign, and divide by equity. Recompute after every fill, every stop adjustment and every material move in equity, and treat a position with no stop as risking its full notional unless a liquidation level is used instead.

Worked example

Given
  • equity = 100,000
  • position A: 1,000 of risk to stop
  • positions B to E: 1,000 each
  • no hedges
Steps
  1. total open risk = 5 x 1,000 = 5,000
  2. H = 5,000 / 100,000 = 0.05
H = 5.0 percent of equity at risk across five open positions.

Interpretation

The figure is the loss if every stop is hit at its level. Read it as a floor on the bad case, not a ceiling: slippage, gaps and funding can all push the realised number above it, and a single correlated event can deliver the whole sum in one session.

Assumptions

  • Every open position has a live stop and that stop can be filled near its level. A position without a stop has no defined contribution and must be handled separately.
  • Stop distances are measured from the current market price, so the figure is a snapshot that goes stale immediately.
  • The arithmetic sum takes no account of correlation. It is the correct total loss when all stops trigger, and it overstates the expected loss when positions are genuinely independent.
  • Leverage and exchange liquidation levels are not represented. A levered book can be liquidated before its stops are reached.
Limits of the formula
  • Correlated positions make the sum arrive in a single event rather than spread over time, and slippage in that event pushes the realised loss above the nominal figure.
  • The measure says nothing about the probability of the loss, only its size under the stop assumption. Pairing it with a tail measure such as expected shortfall gives the missing half.
  • Hedges net out in the arithmetic even when basis risk means they will not net out in practice.

Commonly confused with

Position Sizing

Position sizing decides how large one trade may be. Portfolio heat decides how many such trades may be open at once. A sound per-trade rule with no heat limit still permits an account-threatening book.

Flagged by the editor as uncertain on a linguistic, mathematical or regulatory point.

The arithmetic is elementary; the term itself and the common two-percent-per-trade conventions around it are trading-industry practice rather than a standardised risk measure, and no registry source defines a specific heat limit. The slippage figure in the example is illustrative, not measured. Persian and Kurdish headwords are both proposals by this project, as no settled equivalent was found; practitioners often say the English term. Needs native-expert review.
