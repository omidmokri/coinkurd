Break-Even Stop

Category

Level
Foundation

Simple definition

Moving the stop up to your entry price once the trade has moved far enough in your favour. The trade can then no longer show a loss, but it can close for nothing.

Professional definition

A single, one-way adjustment of the stop to the entry price, triggered when unrealised profit reaches a stated threshold, usually expressed in multiples of the initial risk. It converts a trade with a defined loss into a trade with a defined cost, and it raises the probability of a flat outcome.

Technical definition

The rule needs two numbers: the trigger, as a multiple of initial risk, and the level the stop moves to. A true break-even level is the entry price adjusted for round-trip costs — fees both sides, expected slippage, and funding already accrued on a perpetual — because a stop sitting exactly at the entry price still closes the trade at a small loss.

Academic definition

A conditional, monotone revision of a position's liquidation threshold to its cost basis upon attainment of a specified unrealised gain, trading a reduction in loss exposure against an increased probability of premature exit.

Mechanism

The adjustment removes the left tail of a single trade and moves probability mass onto a zero outcome. Whether that helps depends on where price normally travels after the trigger: if winners routinely retrace past the entry before working, the rule cuts winners; if they rarely do, it removes losses almost for free. The adverse-excursion record is what decides it.

In practice

Who uses it
Traders managing a position they cannot watch continuously, prop desks with per-trade loss limits, and anyone coding a staged exit.
Why it matters
It caps the downside of a trade that has already shown it can move, and in doing so frees risk budget for other positions.
Limitations
The entry price has no structural meaning for the market, so a stop there is often inside normal noise, and after costs it is not actually break-even.

Common mistakes

  • Calling the trade risk-free when fees and funding are still owed.
  • Triggering the move at a fraction of initial risk and being stopped flat repeatedly.
  • Moving the stop on an unrealised gain with no trigger rule at all.

Examples

numeric

Long 10 units at 100.00, stop 98.00. Fees are 0.1 percent each way, so the round trip costs about 0.20 per unit. A stop at 100.00 closes the trade down 2.00 in total; the real break-even level is about 100.20.

Given
  • size = 10 units, entry = 100.00, initial stop = 98.00
  • fee = 0.1 percent of notional per side
  • entry notional = 1,000.00
Steps
  1. entry fee = 0.001 x 1,000 = 1.00
  2. exit fee at 100.00 = 0.001 x 1,000 = 1.00
  3. total round-trip cost = 2.00, which is 0.20 per unit
  4. break-even price = 100.00 + 0.20 = 100.20
A stop at the entry price realises a 2.00 loss; 100.20 is the level that realises zero.

On a perpetual contract, accrued funding is added to the same calculation and moves the level further with every funding interval.

market

A desk moves stops to entry at half the initial risk. Reviewing 200 trades, a third of former winners now close flat, while the loss total falls by a fifth. Expectancy drops. The rule was protective and expensive at the same time.

Commonly confused with

Stop-Loss

A stop-loss defines the loss accepted when the position is opened. A break-even stop is a later revision of that same order, conditional on the trade having moved favourably first.
