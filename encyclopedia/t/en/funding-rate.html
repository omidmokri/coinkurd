Funding Rate

ENGLISHFunding Ratereference
فارسینرخ فاندینگEnglish term retained

Category
Derivatives
Level
Professional

Simple definition

A small payment made every few hours between traders holding a perpetual contract. Whichever side is crowded pays the other, which keeps the contract's price near spot.

Professional definition

The periodic rate applied to open perpetual positions, derived from the premium of the contract over its spot index plus an interest-rate component. Positive funding means longs pay shorts, negative means shorts pay longs. It is charged on notional, so leverage magnifies it.

Technical definition

A venue-defined rate, recomputed each interval from a time-weighted premium index and an interest-rate term that is usually clamped within a band. It is an incentive, not a fee: the exchange routes the payment between traders. Because it is applied to notional at a discrete timestamp it can be avoided by closing beforehand, and persistent extreme funding is a crowding signal rather than a prediction.

Academic definition

A periodic bilateral transfer between long and short holders of a perpetual contract, parameterised by the contract's premium to a reference index, acting as the convergence mechanism in place of settlement.

Mechanism

Funding makes a crowded side pay rent. If the contract trades above its index, long positions bear a recurring cost and the trade of buying spot while shorting the contract becomes profitable, which sells the premium down. The mechanism works through cost and arbitrage incentives, not through any direct link to spot.

In practice

Who uses it
Perpetual traders sizing holding cost, delta-neutral desks earning funding, and analysts reading positioning.
Why it matters
It is the cost of carry for a leveraged position and one of the few public, near-real-time measures of which side of the market is crowded.
Limitations
Each venue defines its own formula, interval and caps, so published rates are not directly comparable. Extreme funding signals crowding, not direction, and can persist for days.

Common mistakes

  • Annualising an 8-hour rate as if the interval were the same everywhere.
  • Treating funding as a fee paid to the exchange.
  • Reading high positive funding as a reliable short signal.

Examples

market

A delta-neutral desk buys spot on one venue and shorts the perpetual on another to collect positive funding. The position is flat on price but not risk-free: the short leg needs margin, a transfer between venues takes time, and a withdrawal limit can trap the collateral.

numeric

A long holds 20,000 of notional through nine consecutive intervals at +0.02% each. It pays 4 per interval, 36 in total. Against 2,000 of margin at ten times leverage, that is 1.8% of the capital posted.

Given
  • position notional at each funding timestamp = 20,000
  • funding rate = +0.02% for every interval
  • nine consecutive intervals
  • margin posted = 2,000 (ten times leverage)
Steps
  1. one interval: 20,000 x 0.0002 = 4
  2. nine intervals: 4 x 9 = 36
  3. as a share of margin: 36 / 2,000 = 1.8%
36 paid in total, 1.8% of the margin posted

The general part is the arithmetic, not the 0.02% or the number of intervals in a day. Both the rate and the interval are venue-specific, and a constant rate across nine intervals is an assumption made to keep the example readable.

Mathematical foundation

f = P + \operatorname{clamp}(I - P,\ -c,\ +c), \qquad \text{Funding} = N \times f

IInterest-rate component per interval, a venue parameter standing for the cost difference between holding the quote currency and the base assetfraction

Procedure

A venue samples the gap between its perpetual's price and its spot index many times through the interval and averages those samples into a premium index. An interest-rate term is combined with it inside a clamp, and the result is usually capped overall. The published rate is then applied to the notional of every position open at the timestamp, and the amount is transferred from one side to the other.

Worked example

Given
  • P = +0.03% for the interval
  • I = 0.01% per interval
  • c = 0.05%
  • N = 20,000, position is long
Steps
  1. I - P = 0.01% - 0.03% = -0.02%
  2. -0.02% lies inside the clamp [-0.05%, +0.05%], so it passes through unchanged
  3. f = 0.03% + (-0.02%) = 0.01%
  4. Funding = 20,000 x 0.0001 = 2.00
f = +0.01% for the interval; the long pays 2.00 and the matched short receives it

Interpretation

A positive rate says the contract is trading above its index and long positions are paying to stay open. The magnitude is a cost per interval on notional. It describes current crowding and carry; it is not a forecast of price.

Assumptions

  • There is no universal funding formula. The structure shown -- a premium index plus a clamped interest-rate component, with an overall cap -- is the shape most venues share, but the sampling method, the interval length, the clamp width c, the cap and the exact definition of the premium all differ by exchange. Treating one exchange's published formula as the definition of funding is a category error: read the specification of the venue being traded.
  • Interval length is itself venue-specific. Eight hours is the most common convention, but one-hour and four-hour intervals and continuous accrual are all in use, so two rates cannot be compared without normalising for interval.
  • Some venues publish the realised rate for the interval just ended, others a predicted rate for the interval ahead. The two are not interchangeable and charts mix them.
  • The interest-rate component I is a venue parameter and is often a fixed constant rather than an observed market rate, so it carries no information about actual financing costs.
  • Which price is used for the premium index (last trade, mark, or an impact-weighted book price) and which index is used as spot are both choices made by the venue.
Limits of the formula
  • Because the rate is applied at discrete timestamps, a position closed just before a stamp pays nothing on most venues, so published rates overstate the cost actually borne by short-horizon traders.
  • Rates from different venues cannot be averaged or compared without normalising for interval and definition, which makes aggregate 'market funding' figures weaker than they look.
  • Funding measures crowding, not value. Extreme readings can persist for days while price continues in the same direction.

Basis

Basis is the gap itself, in price units, on a contract with an expiry. Funding is the recurring payment that substitutes for that expiry on a perpetual.
