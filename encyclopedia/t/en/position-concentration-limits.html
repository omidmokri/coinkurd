Position Concentration Limits

Simple definition

Rules capping how much of a portfolio any single holding, issuer or theme may occupy, written in advance so the cap is not decided while a position is winning.

Professional definition

Pre-set maximum weights per name, issuer, sector, venue or risk factor. They bound the loss a single failure can cause and the share of portfolio variance one position can contribute, independently of any view on that position's merits.

Technical definition

A limit is only meaningful net of correlation: two 5% positions correlated at 0.9 behave close to one 10% position, so limits written name by name can be evaded by holding several near-substitutes. Effective diversification is often summarised by the reciprocal of the sum of squared weights, which counts an unequally weighted portfolio as fewer positions than it holds. Limits also need a rule for a position that grows by appreciation rather than purchase, because that is how most breaches actually occur.

Academic definition

Ex-ante upper bounds on exposure to an individual position, issuer or risk factor, constraining both idiosyncratic loss and the single-position contribution to portfolio variance.

Mechanism

The loss a position can cause is its weight times its possible decline, so a cap on the weight is a cap on that loss. The protection fails when supposedly separate positions share a driver, because correlation turns several small limits into one large exposure. Limits therefore have to be written on the risk, not only on the name.

In practice

Who uses it
Risk officers, fund mandates, prime brokers setting client limits, and individual traders writing their own rules.
Why it matters
A cap decided in advance cannot be argued away by a position that is working, which is exactly when the pressure to raise it is strongest.
Limitations
Limits control the size of an exposure, not its quality. They can be evaded through correlated substitutes and through leverage inside a position that looks small as a share of capital.

Common mistakes

  • Writing limits per name while holding several names driven by one factor.
  • Having no rule for a limit breached by appreciation rather than purchase.
  • Setting the cap after the position has already grown past it.

Examples

numeric

Five positions weighted 40%, 20%, 20%, 10% and 10% have squared weights summing to 0.26, so the effective number of positions is about 3.8. The portfolio holds five names and diversifies like fewer than four.

Given
  • weights: 0.40, 0.20, 0.20, 0.10, 0.10
  • weights sum to 1
  • correlations between positions are ignored by this measure
Steps
  1. sum of squared weights = 0.16 + 0.04 + 0.04 + 0.01 + 0.01 = 0.26
  2. effective number of positions = 1 / 0.26 = 3.85
an effective 3.85 positions against a nominal five

Equal weights of 20% each would give 1 / 0.20 = 5.0, the maximum for five holdings. If the two 20% positions share a driver, the real figure is lower than 3.85.

market

A mandate caps any single name at 5%. The portfolio holds six names in one industry at 5% each. No limit is breached, and 30% of the portfolio moves on one industry's news. The limit was written on the wrong object.

Procedure

For a worst-case check, multiply each position's weight by the decline being tested; the product is the portfolio loss from that single event, and the limit is set so this stays inside tolerance. For a concentration check, square every weight, sum them, and take the reciprocal to get the effective number of equally weighted positions the portfolio behaves like.

Worked example

Given
  • weights 0.40, 0.20, 0.20, 0.10, 0.10
  • a 60% decline tested on the largest position
Steps
  1. largest-position loss = 0.40 x 0.60 = 0.24 of portfolio value
  2. sum of squared weights = 0.26
  3. effective number = 1 / 0.26 = 3.85
a 24% portfolio loss from one name's 60% decline, in a portfolio that diversifies like 3.85 equally weighted holdings

Interpretation

The first expression says how much one event can cost; the second says how much independent variety the weights actually provide. A high effective number is not safety: it is only an upper bound, because the measure takes no account of correlation.

Assumptions

  • Weights are measured on current market values and sum to one; a leveraged position's economic exposure can exceed its capital weight and must be entered on the exposure, not the cash committed.
  • The effective-number measure ignores correlation entirely. Equal weights give the maximum value, equal to the nominal count.
  • The worst-case expression tests one position at a time and says nothing about several positions declining together.
Limits of the formula
  • A portfolio can satisfy every single-name limit and still carry one economic exposure, if the names share a factor, a counterparty or a venue.
  • Neither expression is a risk model: both describe the weights, not the distribution of returns.

Commonly confused with

Position Sizing

Position sizing decides how large to open a trade given its risk. A concentration limit is a ceiling that binds regardless of how the size was computed, including when the position grows afterwards on its own.
