Foundation

Simple definition

The amount written on a security. For a bond it is what gets repaid at the end; for a share it is usually a token legal figure, unrelated to the share's worth.

Professional definition

The contractual reference amount of an instrument. For debt it is the redemption amount and the base for coupon calculation; for equity it is a nominal figure fixed in the company's constitution, often set very low and largely vestigial in modern practice.

Technical definition

A fixed denominational parameter, not a valuation. A bond trades at par only when its required yield equals its coupon rate; above that yield it trades at a discount and below it at a premium. The premium or discount is arithmetic — the fixed coupon and fixed redemption amount cannot adjust, so the price must — and carries no information about sentiment.

Academic definition

The stated nominal amount of a security, serving as the contractual base for interest accrual and redemption in debt instruments and as a legal capital reference in equity.

Mechanism

Par is the one number in the contract that cannot move. Everything that changes in the market — required yield, credit view, liquidity — has to show up somewhere, and the only place left is price. Hence a bond mechanically trades below par when yields exceed its coupon, with no reference to mood or momentum.

In practice

Who uses it
Bond desks quoting prices as a percentage of par, corporate secretaries recording legal capital, and anyone converting a quoted price into cash.
Why it matters
Prices are quoted against par, so par is the unit that turns a quote like 98.50 into an actual settlement amount.
Limitations
For shares, par value carries no valuation information at all, and its legal significance varies by company law and jurisdiction.

Common mistakes

  • Reading share par value as a floor under the share price.
  • Treating a bond at a discount as mispriced rather than simply low-coupon.
  • Confusing par with the amount originally raised.

Examples

market

A company's shares have a par value of 0.01 and trade in the hundreds. The par figure is a bookkeeping and legal artefact; it tells a reader nothing about the business or the share price.

numeric

A bond with par 1,000 is quoted at 98.50. The cash price is 985 before accrued interest. Quoted at 101.25 the cash price is 1,012.50 — the quote is always a percentage of par, never of the last trade.

Given
  • par = 1,000
  • quote A = 98.50 (percent of par)
  • quote B = 101.25
Steps
  1. A: 1,000 x 0.9850 = 985.00
  2. B: 1,000 x 1.0125 = 1,012.50
985.00 at a discount; 1,012.50 at a premium

Clean prices only; accrued interest is added separately under each market's convention.

Commonly confused with

Book Value

Par value is a fixed contractual or constitutional figure. Book value is an accounting measure of net assets that changes every reporting period.
