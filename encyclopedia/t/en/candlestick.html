Simple definition

A single mark on a chart that reports four prices for one period: where trading opened, the highest and lowest it reached, and where it closed.

Professional definition

The standard price glyph: a body spanning open to close, and wicks reaching out to the high and the low. Colour records whether the close finished above or below the open. All four values belong to one fixed interval, so a candle is a summary of that interval, not a sequence of events inside it.

Technical definition

A candle discards the path. Body length measures net directional result over the interval; wick length measures excursion that was rejected. An identical high-to-low range can come from a decisive trend bar or an indecisive doji, and only the body-to-wick split separates them. Because interval boundaries are a convention, the same tick data yields different candles under a different session open or timezone.

Academic definition

A four-value summary of an interval's trading — open, high, low and close — rendered so that net change and rejected range are separately legible.

Mechanism

The body is the net result of the interval: everything buyers and sellers did that was not undone by the close. The wicks are what was undone — prices reached and then given back. That is why the body carries the information. A long wick with a tiny body says the interval finished where it began despite travelling, which is a statement about rejection, not about direction.

In practice

Who uses it
Every chart reader; pattern researchers; and price-action methods that deliberately condition on the body rather than the wick.
Why it matters
It is the densest single glyph for one interval, showing net change and rejected range at the same time and on the same scale.
Limitations
It hides the order of trades inside the interval, and the boundaries are a convention. Named single-candle patterns have weak and highly specification-dependent evidence.

Common mistakes

  • Reading colour alone and ignoring how small the body is.
  • Comparing candle sizes across instruments without scaling by volatility.
  • Treating a named pattern as a signal with no context and no tested definition.

Examples

chart

A candle opens at 100, reaches 108, falls to 99 and closes at 101. The body is one point and the upper wick seven: buyers took price eight points up and kept almost none of it by the close.

numeric

For that candle the range is nine points but the body is one, so the body is about eleven percent of the range. Most of the interval's travel was given back, which is what the body share measures.

Given
  • O = 100
  • H = 108
  • L = 99
  • C = 101
Steps
  1. body = |101 - 100| = 1
  2. upper wick = 108 - max(100, 101) = 7
  3. lower wick = min(100, 101) - 99 = 1
  4. range = 108 - 99 = 9
  5. body share = 1 / 9
body = 1, upper wick = 7, lower wick = 1, body share = 0.111

Body share is a descriptive ratio, not an indicator with established thresholds.

Mathematical foundation

Procedure

Take the four values of one interval. Body is the absolute difference between close and open; the upper wick runs from the higher of open and close up to the high, the lower wick from the low up to the lower of open and close. The three parts always sum to the range.

Worked example

Given
  • O = 100
  • H = 108
  • L = 99
  • C = 101
Steps
  1. B = |101 - 100| = 1
  2. W_up = 108 - 101 = 7
  3. W_dn = 100 - 99 = 1
  4. check: 1 + 7 + 1 = 9 = H - L
B = 1, W_up = 7, W_dn = 1, R = 9

Interpretation

The decomposition separates what the interval kept from what it gave back. A large body relative to range means directional flow dominated; a small body with long wicks means it did not, whatever the colour.

Assumptions

  • The four values come from one venue and one fixed interval. Aggregated or cross-venue data can produce a high or low that never traded on the chart's own book.
  • Interval boundaries and timezone are part of the definition; moving the daily open changes every body and wick.
Limits of the formula
  • The decomposition is arithmetic and exact, but it says nothing about the order in which the high and the low were reached.
  • Body and wick lengths are in price units; comparing them across instruments requires normalising, for example by ATR.

Commonly confused with

Body Break (not a wick break)

A candlestick is the glyph and its arithmetic. Body break is a decision rule built on top of it: which part of the candle must finish beyond a level for a break to count.
