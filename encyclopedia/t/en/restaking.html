Simple definition

Using the same staked coins to secure more than one system at once, and collecting a reward from each. The same deposit now backs several promises.

Professional definition

Committing stake that already secures a base chain as collateral for additional services, each with its own slashing conditions. The return is additive while the risk is not: one deposit becomes answerable to several independent sets of rules.

Technical definition

The design is rehypothecation with on-chain enforcement. Because each service can slash the same underlying stake, exposures are stacked rather than diversified, and a fault in one service can reduce the collateral securing every other and the base chain with it. Correlation is the issue: services that look independent often share operators, clients and infrastructure, so the probability of a joint slashing event is higher than the product of the individual probabilities. Reward is paid for taking that correlated tail.

Academic definition

The reuse of bonded stake as collateral across multiple protocols with independent penalty conditions, producing additive yield and superadditive tail risk through correlated slashing exposures.

Mechanism

Capital is scarce and security is expensive, so letting one bond back several systems looks like pure efficiency. The hidden cost is that independence disappears. A penalty anywhere in the stack reduces the same collateral everywhere, and because operators and software are shared, the events that would trigger those penalties are not independent either. Yield is paid for bearing a tail that is hard to observe and harder to price.

In practice

Who uses it
Stakers seeking an extra return on bonded assets, operators running validator infrastructure, and new protocols that want security without bootstrapping their own token.
Why it matters
It lets a new service rent economic security immediately, and it pays existing stakers for capital that was already committed.
Limitations
Slashing conditions are set by each service, the correlation between them is unmeasured, and the design is recent enough that it has not been tested through a severe joint failure.

Common mistakes

  • Adding the rewards and ignoring that the risks do not simply add.
  • Assuming separate services fail independently when they share operators.
  • Treating a liquid restaking receipt as equivalent to the underlying stake.

Examples

numeric

Base staking pays 3 percent and two services add 2 and 1.5 points, so the quoted total is 6.5 percent. If any one of the three slashes 5 percent of the stake, a year of that extra reward is wiped out several times over.

Given
  • base staking reward = 3%/yr
  • service A = 2.0 points
  • service B = 1.5 points
  • a single slashing event = 5% of principal
Steps
  1. quoted total = 3.0 + 2.0 + 1.5 = 6.5%
  2. extra reward above base = 3.5 points per year
  3. 5% principal loss / 3.5 points = about 1.43 years of extra reward lost
  4. the same 5% applies to the whole stake, including the portion securing the base chain
A single 5% penalty costs roughly 17 months of the entire additional yield.

Slashing fractions and reward rates are protocol parameters; these are illustrative figures chosen to show the asymmetry.

crypto

Several services commit to independent security, but most of their operators run the same client software on the same hosting provider. A bug in that client produces penalties across services at the same moment, against one pool of stake.
