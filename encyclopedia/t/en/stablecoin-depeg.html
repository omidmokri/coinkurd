Stablecoin Depeg

Simple definition

When a token designed to hold a fixed value trades away from it. The size of the gap measures how far the market doubts the mechanism meant to hold it.

Professional definition

A sustained deviation of a pegged token's market price from its reference value, caused by a break somewhere in the arbitrage loop that normally closes the gap. The mechanism of failure depends on the collateral model, so a depeg in a fiat-reserved token, a crypto-collateralised one and an algorithmic one are three different events wearing one name.

Technical definition

Each model fails at a specific link. Fiat-reserved: redemption is gated, slowed or legally unavailable to the holders who are selling, so secondary-market price measures access to redemption rather than reserve adequacy. Crypto-collateralised: collateral falls faster than liquidators can clear it, or an oracle lags, so the debt backing the token is under-secured and liquidation pushes collateral prices lower. Algorithmic: the peg is defended by issuing a sibling token whose value depends on confidence in that peg, so the defence accelerates the failure once confidence goes.

Academic definition

A persistent deviation between the market price of a pegged crypto-asset and its reference value, arising from impairment of the redemption, liquidation or supply-adjustment channel through which arbitrage would otherwise enforce the peg.

Mechanism

A peg is not a property of a token, it is the output of an arbitrage loop, and a depeg is that loop jamming. The jam is informative: a gated redemption says the claim is slow rather than absent, a liquidation cascade says collateral and the liquidity to sell it were correlated, and a reflexive algorithmic defence says the backstop and the thing it backstopped were the same bet.

In practice

Who uses it
Risk teams at lending protocols and exchanges, treasurers holding working balances, supervisors monitoring reserve disclosure, and arbitrageurs trading the gap.
Why it matters
Pegged tokens are the unit of account for most on-chain credit, so a break propagates into collateral values, liquidations and quoted prices everywhere at once.
Limitations
A small deviation is often ordinary friction, not distress. Price alone cannot distinguish a redemption bottleneck from a reserve shortfall, and the two have very different outcomes.

Common mistakes

  • Treating any deviation as evidence reserves are gone.
  • Assuming a peg that has held is a peg that will hold.
  • Using one collateral model's failure logic to judge a different model.

Examples

numeric

A token referencing one unit trades at 0.985. That is a 150 basis point discount — far beyond transfer friction, and a price at which arbitrage would be profitable if redemption were open.

Given
  • reference value = 1.000
  • market price = 0.985
  • round-trip transfer and trading friction assumed under 20 basis points
Steps
  1. deviation = (0.985 - 1.000) / 1.000 = -0.015
  2. -0.015 x 10,000 = -150 basis points
  3. 150 bp far exceeds the 20 bp friction assumption
a 150 basis point discount, well outside ordinary friction

Hypothetical figures. A discount of this size is evidence that the redemption or liquidation channel is impaired; it does not by itself reveal which channel, or whether reserves exist.

comparison

Three tokens trade at a discount on the same day. One has paused redemptions, one has collateral falling faster than liquidators can sell it, one is defending with a sibling token nobody wants. The same price, three mechanisms.

Mathematical foundation

Variables

Procedure

Divide the gap between observed and reference value by the reference value and express it in basis points. Record the venue and the timestamp: pegged tokens can print different prices on different venues at the same moment, and persistence over time is what separates distress from friction.

Worked example

Given
  • P_ref = 1.000
  • P = 0.985
Steps
  1. (0.985 - 1.000) / 1.000 = -0.015
  2. -0.015 x 10,000 = -150
delta = -150 basis points

Interpretation

A negative value is a discount and a positive value a premium. The number measures the market's current price, not the state of reserves; diagnosing the cause requires looking at the redemption, liquidation or supply-adjustment channel specific to that design.

Assumptions

  • The reference value is itself stable in the unit of account used. A token pegged to a currency inherits that currency's own movements against anything else.
  • The observed price is from a venue with meaningful depth. A thin venue can print a large deviation that no size could trade at.
  • Ordinary round-trip friction, including fees and transfer latency, accounts for some deviation; the threshold above which a deviation is informative is a judgement, not a standard.
Limits of the formula
  • The measure is descriptive. It identifies that a gap exists and its size, and says nothing about cause, duration or recovery.
  • Simultaneous prices differ across venues, so a single quoted deviation is venue-specific.
