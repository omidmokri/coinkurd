Simple definition

Doing what your written plan says, including on the days you would rather not. It is not a personality trait; it is how closely your actions match a rule you wrote earlier.

Professional definition

Practitioner vocabulary for consistency between a documented plan and executed orders. Treated as a character quality it cannot be assessed; treated as a property of a process it can, because adherence is countable from a trade log against the rule version that was in force.

Technical definition

The useful formulation is a deviation rate: the share of executed trades that departed from the written rules on entry, size, stop or exit. That makes two separate problems visible. A rule set may be sound and poorly followed, in which case the fix is procedural — pre-set orders, defined session limits, size fixed before entry. Or it may be unfollowable as written, in which case the honest change is to the rules. Without the log, both problems look identical and are attributed to willpower.

Academic definition

An informal construct denoting fidelity between a documented trading process and realised execution, operationalisable as a measured rate of departure from specified rules.

Mechanism

A plan is written in one state and executed in another: the market is moving, the position is live, and waiting feels costly. Rules that require a decision at that moment fail most often. Rules placed as resting orders, or caps set before the session starts, do not depend on how the moment feels, which is why adherence rises when decisions move earlier.

In practice

Who uses it
Any trader with written rules, and anyone trying to work out whether a weak result came from the method or from the execution.
Why it matters
A counted deviation rate turns an argument about character into a number, and numbers can be improved by changing the process.
Limitations
Adherence is not profitability. Following a losing rule set faithfully produces consistent losses, and a high deviation rate sometimes reflects rules that were never usable in live conditions.

Common mistakes

  • Judging discipline by feel instead of counting departures from the plan.
  • Changing rules during a session rather than between them.
  • Pooling results from several rule versions as though one method were being tested.

Examples

numeric

Of 60 trades in a quarter, 12 were entered without the stated trigger and 9 at larger than the stated size. The deviation rate is 35 percent, and the two categories point to different fixes.

Given
  • total trades in the period = 60
  • entries without the written trigger = 12
  • entries above the written size limit = 9
  • no trade counted in both categories
Steps
  1. deviating trades = 12 + 9 = 21
  2. deviation rate = 21 / 60
  3. = 0.35
a deviation rate of 35 percent, split 12 entry breaches and 9 size breaches

Illustrative counts. The measure records what happened against the rule version then in force; it says nothing about whether those rules had an edge.

everyday

A stop placed as a resting order the moment the position opens is followed every time. The same stop held in mind is followed on quiet days. The difference is where the decision lives, not how determined anyone was.

Commonly confused with

Trade Journal

A trade journal is the record. Discipline is what the record measures: how far the executed trades sat from the rules the journal also stores.
