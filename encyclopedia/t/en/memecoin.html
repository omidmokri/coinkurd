Simple definition

A token whose appeal comes from a joke, an image or an online community rather than from any product. Price depends almost entirely on attention, so it can rise and fall very fast.

Professional definition

A cryptoasset launched with no claimed utility beyond cultural reference and community coordination. Value rests on reflexive demand: buying because others are buying. Typical features are a very large nominal supply, concentrated early holdings, and liquidity confined to a single pool or venue.

Technical definition

Mechanically ordinary tokens whose risk profile is dominated by distribution and liquidity rather than by code. Concentrated supply means a small number of addresses can exit into thin liquidity; a single pool means the quoted price is the marginal price of that pool, not a broad market. Documented fraud patterns in digital-asset markets — pump-and-dump schemes, wash trading, insider launches and tokens whose contracts prevent selling — appear in this segment because attention-driven demand attracts them, not because the token standard is unusual.

Academic definition

A class of crypto-asset whose price formation is driven predominantly by social coordination and attention rather than by cash flows, claimed utility or collateral, and whose microstructure is typically characterised by concentrated holdings and shallow liquidity.

Mechanism

Attention is the only input, and attention is reflexive: a rising price produces the coverage that produces more buying. The same loop runs in reverse, and because liquidity in this segment is usually one shallow pool, the exit is far more expensive than the entry. A very large nominal supply is a presentational choice — a low unit price looks cheap even when the implied total is not.

In practice

Who uses it
Short-horizon speculators, liquidity providers in single pools, on-chain analysts tracking holder concentration, and consumer-protection regulators issuing warnings.
Why it matters
The segment is large enough in activity terms that ignoring it leaves a gap in understanding retail flows and sentiment.
Limitations
There is no fundamental to analyse, no cash flow and usually no redemption. Position sizing and exit liquidity are the only controllable variables, and documented fraud risk is high.

Common mistakes

  • Reading a low unit price as cheapness rather than checking implied total value.
  • Sizing from quoted price while ignoring pool depth.
  • Assuming you can exit at the price you can enter at.

Examples

numeric

A token priced at 0.0000004 units with a supply of one trillion implies a total of 400,000 units of value. The small unit price says nothing about size; the product of price and supply does.

Given
  • unit price = 0.0000004 units of account
  • circulating supply = 1,000,000,000,000 tokens
  • no claim about whether that supply is liquid
Steps
  1. 0.0000004 x 1,000,000,000,000 = 400,000
implied total value of 400,000 units of account

Hypothetical figures. The implied total also assumes the quoted price would hold for the whole supply, which shallow liquidity makes unlikely.

market

Where almost all liquidity sits in one pool, a sell of a few percent of that pool can move the quote sharply. Holders discover the exit price only when they try to use it.

Commonly confused with

Altcoin

Altcoin covers every non-bitcoin asset, including ones with substantial infrastructure. Memecoin is a narrower claim about where demand comes from: culture and attention rather than a product.
