Bid Price

ENGLISHBid Pricereference
فارسیقیمت پیشنهادی خریدRecommended equivalent
کوردینرخی داواکراوی کڕینProposed equivalent — not standard
Category
Trading Fundamentals
Level
Foundation

Simple definition

The best price a buyer in the market is willing to pay right now. If you sell immediately, this is roughly the price you receive.

Professional definition

The highest resting buy price on the order book, carrying a size. It is the price an immediate seller transacts at, the lower boundary of the spread, and a quote that can be withdrawn before you reach it.

Technical definition

The top of the buy side of the limit order book: the highest price level with non-zero resting quantity, reported together with that quantity as the bid size. Depth behind the level matters more than the level itself, because an order larger than bid size executes down the book at worse prices. Quoted bids are cancellable, so a large displayed bid is intent rather than commitment — which is what makes spoofing possible.

Academic definition

The highest price at which a counterparty stands ready to buy a stated quantity, representing the immediate liquidation price available to a seller who demands immediacy.

Mechanism

A bid is an offer of liquidity to sellers, so it is placed below what the bidder thinks fair value is; the discount pays for the risk that the seller knows something the bidder does not. When that risk rises, bids are pulled or lowered, which is why the bid side falls away fastest in a shock.

In practice

Who uses it
Sellers measuring their real exit price, market makers quoting two sides, and anyone reading level-one data or valuing a position.
Why it matters
It is one of the only two prices that exist at the moment of trading: what you can sell at right now, for the size displayed.
Limitations
It describes the top of book only, for the displayed quantity, for as long as the quote stands — which can be milliseconds.

Common mistakes

  • Valuing a holding at the last price when only a thin bid exists.
  • Reading a large displayed bid as committed buying interest.
  • Ignoring bid size when planning an exit.

Examples

everyday

A dealer offers 9,000 for your car while advertising similar cars at 10,500. The 9,000 is the bid — what you actually receive today — and the gap is the dealer's spread.

numeric

The best bid is 99.80 for 50 units, with 120 more at 99.50 and 30 at 99.00. Selling 200 units averages 99.50, not 99.80 — the quote described only a quarter of the order.

Given
  • sell 200 units at market
  • bids: 50 at 99.80, 120 at 99.50, 30 at 99.00
  • fees excluded
Steps
  1. proceeds = 50 x 99.80 + 120 x 99.50 + 30 x 99.00 = 4990 + 11940 + 2970 = 19900
  2. average = 19900 / 200 = 99.50
  3. versus best bid = (99.80 - 99.50) / 99.80 = 0.0030
average realised 99.50, about 30 basis points below the best bid

Recomputed from the stated depth; the headline bid applied to 50 of the 200 units only.

Commonly confused with

Last Price

The last price is a trade that already happened. The bid is a live commitment to buy, for a stated size, that you can act on now.
