Margin Call

Simple definition

A demand for more collateral because a position's equity has fallen too low. You either add money, reduce the position, or have it closed for you.

Professional definition

The notice issued when account equity falls below the maintenance requirement. It states the deficiency, the amount needed to restore compliance, and the deadline. On regulated brokerage accounts there is normally a short window to act; on most crypto venues there is no call at all, only automatic closure.

Technical definition

A procedural step between a failed equity test and forced closure, and it exists only where rules provide for it. The deficiency is the requirement minus equity. It can be cured by depositing collateral equal to the deficiency, or by liquidating positions worth the deficiency divided by the maintenance rate, because selling reduces the requirement faster than it reduces equity. Brokers may require more than the regulatory minimum, may shorten the window, and may sell without further notice.

Academic definition

A formal demand for additional collateral arising when account equity falls below a stipulated maintenance requirement, specifying the deficiency and the period within which compliance must be restored.

Mechanism

The call is the lender's attempt to restore its cushion before the loss can exceed the collateral. Selling works faster than depositing because the requirement is a percentage of what is held: shedding exposure cuts the requirement while leaving equity untouched. The same arithmetic explains why calls arrive in clusters and why meeting them pushes price further in the direction that caused them.

In practice

Who uses it
Margin-account holders and their brokers, clearing members facing a clearing house, and anyone running leverage without a monitored collateral buffer.
Why it matters
It is the last point at which the holder, rather than the venue, still chooses how the exposure is reduced.
Limitations
The window is short and discretionary: a broker may sell immediately in fast markets, choose which positions to sell, and is not obliged to wait for a deposit in transit.

Common mistakes

  • Planning to meet a call with funds that take days to arrive.
  • Depositing the deficiency and staying at the same size.
  • Expecting a call on a venue whose rules provide only for automatic closure.

Examples

market

A broker issues calls across many accounts after a sharp fall. Holders who choose to sell rather than deposit all sell the same liquid names, which deepens the fall and generates the next round of calls.

numeric

Stock worth 20,000 was bought with 10,000 cash and a 10,000 loan, with a 25% maintenance requirement. The call comes once value falls under 13,333. At 12,000 the deficiency is 1,000: deposit 1,000, or sell 4,000 of stock.

Given
  • Market value at purchase = 20,000
  • Cash = 10,000, loan = 10,000 (a 50% initial requirement)
  • Maintenance requirement m_m = 25% of market value
  • Loan amount does not change as prices move
Steps
  1. Call level: MV - 10,000 = 0.25 x MV, so 0.75 MV = 10,000 and MV = 13,333.33
  2. At MV = 12,000: equity = 12,000 - 10,000 = 2,000
  3. Requirement = 0.25 x 12,000 = 3,000, so deficiency = 1,000
  4. Cure by deposit: 1,000
  5. Cure by selling: 1,000 / 0.25 = 4,000 of stock, which leaves equity 2,000 on MV 8,000 = 25%
The call triggers at 13,333.33, a 33.3% fall; at 12,000 either 1,000 of cash or 4,000 of stock restores compliance

Illustrates US regulated equity margin as the cited regulator pages describe it: a 50% initial requirement and a 25% minimum maintenance level, with brokers free to require more. Futures and crypto venues work differently.

Mathematical foundation

D = m_m N - E, \qquad \text{deposit } D \quad \text{or sell } \frac{D}{m_m}

Variables

m_mMaintenance margin ratefraction
NCurrent market value of the position, or notionalcurrency
EAccount equity: market value less the loan, or collateral plus unrealised resultcurrency

Procedure

Work out the requirement on the current market value and subtract equity to get the deficiency. Depositing that amount restores compliance one-for-one. Selling restores it faster: because the requirement is a percentage of what remains, liquidating the deficiency divided by the maintenance rate is enough.

Worked example

Given
  • Market value N = 12,000
  • Loan = 10,000 so equity E = 2,000
  • m_m = 0.25
Steps
  1. Requirement = 0.25 x 12,000 = 3,000
  2. D = 3,000 - 2,000 = 1,000
  3. Deposit route = 1,000
  4. Sell route = 1,000 / 0.25 = 4,000
  5. Check: equity 2,000 on remaining value 8,000 = 25%
D = 1,000; cured by depositing 1,000 or selling 4,000 of the position

Interpretation

Depositing keeps the exposure and the risk; selling cuts both. That the sell figure is several times the deposit figure is not a penalty but arithmetic: at a 25% requirement, each unit sold releases four units of requirement.

Assumptions

  • A long cash-account purchase where the loan is fixed in money terms and equity equals market value less the loan; a derivatives account instead computes equity as collateral plus unrealised result.
  • A flat maintenance rate on the whole position, no accrued interest, fees or dividends, and sale proceeds applied directly against the loan.
  • Rules are jurisdiction-specific. The 50% initial and 25% minimum maintenance levels in the worked example describe US regulated equity margin; brokers set higher house requirements, and futures and crypto venues use different frameworks.
Limits of the formula
  • The identity says what restores compliance now, not what is prudent. Curing a call at the minimum leaves the position one further move from the next one.
  • It assumes the holder gets to act. Where rules allow immediate closure, or where the market gaps, there is no deficiency to cure and the position is simply gone.

Commonly confused with

Liquidation

A margin call is a demand with a deadline, where the holder still decides what to sell. Liquidation is the venue closing the position itself, at its own mark and under its own rules. Many crypto venues provide only the second.
