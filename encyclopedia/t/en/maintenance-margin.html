Maintenance Margin

Simple definition

The minimum collateral a position must keep once it is open. Fall below it and the account is asked for more money, or the position is closed for you.

Professional definition

The equity floor applied continuously to an open position, expressed as a percentage of its current notional. Equity is collateral plus unrealised profit and loss; when equity drops under the requirement the account is deficient, which triggers a call on regulated accounts and automatic closure on most crypto venues.

Technical definition

A test, not a payment: compare equity against the requirement computed on the venue's mark price and current notional. Because the requirement scales with notional while equity shrinks with the loss, both sides of the comparison move against a losing position at once. The trigger price follows exactly from the two rates, and the rate itself is usually tiered by position size and revised with volatility, so the threshold is not a fixed distance from entry.

Academic definition

The minimum ratio of account equity to current notional exposure that an open margined position must satisfy, below which the account is deemed deficient under the venue's or regulator's rules.

Mechanism

The requirement exists so that closure happens while collateral still covers the loss. Setting it below the initial rate creates a deliberate buffer: the gap between the two is the adverse move the position may absorb before anyone intervenes. Because both equity and the requirement move with price, the trigger arrives sooner than a trader reading only the entry price expects.

In practice

Who uses it
Margin and futures traders, brokers monitoring accounts in real time, and risk teams setting internal buffers above the venue minimum.
Why it matters
It defines the exact price at which an account stops being the trader's decision, which is the only distance that matters when sizing leverage.
Limitations
The rate can be tiered and revised, the mark used is the venue's not the market's, and a thin book can gap straight through the threshold without any chance to add collateral.

Common mistakes

  • Sizing from the entry price instead of from the maintenance threshold.
  • Assuming the published rate applies at every position size.
  • Expecting time to react when the venue closes positions automatically.

Examples

market

Two accounts hold the same contract at the same entry. One posted double the minimum collateral. The maintenance test is identical for both, but the second account's threshold sits far further away, so the same adverse candle removes only one of them.

numeric

A long is opened at 50,000 with 10% initial margin and a 5% maintenance rate. Equity falls under the requirement at 47,368, a move of only 5.26% — far less than the 10% a trader might assume from the leverage alone.

Given
  • Long opened at P_0 = 50,000
  • m_i = 10%, so collateral = 10% of notional
  • m_m = 5% maintenance rate
  • Linear contract, collateral in the quote currency, fees ignored
Steps
  1. Equity test: C + Q(P - P_0) < m_m x Q x P
  2. With C = m_i x Q x P_0: P* = P_0 x (1 - m_i) / (1 - m_m)
  3. P* = 50,000 x 0.90 / 0.95 = 47,368.42
  4. Move required = (47,368.42 - 50,000) / 50,000 = -5.26%
The maintenance test fails at 47,368.42, a 5.26% adverse move

Fees, funding and a tiered maintenance rate all pull the threshold closer. What happens after the test fails is covered in the liquidation entry.

Procedure

Compute equity as collateral plus unrealised result at the venue's mark. Compute the requirement as the maintenance rate times current notional. Compare the two: if equity is lower the account is deficient. Setting the two sides equal and solving for price gives the exact level at which that happens.

Worked example

Given
  • P_0 = 50,000
  • m_i = 0.10
  • m_m = 0.05
  • Long, linear contract, fees ignored
Steps
  1. P* = P_0 (1 - m_i) / (1 - m_m)
  2. 1 - 0.10 = 0.90 and 1 - 0.05 = 0.95
  3. P* = 50,000 x 0.90 / 0.95
  4. P* = 47,368.42
P* = 47,368.42, a 5.26% adverse move from entry

Interpretation

The threshold is a distance, and it is shorter than leverage alone suggests because the requirement itself shrinks more slowly than equity. A trader who knows only the entry price and the leverage does not know where the position ends; this is the figure that tells them.

Assumptions

  • A single linear position, collateral in the quote currency, and a flat maintenance rate. Tiered rates raise m_m with size and move the threshold closer.
  • Fees, funding payments and any realised costs are excluded; each of them reduces equity and therefore brings the threshold nearer.
  • The test is run on the venue's mark price, which is normally index-derived and need not match the last trade.
  • Regulated equity margin works differently: FINRA's rules set a minimum maintenance level as a percentage of the current market value of the securities, with brokers free to require more, and a call rather than immediate closure is the normal response.
Limits of the formula
  • The formula gives where the test first fails, not the price at which the position is actually closed. Slippage, partial liquidation and venue procedure decide that, and the liquidation entry covers it.
  • Cross margin changes the inputs entirely, because equity is then the whole account balance and other positions' results feed into the same test.

Commonly confused with

Liquidation Threshold

Maintenance margin is the rate: the percentage of notional equity must keep. The liquidation threshold is the resulting price level for a particular position. One is a rule, the other is where that rule bites on your own entry and size.

Maintenance rates are venue-specific and tiered, and the worked figures are illustrative rather than any venue's current schedule. Regulated equity margin and crypto venue margin differ in substance: the first sets a minimum maintenance percentage of market value and normally issues a call, the second closes positions automatically against an internally computed mark.
