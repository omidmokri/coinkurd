Circulating Supply

Simple definition

How many units of an asset are actually out in the market and able to be traded right now, as opposed to still unissued, locked up, or held back by the issuer.

Professional definition

The portion of issued supply that is transferable and not subject to a lock, vesting contract or issuer reserve. It is the quantity used as the multiplier in market capitalisation, and the denominator against which upcoming unlocks are compared.

Technical definition

There is no single authoritative definition: providers differ on whether to deduct team allocations, foundation reserves, staked balances, bridged wrappers or provably burned units, so two reputable sources can report different figures for the same asset on the same day. Because market capitalisation is price times this number, a methodology choice propagates directly into every ranking built on it. On-chain measurement is further complicated by one entity controlling many addresses.

Academic definition

The transferable subset of issued units of a crypto-asset at a point in time, measured by convention rather than by a standardised rule, and used as the scaling quantity in aggregate market-value statistics.

Mechanism

The number matters because it is a multiplier and a divisor at once: it scales market value up and per-unit metrics down. Since what counts as circulating is a judgement, the figure is softer than it looks, and the softest judgements — staked balances, treasury holdings, long vesting — are the largest ones. The same unlock is then trivial against a large float and dominant against a small one.

In practice

Who uses it
Index and data providers, valuation analysts, listing committees, and anyone comparing market capitalisation across assets.
Why it matters
Nearly every aggregate crypto statistic is price times this quantity, so the figure sets the scale of the whole comparison.
Limitations
It is a convention, not a measurement. Methodologies differ, staked and bridged balances are treated inconsistently, and no provider's number is definitive.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing market caps from two providers without checking their supply methodology.
  • Treating staked supply as unavailable when unstaking is quick.
  • Ignoring how much supply is still to be issued.

Examples

numeric

Total issued is 500 million; 180 million is locked under vesting and 70 million sits in a treasury. Circulating is 250 million, half of total, so a quoted market value understates eventual dilution by a factor of two.

Given
  • total issued = 500,000,000 tokens
  • locked under vesting = 180,000,000
  • issuer treasury = 70,000,000
  • no further issuance assumed
Steps
  1. 500,000,000 - 180,000,000 - 70,000,000 = 250,000,000
  2. 250,000,000 / 500,000,000 = 50 percent
circulating supply 250,000,000, which is 50 percent of issued supply

Hypothetical figures. Whether a treasury balance is deducted is exactly the kind of methodology choice that differs between providers.

comparison

Two assets quote identical market values. One has nearly all supply circulating; the other has a quarter. The second must absorb three times its current float from future issuance before the two are comparable.

Commonly confused with

Market Capitalisation

Circulating supply is a count of units. Market capitalisation is that count multiplied by price. Most disputes about a market cap are really disputes about which units were counted.
