Level
Foundation

Simple definition

Failing to keep a payment promise. Missing a scheduled interest or principal payment is the clearest case, but a contract can define other breaches as default too.

Professional definition

A breach of the terms of a debt contract, most often a missed payment past any grace period, but also covenant breaches, bankruptcy filings or restructurings that the documentation treats as triggering events.

Technical definition

A contractually and legally defined state, not an economic measurement. Definitions differ across loan agreements, bond indentures, rating-agency methodologies and regulatory frameworks, so default rates from different sources are not directly comparable. Cross-default and acceleration clauses mean one breach can mature unrelated obligations at once, which is how a single missed payment becomes a systemic event.

Academic definition

The failure of an obligor to perform a contractual obligation under a debt instrument, as defined by that instrument's terms and the applicable legal framework.

Mechanism

Debt works because payment is non-discretionary; default is the moment that assumption breaks. Once it does, control shifts from the borrower to the creditors and to the insolvency process, and recovery depends on priority, collateral and the legal regime rather than on the original promise.

In practice

Who uses it
Credit analysts, workout and restructuring teams, bondholders reading indenture terms, and lenders drafting covenants.
Why it matters
The precise definition decides when rights are triggered, so it drives negotiation leverage long before any court is involved.
Limitations
What counts as default is jurisdiction- and document-specific, and a restructuring that avoids the legal label can still impose losses as large as one.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming default means total loss.
  • Comparing default rates from sources with different definitions.
  • Overlooking cross-default clauses in unrelated agreements.

Examples

market

A borrower makes its coupon payment four days late, inside a five-day grace period. No default has occurred under the contract, even though the market may already be trading the bond as if one had.

numeric

A bondholder with 100,000 of face value in a default that ultimately recovers 35 cents on the unit receives 35,000. The loss is 65,000, which is why recovery assumptions matter as much as default probabilities.

Given
  • face held = 100,000
  • final recovery = 35% of face
  • no collateral pledged
Steps
  1. recovered = 100,000 x 0.35
  2. loss = 100,000 - 35,000
35,000 recovered, 65,000 lost

Illustrative recovery. Actual recoveries depend on priority, collateral and the insolvency regime, and arrive years later.

Commonly confused with

Liquidation

In crypto lending, liquidation is an automatic sale of collateral when a ratio breaks, and it usually prevents a shortfall. Default is the failure to pay, which liquidation is designed to avoid.
