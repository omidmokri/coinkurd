Time value of money

ENGLISHTime value of moneyreference

Category
Financial Foundations
Level
Foundation

Simple definition

A sum today is worth more than the same sum later, because today's sum can be put to work. That is why interest exists and why future money must be discounted.

Professional definition

The principle that cash flows are only comparable once dated and adjusted by a rate. Moving money forward in time is compounding; moving it backward is discounting. Every valuation method in finance is an application of this one idea.

Technical definition

Equivalence of dated cash flows under a given periodic rate: a unit at time zero maps to (1+r)^n at time n, and the mapping is invertible. The rate carries the opportunity cost of capital and, when applied to uncertain flows, a risk adjustment. Compounding frequency is part of the specification: the same quoted rate gives different factors annually, monthly or continuously.

Academic definition

The formal equivalence between cash flows at different dates established by a periodic discount rate, under which present and future values are related by a deterministic compounding factor.

Mechanism

Capital can be lent, invested or consumed, so holding it idle has a cost; the rate measures what the next-best use would have paid. Compounding is then just the same rate applied to a growing base, which is why the gap between a present and a future sum widens non-linearly with horizon.

In practice

Who uses it
Anyone comparing a payment now with a payment later: borrowers, lenders, project analysts, pension planners, and valuation teams.
Why it matters
It is the only honest way to compare a lump sum with an instalment plan, or this year's cash flow with next decade's.
Limitations
The answer is only as good as the rate chosen, and the choice of rate is a judgement, not an observation.

Common mistakes

  • Adding cash flows from different dates without discounting.
  • Mixing an annual rate with monthly periods.
  • Treating the discount rate as a fact rather than an assumption.

Examples

everyday

A seller offers 10,000 today or 11,000 in two years. Which is better is not a matter of taste: it depends entirely on the rate you could otherwise earn over those two years.

numeric

At 6% a year, 10,000 today grows to 11,236 in two years, so 11,000 in two years is worth less than 10,000 now. At 4%, the ranking reverses. The rate decides, not the larger number.

Given
  • PV = 10,000
  • n = 2 years
  • annual compounding
  • case A: r = 6%; case B: r = 4%
Steps
  1. case A: 10,000 x 1.06^2 = 11,236
  2. case B: 10,000 x 1.04^2 = 10,816
11,000 in two years beats 10,000 today only if the available rate is below about 4.88%

The break-even rate solves (1+r)^2 = 1.1, giving r ≈ 4.88%.

Mathematical foundation

FV = PV \times (1+r)^{n}, \qquad PV = \frac{FV}{(1+r)^{n}}, \qquad FV = PV \times \left(1+\frac{r}{m}\right)^{mn}

Variables

PVPresent value: the dated-today equivalentcurrency
FVFuture value at time ncurrency
rPeriodic rate, as a decimal, matching the period of ndecimal
nNumber of periodsperiods
mCompounding frequency per year, in the third formtimes per year

Procedure

Fix the period. Convert the quoted rate to that period, compound forward with (1+r)^n for a future value, or divide by the same factor for a present value. With m compounding dates per year, use r/m per period and mn periods.

Worked example

Given
  • PV = 10,000
  • r = 6% per year
  • n = 2 years
  • m = 1
Steps
  1. (1.06)^2 = 1.1236
  2. FV = 10,000 x 1.1236
FV = 11,236

Interpretation

The factor (1+r)^n is an exchange rate between dates, not a forecast. It states what the two sums would have to be for an indifferent party at that rate; change the rate and the comparison changes.

Assumptions

  • The rate is constant over the horizon and reinvestment happens at that same rate.
  • The period of r and the period of n are the same; mixing an annual rate with monthly counts is the most common error.
  • Nominal rates and nominal cash flows must be paired; a real rate requires real cash flows.
Limits of the formula
  • Says nothing about whether the cash flows will actually arrive; credit risk and default are handled separately or inside the rate.
  • A single rate for all horizons ignores the term structure, which is why bond work uses a curve rather than one number.

Commonly confused with

Compounding versus Simple Returns

Time value is the principle that dated sums differ. Compounding versus simple return is a question about how a given rate is applied to a base.
