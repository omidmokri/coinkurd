Colocation

Simple definition

Renting rack space for your server in the same building as the exchange's matching engine, so your orders travel the shortest possible path.

Professional definition

A venue service placing a participant's hardware in the data centre that hosts the matching engine, with standardised cable lengths so that no tenant is physically closer than another. It removes wide-area propagation delay, the one latency component software cannot reduce.

Technical definition

Equalised cross-connect lengths are the point: the venue sells a floor on propagation delay, not an advantage over other tenants. Remaining differences come from network cards, kernel bypass, feed handlers and code path, and from whether the participant consumes a raw feed or a consolidated one. The crypto analogue is renting cloud capacity in the region hosting the venue's gateway, which gives much of the benefit with none of the standardisation, so tenant distances are not equal.

Academic definition

The provision of hosting adjacent to a trading venue's matching infrastructure, with standardised connectivity, in order to minimise and equalise propagation latency among participants.

Mechanism

Distance is a hard constraint: a signal cannot cross a kilometre of fibre in less than roughly five microseconds, so a server a hundred kilometres away begins every race half a millisecond behind. Once that term is removed, competition shifts to components a firm can buy or write, which is why colocation is a precondition for latency-sensitive strategies rather than an edge by itself.

In practice

Who uses it
High-frequency firms, exchange market makers, and crypto desks renting cloud instances in the venue's own region.
Why it matters
It removes the purely geographic part of latency and is a precondition for queue-sensitive quoting and cross-venue arbitrage.
Limitations
It is a fixed cost that buys parity rather than advantage, and it concentrates operational and jurisdictional risk in one facility.

Common mistakes

  • Expecting colocation to make a slow code path competitive.
  • Assuming cloud regions equalise distance the way an exchange cage does.
  • Ignoring single-facility outage and connectivity risk.

Examples

numeric

A server 100 kilometres from the matching engine starts every interaction about one millisecond behind on the round trip. Inside the facility that term falls below a hundred microseconds, and the race moves to software.

Given
  • route length 100 km each way
  • signal speed in fibre about 2.0 x 10^5 km/s
Steps
  1. 100 / 200000 = 0.0005 s = 0.5 ms one way
  2. round trip = 1.0 ms
about 1 ms of round-trip propagation removed by colocating

Propagation only. Switching, queuing and matching time remain, and in a single facility they are what separates tenants.

institutional

A venue publishes identical cable lengths for every cage, so tenants cannot buy a shorter path. Competition then runs on network hardware and software, which is where the recurring spend goes.

Commonly confused with

High-Frequency Trading

Colocation is infrastructure available to anyone who pays for it, including slow strategies that merely want reliable connectivity. HFT is a class of strategy that needs that infrastructure; buying the rack does not make a strategy high-frequency.
