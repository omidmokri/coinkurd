Simple definition

The idea that markets repeat four phases: quiet buying, a rising phase, quiet selling near the top, then a falling phase. Each phase is named after who is doing the trading.

Professional definition

A four-phase description of price behaviour — accumulation, markup, distribution, markdown — in which two range-bound phases of transfer between patient and impatient participants separate the two directional phases. The labels are descriptive annotations, not a timetable.

Technical definition

Accumulation and distribution are ranges in which supply is absorbed without net progress; markup and markdown are the trending phases between them. The framework offers no identification test: naming a phase requires knowing where its range ends, which is known only afterwards, so any phase label on the current bar is a hypothesis. Phase length, amplitude and even the order of events are unconstrained, which is why the model describes history well and restricts the future weakly.

Academic definition

A descriptive four-stage taxonomy of price and volume behaviour characterising transitions between range-bound transfer and directional trend, without a formal identification criterion or out-of-sample test.

Mechanism

The phases follow from one asymmetry: patient capital accumulates while the news is bad and prices are flat, and distributes while the news is good and prices are high. A range forms because one side absorbs the other's flow without conceding price, and it ends when that absorption stops. The cycle is therefore a story about who holds the asset, told through the shape of price and volume.

In practice

Who uses it
Swing and position traders, analysts writing market commentary, and developers building a regime filter into a strategy.
Why it matters
It gives one vocabulary for a chart's regime, which keeps strategy choice and risk appetite tied to context instead of to the last candle.
Limitations
Phase boundaries are only unambiguous in hindsight and nothing fixes a phase's length. Past alignment of crypto cycles with the halving is an observation over very few cycles, not a law.

Common mistakes

  • Declaring accumulation while the range is still unresolved.
  • Projecting a past cycle's length onto the present one.
  • Treating the four phases as a schedule rather than a description.

Examples

market

A long sideways range after a strong rally is called distribution once the decline has started. While it was forming, the same range was read by many as a pause before another leg up, and nothing on the chart settled the argument.

numeric

Bitcoin's subsidy halves on a fixed schedule of roughly four years, so by 2026 only about four complete cycles can be observed. Four observations cannot separate a repeating law from a coincidence, whatever the chart suggests.

Given
  • the block subsidy halves on a fixed schedule of roughly four years
  • the chain began in January 2009
  • complete halving-to-halving cycles observable by 2026: about four
Steps
  1. a four-year cadence over seventeen years gives about four completed intervals
  2. fitting phase length, amplitude and timing needs at least three estimates per cycle
  3. four observations against three or more free parameters leaves no degrees of freedom for a test
The sample is too small to test the claim that cycles repeat on schedule.

This is a counting argument about sample size, not a forecast of the next cycle.

Commonly confused with

Bull and Bear Market

Bull and bear name the direction of a sustained regime. The cycle model adds the two quiet phases between them and asserts an order of events, which is a stronger and less testable claim.
