Call Option

Level
Foundation

Simple definition

A contract giving its buyer the right to buy the underlying at a fixed price until a set date. The buyer pays a premium; the seller must deliver if the right is used.

Professional definition

The right, not the obligation, to buy a stated quantity of the underlying at the strike price on or before expiry. Payoff at expiry is max(S−K,0). The holder's loss is capped at the premium paid; the writer's loss has no ceiling.

Technical definition

A contingent claim whose terminal value is max(S−K,0), with S the underlying price at expiry and K the strike. Before expiry the premium sits above that floor by its time value. A long call carries positive delta, positive gamma and negative theta; a naked short call inverts all three and leaves the writer with an unbounded loss, which is why venues margin writers and not buyers.

Academic definition

A contingent claim conferring the unilateral right to purchase a specified underlying at a predetermined strike on or before a stated expiry, with terminal payoff equal to the positive part of the underlying price less the strike.

Mechanism

The asymmetry is the product. A buyer can walk away from an unused right, so the floor is the premium, while above the strike the position tracks the underlying one for one. The writer is paid in advance for accepting the mirror image of that payoff, and must post collateral because the obligation has no natural ceiling.

In practice

Who uses it
Hedgers capping the cost of a future purchase, directional traders who want a known maximum loss, and desks writing calls against inventory they already hold.
Why it matters
It turns an open-ended directional exposure into a prepaid, known maximum loss, and gives exposure per unit of capital that a spot position cannot.
Limitations
The premium is spent whatever happens. The underlying can rise and the trade still lose, if the move is too small or arrives too late.

Common mistakes

  • Treating a cheap far out-of-the-money call as a free lottery ticket.
  • Taking breakeven as the strike rather than the strike plus the premium.
  • Writing naked calls sized as though the upside were bounded.

Examples

market

A treasurer who must buy an asset in three months can buy calls instead of the asset now. The cost of the hedge is known on day one; the worst case is that the calls expire worthless and the purchase is made in the open market.

numeric

A call with strike 100 costs 6. At expiry at 112 it pays 12, a profit of 6. At 104 it pays 4, a loss of 2 even though the underlying rose. At 98 it pays nothing and the loss is the full 6.

Given
  • K = 100
  • premium c = 6
  • European exercise, one unit of underlying, fees ignored
Steps
  1. S_T = 112: payoff = max(112 - 100, 0) = 12, profit = 12 - 6 = 6
  2. S_T = 104: payoff = 4, profit = 4 - 6 = -2
  3. S_T = 98: payoff = 0, profit = -6
breakeven at expiry = K + c = 106

Rising underlying and a profitable call are not the same thing. The premium has to be earned back before the first unit of profit.

Mathematical foundation

\text{Payoff}_{\text{call}} = \max(S_T - K,\ 0), \qquad \text{Profit}_{\text{long}} = \max(S_T - K,\ 0) - c

Variables

S_TPrice of the underlying at expiryprice units
KStrike price fixed in the contractprice units
cPremium paid by the buyer at inceptionprice units

Procedure

Subtract the strike from the underlying price at expiry and floor the result at zero: a right is never exercised at a loss. Subtract the premium to get profit, and note that breakeven is the strike plus the premium, not the strike.

Worked example

Given
  • K = 100
  • c = 6
  • S_T = 112
Steps
  1. 112 - 100 = 12
  2. max(12, 0) = 12
  3. 12 - 6 = 6
payoff 12, profit 6, breakeven 106

Interpretation

The identity describes the value of the right at the moment it dies. It says nothing about the premium before expiry, which carries time value and needs a model.

Assumptions

  • Stated for European-style exercise at expiry. An American-style call can be exercised earlier, which adds an early-exercise premium this identity does not capture.
  • Written per unit of underlying. Real contracts carry a multiplier or contract size, and the premium is quoted per unit but paid per contract.
  • Fees, commissions and the financing cost of the premium between trade date and expiry are excluded.
  • The payoff is exact arithmetic. It is not a pricing model and implies nothing about whether the premium was fair.
Limits of the formula
  • The payoff diagram is an expiry picture. A call held to expiry and a call traded before it behave differently, because time value and the volatility input only matter before expiry.
  • Cash settlement and physical delivery give the same payoff number but different operational outcomes, and venues differ on which applies.

Commonly confused with

Put Option

A call is the right to buy, a put the right to sell. Buying a call and writing a put are both bullish, but only the first has a capped loss.
