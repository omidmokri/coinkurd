Engulfing Pattern

Simple definition

A two-candle shape where the second candle's body is larger than the first, covers it, and closes on the opposite side.

Professional definition

A two-candle reversal shape. Bullish: a small down body followed by an up body that covers it. Bearish: the mirror. The classical description also requires the second candle to open beyond the first candle's close — a gap — which rarely happens in continuously traded markets, so most code relaxes the rule to body containment alone.

Technical definition

Three coding choices decide everything: whether engulf means body over body or range over range, whether the classical opening gap is required, and how the prior trend is defined. The gap condition matters most. In a session market the second open is a fresh auction, so the pattern encodes an overnight repricing that was then fully reversed. In a 24/7 market the second open equals the previous close, so the same label describes a different event.

Academic definition

A two-interval candlestick configuration in which the second interval's directional body contains that of the first and closes beyond its opposite extreme, conventionally read as a reversal signal.

Mechanism

The shape compresses a specific sequence: one side owned the first period, and in the next the other side not only took it back but finished past where the first began. Where a session open can gap, that requires absorbing an overnight imbalance, which is a real and costly event. Where trading is continuous, the same pattern can form from ordinary drift, so the two cases should not be pooled.

In practice

Who uses it
Candlestick traders who want a defined entry trigger, and developers coding reversal filters for a systematic rule set.
Why it matters
It gives a precise two-bar, fully mechanical definition with an obvious invalidation level at the extreme of the engulfing candle.
Limitations
Published out-of-sample evidence for engulfing signals is weak, measured results move with the engulfment and trend definitions, and the classical gap condition is nearly absent in 24/7 markets.

Common mistakes

  • Coding body engulfment, then citing a range-engulfment study.
  • Ignoring the prior-trend condition the classical reading requires.
  • Taking the signal without checking the higher timeframe.

Examples

chart

After five declining candles, one wide up candle covers the body of the last and closes above its open. The same two bodies appearing in the middle of a flat range would be labelled the same way and mean something quite different.

numeric

Candle one runs 102 down to 100. Candle two opens 99.8 and closes 103. The classical conditions hold, because the second opened below the first close and closed above its open. In a 24/7 market the second would open at exactly 100 and fail that first test.

Given
  • candle 1: open 102, close 100 (down body)
  • candle 2: open 99.8, close 103 (up body)
  • classical rule: candle 2 opens below candle 1 close and closes above candle 1 open
Steps
  1. candle 1 body spans 100 to 102
  2. candle 2 body spans 99.8 to 103 and contains it
  3. 99.8 < 100 and 103 > 102, so both classical conditions hold
  4. with a continuous open at 100, the first condition fails
Classical bullish engulfing in a session market; body engulfing only in a continuous market.

The same two bodies pass or fail depending on whether an opening gap is structurally possible. That is a venue property, not a signal property.

Commonly confused with

Hammer and Shooting Star

Engulfing is a relationship between two bodies. Hammer and shooting star are single-candle shapes defined by the proportion of wick to body.
