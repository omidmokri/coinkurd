Simple definition

A counter-trend structure break that also required the move to be sharp: price must take out the last swing in the opposite direction and do it with a strong, gap-like leg.

Professional definition

In retail smart-money teaching, a reversal-side structure break qualified by displacement: the counter-trend swing is broken and the breaking leg must be impulsive, usually evidenced by a large range relative to recent bars or by an imbalance left behind. Teachers who separate it from change of character use the displacement filter as the distinction.

Technical definition

Codeable as a two-condition test: first, a body close beyond the relevant counter-trend swing point; second, a displacement filter on the breaking leg, for example range greater than a multiple of average true range, or the presence of a three-candle imbalance inside the leg. Both the multiple and the imbalance definition are free parameters, so the same chart yields different shift counts under different settings.

Academic definition

A composite rule-based annotation combining violation of a counter-trend extreme with a volatility-conditioned displacement filter, used in practitioner frameworks and lacking an established empirical validation.

Mechanism

The displacement filter is what separates this label from a slow drift through a swing low. A wide, one-sided leg means the book was cleared faster than quotes were replaced, which is a real microstructure statement about speed, not about who was trading. The reversal story attached to it is an interpretation; the filter itself is just a volatility threshold.

In practice

Who uses it
Price-action and smart-money traders who want a stricter reversal trigger than a plain counter-trend break, and developers adding a volatility filter to structure logic.
Why it matters
The displacement condition removes many slow, overlapping breaks, which raises the average size of the moves that qualify and so reduces signal count.
Limitations
The filter is a free parameter with no standard value, usage overlaps with change of character, and no peer-reviewed evidence base exists.

Common mistakes

  • Tuning the displacement multiple until past trades look good.
  • Measuring displacement on a different timeframe from the break.
  • Treating it as proof of a reversal rather than a filter.

Examples

market

Price grinds down through a swing high over six overlapping candles: a counter-trend break, but no shift under a displacement rule. Two sessions later one candle takes out the next swing high with a range three times the recent average and leaves a gap inside the leg. That one qualifies.

numeric

Average true range is 1.20. The breaking candle has a range of 3.90, so the multiple is 3.25. A filter set at two accepts it; one set at four rejects it and the trade never happens. Nothing about the market changed, only the threshold.

Given
  • average true range = 1.20
  • breaking candle range = 3.90
  • candidate filters: 2.0x and 4.0x ATR
Steps
  1. multiple = 3.90 / 1.20 = 3.25
  2. 3.25 > 2.0, so the 2x filter accepts the shift
  3. 3.25 < 4.0, so the 4x filter rejects it
One candle, two valid settings, opposite conclusions.

The displacement multiple must be fixed before testing and reported with any result, or the signal count is not reproducible.

Commonly confused with

Change of Character

Many teachers use the two names for the same counter-trend break. Where they are separated, the shift adds a displacement requirement on the breaking leg and the change of character does not. Ask which definition is in use before comparing results.
