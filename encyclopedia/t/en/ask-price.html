Ask Price

Category
Trading Fundamentals
Level
Foundation

Simple definition

The best price a seller in the market is willing to accept right now. If you buy immediately, this is roughly the price you pay.

Professional definition

The lowest resting sell price on the order book, carrying a size. It is the price an immediate buyer transacts at, the upper boundary of the spread, and a quote the seller may cancel at any moment.

Technical definition

The top of the sell side of the book: the lowest price level with non-zero resting quantity, reported with that quantity as the ask size. An order larger than ask size climbs the book and pays progressively more, so the quoted ask bounds the cost only of a small order. Because ask quotes are free to cancel, displayed offers overstate committed supply, and a buy limit priced above the ask executes immediately as a taker rather than resting.

Academic definition

The lowest price at which a counterparty stands ready to sell a stated quantity, representing the immediate acquisition cost faced by a buyer who demands immediacy.

Mechanism

An ask is an offer of liquidity to buyers, so it sits above the seller's own estimate of fair value. The markup pays for inventory risk and for the chance that the buyer is better informed. Widen that risk and the ask moves up or disappears, which is how spreads blow out before news.

In practice

Who uses it
Buyers measuring their real entry cost, market makers quoting two sides, and anyone pricing a purchase from level-one data.
Why it matters
It is the other of the two prices that exist at the moment of trading: what you must pay right now for the size displayed.
Limitations
It bounds the cost of a small order only. Beyond the ask size the real cost is the weighted average up the book, not the quote.

Common mistakes

  • Budgeting a large purchase at the quoted ask.
  • Assuming visible offers will still be there after a signal fires.
  • Confusing the ask with the price of the last trade.

Examples

market

Minutes before an earnings release the best ask jumps two ticks and its size shrinks to a tenth. Nothing traded; sellers simply repriced the risk of selling to a better-informed buyer.

numeric

The best ask is 100.20 for 30 units, then 100.50 for 70 and 101.00 for 100. Buying 150 units averages 100.61 — forty-one basis points above the quote everyone was watching.

Given
  • buy 150 units at market
  • asks: 30 at 100.20, 70 at 100.50, 50 at 101.00
  • fees excluded
Steps
  1. cost = 30 x 100.20 + 70 x 100.50 + 50 x 101.00 = 3006 + 7035 + 5050 = 15091
  2. average = 15091 / 150 = 100.6067
  3. versus best ask = (100.6067 - 100.20) / 100.20 = 0.0041
average paid about 100.61, roughly 41 basis points above the quoted ask

Only 50 of the 100 units at 101.00 were needed. The quoted ask bounded 30 units of a 150-unit order.

Commonly confused with

Bid-Ask Spread

The ask is one of the two quotes. The spread is the distance between them, and it is the quantity used to compare liquidity across instruments.
