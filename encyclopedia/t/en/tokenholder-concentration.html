Tokenholder Concentration

Simple definition

How much of a token's supply sits in a few hands. If ten addresses hold most of it, a decision by one of them can move the market.

Professional definition

The distribution of token supply across holders, usually summarised as the share held by the largest N addresses and sometimes as a Herfindahl index. Read as a measure of how exposed the market is to a single participant's exit and of how much governance power is concentrated.

Technical definition

Measured on addresses, which are not holders in either direction. Custodial and exchange addresses aggregate thousands of customers into one apparent whale, while a single entity can hold across many addresses and appear dispersed. Treasury, vesting, bridge and burn addresses are frequently counted as holders unless excluded explicitly. Two distributions with the same top-ten share can differ by a factor of five on a Herfindahl measure.

Academic definition

A measure of the inequality of cryptoasset ownership across ledger addresses, computed as a top-N supply share or a concentration index, subject to the non-identity of addresses and controlling entities.

Mechanism

A thin market can only absorb so much selling, so when a large share of supply sits behind one decision, the price is contingent on that decision rather than on anything about the project. The same concentration decides governance outcomes where voting is by token weight. Both effects are about control, which is why the measure belongs in diligence rather than in valuation.

In practice

Who uses it
Diligence analysts, exchange listing and risk teams, governance researchers, and anyone sizing exposure to a thinly held token.
Why it matters
It is computable from the ledger without the project's cooperation, and it bears directly on exit risk and on who controls governance votes.
Limitations
Addresses are not holders. Without labelling of exchange, treasury, bridge and vesting addresses, a top-ten share can overstate or understate real concentration by a wide margin.

Common mistakes

  • Counting an exchange's custodial address as one whale.
  • Quoting a top-ten share without excluding treasury and burn addresses.
  • Assuming a dispersed address distribution means dispersed control.

Examples

numeric

Two tokens both show a top-ten share of 68 per cent. In one, ten addresses hold 6.8 per cent each; in the other, one holds 50 and nine hold 2 each. The Herfindahl measures are 462 and 2,536 — the same headline, a fivefold difference in concentration.

Given
  • Case A: ten addresses at 6.8% of supply each
  • Case B: one address at 50%, nine at 2% each
  • Both cases: top-ten share = 68%
Steps
  1. HHI_A = 10 x 6.8^2 = 10 x 46.24 = 462.4
  2. HHI_B = 50^2 + 9 x 2^2 = 2,500 + 36 = 2,536
  3. Ratio = 2,536 / 462.4 = 5.5
Identical top-ten shares; Herfindahl concentration differs by a factor of 5.5

Illustrative figures. Neither measure knows whether any address is an exchange holding customer balances.

case

A token looks highly concentrated because its second-largest address holds eleven per cent of supply. That address turns out to be an exchange's omnibus wallet holding balances for many customers, while a genuinely large private holder sits dispersed across forty smaller addresses.

Mathematical foundation

S_N = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} b_i}{S}, \qquad HHI = \sum_{i} \left(100 \times \frac{b_i}{S}\right)^2

Variables

b_iBalance held by the i-th address, ranked largest firsttokens
SSupply base used as the denominator: circulating, or total if statedtokens
S_NShare of supply held by the largest N addressesratio or %
HHIHerfindahl-Hirschman index of concentration, percentage shares squared and summedindex, 0 to 10,000

Procedure

Take address balances at one block height, exclude and label treasury, vesting, bridge, burn and known exchange addresses, then rank and sum the largest N to get the top-N share. For the index, square each address's percentage share and add them. State the supply base and the exclusions with the result.

Worked example

Given
  • Case A: ten addresses each holding 6.8% of circulating supply
  • Case B: one address holding 50%, nine holding 2% each
Steps
  1. S_10 in both cases = 68%
  2. HHI_A = 10 x (6.8)^2 = 462.4
  3. HHI_B = (50)^2 + 9 x (2)^2 = 2,536
Top-ten share 68% in both; HHI 462 against 2,536

Interpretation

A higher share or index means fewer decisions control more of the supply, so exit risk and governance capture are larger. It is a statement about addresses and therefore an upper or lower bound on control, never a count of people.

Assumptions

  • Addresses are not holders in either direction: custodial and exchange addresses aggregate many customers, and one entity can split a position across many addresses.
  • The supply base is a convention. Circulating, free-float and total supply give different shares, and treasury, vesting, bridge and burn addresses must be labelled and excluded explicitly.
  • Both measures are snapshots at a block height; a vesting unlock or a treasury transfer changes them overnight without any trade.
Limits of the formula
  • A top-N share hides the shape of the distribution. Two sets with the same share can differ several-fold on a Herfindahl measure, as the worked example shows.
  • Clustering heuristics used to group addresses into entities are estimates with error, so entity-level concentration is an inference rather than a measurement.

Commonly confused with

Circulating Supply

Circulating supply is how many tokens are deemed to be in the market. Concentration is how those tokens are spread across addresses. A small circulating supply can be widely held, and a large one can sit in three wallets.
