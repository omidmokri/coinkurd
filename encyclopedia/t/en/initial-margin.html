Initial Margin

Simple definition

The money you must put up before a leveraged position can be opened. It is not a payment and not a fee: it is collateral that stays yours while the position lives.

Professional definition

The equity required at the moment of opening, set as a percentage of notional by the venue, clearing house or regulator. It fixes the maximum leverage available, since the inverse of the margin rate is the largest exposure the deposited equity can support.

Technical definition

A rule-set requirement, not a market quantity. On cleared futures it is a performance bond calibrated by the clearing house to cover a plausible one-day or multi-day move in the contract, and it is revised when volatility changes. On regulated equity accounts it is a fixed regulatory percentage of the purchase price. On crypto venues it is usually implied by a leverage selector and tiered, so larger notional carries a higher rate. The three are set by different bodies for different purposes and are not comparable.

Academic definition

The collateral an account must hold at trade inception, expressed as a specified fraction of notional exposure, determining the maximum attainable leverage of the position.

Mechanism

The requirement is sized from the lender's worst plausible day, not from the trader's plan. That is why it rises with the volatility of the underlying and with the size of the position: a bigger line is harder to exit without moving the price. Raising the rate also removes leverage from everyone at once, which is why requirements tighten exactly when positions are hardest to reduce.

In practice

Who uses it
Every margin trader, clearing members posting to a clearing house, and risk teams that must forecast collateral needs before a trade is placed.
Why it matters
It decides how large a position a given account can open at all, and how much free collateral is left to absorb an adverse move.
Limitations
It is a minimum, not a safe level. Brokers may require more than the regulator, venues can raise rates intraday, and a position opened at the minimum has almost no buffer before the maintenance test fails.

Common mistakes

  • Opening at maximum leverage so that initial and maintenance levels nearly coincide.
  • Assuming the rate is fixed for the life of the position.
  • Comparing a crypto margin rate with a regulated equity requirement.

Examples

market

A clearing house raises the initial requirement on a contract after a volatile week. Nobody's position changed, but every holder must either post more collateral or reduce size — and many reduce at the same time, into the same market.

numeric

A notional of 50,000 at a 10% initial margin rate needs 5,000 of equity, which is ten times leverage. On a regulated US equity purchase the initial requirement is 50% of the purchase price, so the same 5,000 supports only 10,000 of stock.

Given
  • N = 50,000
  • m_i = 10% (venue margin rate)
  • Comparison: Regulation T initial requirement of 50% for a margin purchase of equity securities
Steps
  1. IM = 0.10 x 50,000 = 5,000
  2. Maximum leverage = 1 / 0.10 = 10x
  3. Under a 50% requirement: 5,000 / 0.50 = 10,000 of exposure, i.e. 2x
The same 5,000 of equity supports 50,000 of exposure at a 10% rate and 10,000 at a 50% requirement

Regulation T applies to margin purchases of securities in the United States, not to futures or to crypto venues, which set their own rates. Rules differ by jurisdiction and change.

Mathematical foundation

IM = m_i \, N, \qquad L_{\max} = \frac{1}{m_i}, \qquad \text{open only if } E_0 \geq IM

Procedure

Read the current initial rate for that product and tier. Multiply it by the notional you intend to hold to get the collateral required. The position may be opened only if available equity meets that figure; the reciprocal of the rate is the most leverage the account can reach.

Worked example

Given
  • N = 50,000
  • m_i = 0.10
Steps
  1. IM = 0.10 x 50,000 = 5,000
  2. L_max = 1 / 0.10 = 10
  3. Open permitted if E_0 >= 5,000
IM = 5,000 and maximum leverage 10x

Interpretation

The rate is the venue's statement of how much of the position's risk it refuses to finance. A low rate is not a measure of safety; it transfers more of the price risk onto the holder's equity, which is why the same move empties a thin margin faster.

Assumptions

  • A linear contract whose notional is measured in the same currency as the collateral; an inverse contract converts notional differently.
  • A flat margin rate. Tiered schedules raise the rate as notional grows, so a single m_i understates the requirement for a large position.
  • Portfolio or span-style margining can net offsetting positions, so the requirement for a book is not the sum of the per-position figures.
Limits of the formula
  • The identity gives the requirement, not the risk. It says nothing about the probability of the move that would consume it.
  • Regulatory and venue requirements are jurisdiction-specific and are revised, so any rate must be read from the current rulebook rather than from this entry.

Commonly confused with

Maintenance Margin

Initial margin is tested once, at opening, and sets the maximum size. Maintenance margin is tested continuously thereafter and is normally lower; the gap between the two is the whole buffer a position has.

Flagged by the editor as uncertain on a linguistic, mathematical or regulatory point.

The 50% figure describes Regulation T for margin purchases of equity securities in the United States as the cited regulator pages state it; requirements differ by jurisdiction, by product and by broker, and they change. Cleared futures margin is set by the clearing house per product and revised with volatility. Crypto venue margin is not regulated equity margin and the two should not be compared; crypto rates are typically tiered by notional. Any live rate must be read from the current rulebook. Kurdish and Persian headwords retain the English word margin, which is what practitioners in both languages use.
