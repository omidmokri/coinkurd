Simple definition

The duty to tell the tax authority about transactions that matter for tax, and to keep the records that support what you reported.

Professional definition

The obligation to identify taxable events, measure them in the reporting currency, report them on the prescribed returns and retain supporting records. For digital assets the number of reportable events is the practical problem: a disposal can occur without any fiat ever being received.

Technical definition

Reporting duties follow the characterisation the local tax system applies. Where digital assets are treated as property, as United States federal guidance does, each disposal is potentially a realisation event, including an exchange of one asset for another, so acquisition date, cost basis and proceeds must be tracked per unit. Specific rules on income recognition exist for some activities: United States guidance treats staking rewards as gross income when the taxpayer gains dominion and control. Other countries characterise the same activity differently.

Academic definition

The set of obligations requiring a taxpayer to identify, measure, report and substantiate transactions with tax consequences, the scope of which depends on the characterisation of the underlying asset in the relevant tax system.

Mechanism

Tax systems tax changes in wealth at identifiable moments. On-chain activity multiplies those moments without producing cash: a swap, a conversion into a stablecoin, a reward credited to an address. The administrative burden therefore grows with activity rather than with profit, which is why record keeping, not the rate, is where most non-compliance begins.

In practice

Who uses it
Individual taxpayers, accountants, exchange reporting teams, and anyone holding assets across several platforms and wallets.
Why it matters
It determines what must be declared, in what currency, and what evidence must exist years later if the return is questioned.
Limitations
Characterisation, taxable events, basis methods, rates and deadlines all differ by country and change. This entry describes documented obligations and is not tax advice; readers must check their own jurisdiction.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming nothing is reportable because no fiat currency was received.
  • Reconstructing cost basis from memory years later instead of keeping contemporaneous records.
  • Applying one country's treatment of an activity to another country's return.

Examples

regulatory

Under United States federal guidance digital assets are treated as property, so exchanging one token for another can be a disposal even though no currency was received. Many other countries reach a different conclusion on the same facts.

numeric

A trader makes 300 swaps in a year with no withdrawal to currency. In a property-based system that is up to 300 disposals needing a date, a basis and proceeds each — roughly 900 data points to retain.

Given
  • 300 asset-for-asset swaps in the tax year
  • No conversion to fiat currency at any point
  • Each disposal requires acquisition date, cost basis and proceeds
Steps
  1. Reportable disposals = 300
  2. Data points per disposal = 3
  3. Records to retain = 300 x 3 = 900
About 900 data points from a year with no cash withdrawal

Illustrative count. It applies only where the tax system treats each asset-for-asset exchange as a disposal; other systems do not, and the reader must check their own. No rate, threshold or deadline is stated.

Commonly confused with

Capital Gains

Capital gain is the amount computed on a disposal. Tax reporting is the duty to identify, declare and substantiate it.
