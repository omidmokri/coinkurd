Calmar Ratio

Simple definition

Annualised return divided by the worst peak-to-trough fall over the same period. It asks how much growth you were paid for the deepest loss you had to sit through.

Professional definition

A reward-to-pain measure: compound annual growth rate over the absolute value of maximum drawdown across the measurement window. Unlike the Sharpe ratio its denominator is a single worst-case realisation rather than a dispersion estimate, which makes it intuitive and extremely period-dependent.

Technical definition

Numerator is the compound annual growth rate over the window; denominator is the largest percentage decline from any equity peak to the subsequent trough inside the same window. The denominator is an extreme order statistic, so it is not an unbiased estimate of anything and it never falls as the sample lengthens — it can only stay the same or get worse. Lengthening the window therefore drives the ratio down almost mechanically.

Academic definition

A reward-to-risk ratio relating annualised compound return to the maximum observed peak-to-trough decline over the same sample, where the denominator is an extreme-value statistic rather than a moment.

Mechanism

Maximum drawdown is a running maximum of a running loss, so it is monotone in sample length: add data and it can only rise. The numerator, an annualised rate, has no such ratchet. The ratio therefore mixes a stable statistic with one that deteriorates with observation, and most of the variation between reported Calmar figures comes from the window, not the strategy.

In practice

Who uses it
Managed-futures and trend-following allocators, strategy developers comparing systems, and anyone whose real constraint is the loss a client will tolerate.
Why it matters
It states reward in the unit investors actually abandon strategies over: the deepest fall from a high-water mark, not a standard deviation.
Limitations
One historical path produced one maximum drawdown. The next path will produce a different one, so the denominator carries no confidence interval and the ratio does not forecast.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing a three-year Calmar ratio with a ten-year one.
  • Quoting it without stating the measurement window.
  • Treating the historical maximum drawdown as a worst-case bound.

Examples

numeric

Equity grows to 1.72 times over three years, so the annualised rate is 19.81%. The worst peak-to-trough fall in that window was 25%, giving a Calmar ratio of 0.79.

Given
  • terminal equity multiple = 1.72 over 3 years
  • maximum drawdown in the window = 25%
  • no cash flows in or out
Steps
  1. CAGR = 1.72^(1/3) - 1 = 0.198145, so 19.81% per year
  2. Calmar = 19.81% / 25% = 0.792
Calmar ratio = 0.79

Verified arithmetic. The same strategy measured over a longer window that contains a 50% drawdown and a 15% annualised rate would report 0.30 — a quarter of the figure, from the same system.

pitfall

A manager quotes a Calmar ratio above three. The window turns out to be the eighteen months since the last deep drawdown. Extend the window back through that drawdown and the figure falls below one.

Mathematical foundation

\mathrm{Calmar} = \frac{\mathrm{CAGR}}{\left|\mathrm{MDD}\right|}, \qquad \mathrm{CAGR} = \left(\frac{V_T}{V_0}\right)^{1/y} - 1, \qquad \mathrm{MDD} = \min_{t}\left(\frac{V_t}{\max_{s \le t} V_s} - 1\right)

Variables

\mathrm{CAGR}Compound annual growth rate over the window% per year
\mathrm{MDD}Maximum drawdown: the largest decline from an equity peak to a later trough% (negative)
V_tAccount equity at time tcurrency
yLength of the measurement window in yearsyears

Procedure

Build the equity curve. Track the running peak and record the largest percentage shortfall from that peak; its minimum over the window is maximum drawdown. Compute the compound annual growth rate from the first and last equity values and the exact window length. Divide the rate by the absolute drawdown and always publish the window alongside the number.

Worked example

Given
  • V_0 = 100, V_T = 172
  • window = 3 years exactly
  • maximum drawdown over the window = -25%
Steps
  1. V_T / V_0 = 1.72
  2. CAGR = 1.72^(1/3) - 1 = 0.198145
  3. Calmar = 0.198145 / 0.25 = 0.7926
Calmar ratio = 0.79

Interpretation

A value of one means the annualised return equalled the worst fall endured. Higher means more growth per unit of maximum pain, but only for that window — the number is a description of one realised path.

Assumptions

  • The measurement window must be stated. Conventions differ: the original presentation used a rolling thirty-six month window, while much vendor software uses the whole available sample.
  • Drawdown may be computed on closing equity or on intraperiod extremes, and the two give different denominators for the same strategy.
  • Annualisation of the numerator requires the exact window length; approximating three years as 750 trading days changes the result.
Limits of the formula
  • The denominator is a single extreme observation with no standard error, so the ratio cannot be tested for significance in the ordinary way.
  • Maximum drawdown is monotone in sample length, which biases long track records downwards and short ones upwards.
  • It ignores drawdown duration entirely: a 25% fall recovered in a month and one lasting three years score identically.

Commonly confused with

Drawdown

Drawdown is the raw loss measure. The Calmar ratio is one way of pricing return against it, and inherits every weakness of the underlying statistic.

Commonly confused with

Sharpe Ratio

Sharpe scales return by dispersion across all periods; Calmar scales it by one worst outcome. Sharpe has a sampling distribution, Calmar effectively does not.
