Simple definition

A short pause after a sharp move. A small sloping rectangle is a flag; a small converging triangle is a pennant.

Professional definition

Continuation shapes. After a steep move — the pole — price consolidates briefly, either in a narrow channel sloping against the move (a flag) or in a small converging range (a pennant). The convention measures the pole and projects it from the breakout, and treats the pause as short by definition.

Technical definition

The definition is entirely parametric: how steep the pole must be, how long the pause may run before it is something else, and how tight the boundaries must be. Short pauses after large moves are frequent, so a loose specification finds flags everywhere and a tight one finds almost none. The pole-projection target has no empirical support in this registry; what is observable is that realised range contracts during the pause.

Academic definition

A short consolidation following a steep directional move, bounded either by parallel or by convergent lines, conventionally classified as a continuation configuration.

Mechanism

A steep move leaves some holders in profit and some latecomers chasing. A brief, narrow pause means neither group forces a resolution: profit-taking is absorbed without a deep retracement. That absorption is why the next move can be quick when the pause ends, and also why a pause that deepens stops being a flag and starts being a reversal.

In practice

Who uses it
Momentum and breakout traders looking for re-entry after a strong move, and developers writing continuation filters.
Why it matters
It isolates a brief contraction inside an established move, which gives a near trigger and a tight invalidation at the far boundary.
Limitations
The pattern is defined by thresholds the author chooses, published out-of-sample evidence is weak, and the pole-projection target is a convention with no measured expectation behind it.

Common mistakes

  • Letting a flag run on until it is really a range, then keeping the label.
  • Projecting the pole and calling the result a forecast.
  • Measuring the pole from a different starting point than the one tested.

Examples

chart

Price runs from 100 to 120 in four candles, then spends six candles drifting between 116 and 119. Range has collapsed while the trend direction is unchanged — the condition the label describes, before any claim about what follows.

numeric

The pole runs twenty points, from 100 to 120. The pause holds between 116 and 119, retracing one fifth of the pole. A break above 119 projects 139 by the usual convention.

Given
  • pole from 100 to 120
  • pause range 116 to 119
  • breakout above 119
  • convention: project the pole length from the breakout
Steps
  1. pole length = 120 - 100 = 20
  2. retracement during the pause = 120 - 116 = 4
  3. 4 / 20 = 20% of the pole retraced
  4. target = 119 + 20 = 139
Measured target 139, with the pause retracing 20% of the pole.

Had the pause retraced most of the pole it would not be called a flag. The depth threshold is a convention and the target is geometry, not a probability.

Commonly confused with

Pullback

A pullback is any counter-move inside a trend. A flag is a pullback that stays shallow and narrow, and carries a continuation claim plus a projected target.
