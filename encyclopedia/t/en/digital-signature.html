Digital Signature

Simple definition

Proof, made with your private key, that you approved one specific message. Anyone can check it with your public key, and no one learns the key itself.

Professional definition

A scheme of three operations: key generation, signing and verification. The private key signs a message and produces a signature; the matching public key verifies that the signature is authentic and bound to that exact message. Verification needs no secret, so every node can do it.

Technical definition

Signing proves authorisation without revealing the signing key: the signature is a function of the message and the private key from which the key cannot be recovered. Altering one byte of the message invalidates the signature, so integrity comes with authenticity. On a chain a signature authorises a state change and nothing more — it carries no statement about intent, legal capacity or who held the key, and a stolen key signs exactly as validly as its owner.

Academic definition

An asymmetric authentication scheme in which a secret key yields a message-bound tag verifiable under the corresponding public key, providing authenticity, integrity and non-repudiation under a standard hardness assumption.

Mechanism

Asymmetry does the work: the two halves of a key pair are joined by a one-way operation, so the public half can check a signature but cannot produce one. That is why a chain can let strangers police spending — every node verifies authorisation, and only the key holder can grant it.

In practice

Who uses it
Wallet developers, custody and multisignature operators, exchange withdrawal systems, and node implementers.
Why it matters
It is how ownership is exercised on a chain: no account record is consulted, only whether a valid signature authorises the state change.
Limitations
It proves a key signed, not that a person did. Key theft, blind-signing a hostile payload and social engineering all produce perfectly valid signatures.

Common mistakes

  • Approving a signing request without reading what is actually being signed.
  • Treating a valid signature as proof of a named person's consent.
  • Assuming a signature encrypts or conceals the message.

Examples

crypto

A spend transaction carries a signature over its own contents. Change the destination address and the signature no longer verifies, so an intermediary cannot redirect the funds in flight.

everyday

A handwritten signature can be copied and is tied to a person by resemblance. A digital signature cannot be copied onto a different document, because it is computed from that document's contents.
