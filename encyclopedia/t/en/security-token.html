Simple definition

A token that a legal system treats as an investment product rather than as a simple digital item. That treatment brings registration, disclosure and sales rules with it.

Professional definition

A crypto-asset characterised under applicable law as a security or transferable financial instrument, so the issuer and intermediaries fall under securities rules: prospectus or exemption, licensed distribution, market-abuse rules and ongoing disclosure. The characterisation follows economic substance, and the same instrument may be classified differently in different jurisdictions.

Technical definition

Classification is legal, not technical. In the European Union, assets qualifying as financial instruments fall outside MiCA and under the existing financial-instrument regime, which is why MiCA's own categories matter mainly for assets that are not securities. In the United States the question is whether an arrangement is an investment contract, assessed through case law on substance over form. Because the test is applied to the whole offering and not only to the code, identical token standards can sit on either side of the line.

Academic definition

A digital representation of an interest that satisfies the statutory or jurisprudential definition of a security in the relevant jurisdiction, with legal consequences attaching to the issuance, distribution and trading of that representation.

Mechanism

Securities law targets the relationship, not the container: if money is raised from the public on an expectation of return generated by others, the protective rules attach whatever the wrapper is called. That is why wrapping an interest in a token changes settlement and transferability but not the legal characterisation, and why the same token can be lawfully distributed in one country and not in another.

In practice

Who uses it
Issuers and their counsel, licensed intermediaries and venues, compliance and listing committees, and supervisors applying each jurisdiction's own test.
Why it matters
The classification decides whether an offering needs a prospectus or exemption, who may distribute it, and what continuing disclosure the holder is owed.
Limitations
This encyclopedia does not classify assets. Determinations are made by competent authorities and courts, differ between jurisdictions, and change over time.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming a token is not a security because its documents say utility.
  • Carrying a classification from one country into another.
  • Treating tokenisation as a way to avoid securities rules rather than a change of settlement rail.

Examples

regulatory

In the EU, a token that qualifies as a financial instrument is not governed by MiCA's crypto-asset regime but by the existing instrument rules. The same token offered elsewhere may be assessed under a different national test entirely.

comparison

Tokenising a bond changes how it settles and who can hold it on-chain. It does not convert it into something other than a debt security, so the disclosure and distribution duties travel with it.

Commonly confused with

Utility Token

Both names describe how law reads an offering, not how the code is written. A single token can be a utility token in one framework's categories and a financial instrument in another's.
