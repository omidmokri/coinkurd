Equal Highs and Equal Lows

Simple definition

Two or more swing highs at almost the same price, or two swing lows at almost the same price. They are watched because stop orders tend to pile up just beyond them.

Professional definition

A pair or cluster of swing extremes within a small tolerance of each other. In smart-money teaching they are read as a visible pool of resting stop orders, and the expectation is that price reaches beyond them before the real direction resolves. Classical charting calls the same shape a double top or bottom.

Technical definition

Codeable with an explicit tolerance: two confirmed swing highs qualify when the absolute difference between them is no greater than a stated epsilon, best expressed as a fraction of average true range rather than in ticks so the rule travels across instruments. Teachings differ on whether wicks or closes are compared, how many bars may separate the two, and how many highs are needed, so the detected set is tolerance-dependent.

Academic definition

A cluster of approximately coincident local extrema in a price series, identified within a stated tolerance and interpreted in practitioner frameworks as a concentration of conditional orders.

Mechanism

Stops do cluster beyond obvious highs and lows, because that is where traders place them, and a move through such a level can trigger them and add one-directional orders. That part is ordinary microstructure. What cannot be seen on a candle chart is whether the orders are actually there, or who put them there, so the pool is inferred rather than observed.

In practice

Who uses it
Price-action and smart-money traders choosing targets and stop locations, and developers writing a tolerance-based cluster detector.
Why it matters
It marks a level that many participants are watching, which is useful both as a target to aim at and as a place not to leave a stop sitting.
Limitations
The tolerance is a free parameter, the resting-order claim cannot be verified from candles, and no peer-reviewed evidence base exists.

Common mistakes

  • Using a fixed tick tolerance across instruments of different volatility.
  • Placing a stop exactly on the level.
  • Assuming the sweep must happen before the move.

Examples

market

A market stalls twice within a few ticks of the same high over two weeks. Traders mark the level as a pool, aim partial targets just above it, and move their own stops away from the obvious spot underneath the matching lows.

numeric

Two swing highs print at 1.0842 and 1.0846, four pips apart. With average true range at 80 pips, a tolerance of five percent of range allows four pips, so they qualify. A one-pip tolerance rejects them and no level is marked.

Given
  • swing high 1 = 1.0842
  • swing high 2 = 1.0846
  • average true range = 80 pips
  • tolerance = 5 percent of ATR
Steps
  1. difference = 1.0846 - 1.0842 = 4 pips
  2. tolerance = 0.05 x 80 = 4 pips
  3. 4 <= 4, so the pair qualifies as equal highs
  4. under a 1-pip tolerance: 4 > 1, so it does not
The same two highs are equal or unequal depending on the tolerance.

A volatility-scaled tolerance keeps the rule comparable across instruments; a fixed tick tolerance does not.

Commonly confused with

Double Top and Double Bottom

The two describe the same geometry with opposite expectations: classical charting reads a double top as a reversal pattern, smart-money teaching reads equal highs as a level price is likely to run through first. Same candles, contradictory readings.

Flagged by the editor as uncertain on a linguistic, mathematical or regulatory point.

That conditional orders cluster beyond obvious levels is supported by the microstructure literature cited; that a given pair of equal highs holds a pool of such orders, and that price is likely to take it, is an inference from candles and belongs to the retail smart-money tradition, which no registry source documents and for which no peer-reviewed evidence base exists. The tolerance, the number of highs required and wick-versus-close comparison all vary between teachers. Kurdish headword is a proposal by this project and needs native-expert review.
