Maker and Taker

Trading Fundamentals

Foundation

Simple definition

Two roles in one trade. The maker's order was already resting in the book; the taker's order arrived and hit it. Venues usually charge them different fees.

Professional definition

A classification applied per fill, not per trader. The resting side is the maker and supplies liquidity; the arriving side is the taker and consumes it. Fee schedules price the two asymmetrically, often with a rebate or a lower rate for makers, in order to pay for displayed depth.

Technical definition

The venue labels each execution by which order was already in the book at match time. A single order can be both across its fills: part matches on arrival as taker and the remainder rests and later fills as maker. A post-only flag forces the maker role by rejecting anything that would take; reduce-only is orthogonal to it. The fee asymmetry is a subsidy for liquidity provision funded by taker fees, and its size determines whether a passive strategy is viable at all.

Academic definition

A per-execution classification distinguishing liquidity supply from liquidity demand, used as the basis for asymmetric transaction pricing intended to subsidise quote provision.

Mechanism

Displayed depth is a public good for the venue: it attracts flow, and flow attracts more depth. Paying makers out of taker fees buys that depth. If the two rates converge, posting stops paying and the book thins — so the asymmetry is a deliberate design choice, not an accident of pricing.

In practice

Who uses it
Fee-sensitive strategies, market makers, high-frequency desks, and any trader comparing passive with aggressive execution.
Why it matters
It changes the sign of the cost on half your fills. At scale a maker rebate can turn a break-even strategy into a profitable one.
Limitations
Maker status is not free: it costs queue time and carries adverse selection. Tiers and rebates depend on rolling volume and are revised by venues.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing venues on the taker fee alone.
  • Assuming a post-only order will fill because it would have.
  • Counting a maker rebate as profit while ignoring adverse selection.

Examples

market

An order for 100 units matches 40 against resting offers on arrival and rests with 60. The venue bills the first 40 at the taker rate and, when the remainder fills later, the last 60 at the maker rate.

numeric

On a 100,000 round trip, taker on both legs at 0.05% costs 100. Maker on both legs at 0.02% costs 40. Over a thousand round trips that difference is 60,000.

Given
  • notional 100,000 per leg
  • taker rate 0.05%, maker rate 0.02% (illustrative)
  • two legs per round trip, 1,000 round trips
Steps
  1. taker round trip = 2 x 100000 x 0.0005 = 100
  2. maker round trip = 2 x 100000 x 0.0002 = 40
  3. difference per round trip = 60
  4. over 1,000 round trips = 60,000
60 per round trip, 60,000 over a thousand

The maker path is conditional on being filled while resting; weight it by fill probability before treating the saving as earned.

Commonly confused with

Market Maker versus Taker

Maker and taker are labels the venue puts on each fill for billing. Market maker versus taker is a contrast of business models, with different risks and different sources of edge.
