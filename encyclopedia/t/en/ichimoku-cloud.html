Ichimoku Cloud

Simple definition

A five-line system on one chart: two midpoint lines, a shaded cloud drawn ahead of price, and the close plotted back in time.

Professional definition

Ichimoku Kinko Hyo. Tenkan-sen is the midpoint of the last 9 periods' range and Kijun-sen of the last 26. Senkou Span A is the average of those two and Senkou Span B the midpoint of 52, both plotted 26 periods ahead; the shaded area between them is the cloud. Chikou Span is the current close plotted 26 periods back.

Technical definition

Every line is a midpoint of extremes rather than a mean of closes, so the system tracks the high-low envelope and not closing drift. The forward displacement is not lookahead: the cloud drawn over the current bar was computed 26 periods ago from data available then. The trap is in code, where the offset must be applied deliberately, since comparing price at t with an undisplaced Span A from the same bar compares price against a line belonging 26 bars later. The 9/26/52 settings are conventions.

Academic definition

A multi-component trend system built from midpoints of the high-low range over three lookbacks, with two components displaced forward and one displaced backward by a fixed offset.

Mechanism

Taking the midpoint of a window's highest high and lowest low makes each line a centre of the recent range rather than an average of prices, so the lines flatten when the range is stable and step when a new extreme arrives. Displacing two of them forward produces a reference band whose values for today were fixed weeks ago, which makes the cloud a record rather than a forecast.

In practice

Who uses it
Trend traders wanting one framework for direction, momentum and reference levels, and developers implementing displaced series carefully.
Why it matters
It supplies trend direction, a reference band and two crossover lines from one parameter set, with every value determined by data already in hand.
Limitations
The parameters are historical conventions tied to a six-day trading week, five correlated lines invite selective reading, and the displacement is a frequent source of implementation error.

Common mistakes

  • Indexing the cloud without applying the 26-period displacement.
  • Reading the chikou span against current price without aligning the offset.
  • Retuning 9, 26 and 52 and then citing conventional interpretations.

Examples

chart

The cloud drawn over today was computed from prices 26 periods ago, so it is already fixed whatever happens now. The portion drawn to the right of the last candle is likewise fixed, and will not change as new bars arrive.

numeric

The 9-period range is 104 to 120 and the 26-period range 96 to 124, so Tenkan is 112 and Kijun 110. Span A is 111 and, with a 52-period range of 86 to 130, Span B is 108. Both are plotted 26 periods ahead.

Given
  • 9-period high 120, low 104
  • 26-period high 124, low 96
  • 52-period high 130, low 86
  • current close = 118
Steps
  1. Tenkan = (120 + 104) / 2 = 112
  2. Kijun = (124 + 96) / 2 = 110
  3. Senkou A = (112 + 110) / 2 = 111, plotted at t+26
  4. Senkou B = (130 + 86) / 2 = 108, plotted at t+26
  5. Chikou = 118, plotted at t-26
At bar t+26 the cloud spans 108 to 111, a thickness of 3 points, already determined at bar t.

Every one of these numbers comes from data at or before bar t. The forward plotting is presentation, not a use of future information.

Mathematical foundation

\begin{aligned} \text{Tenkan-sen}_t &= \frac{HH_9 + LL_9}{2} \\ \text{Kijun-sen}_t &= \frac{HH_{26} + LL_{26}}{2} \\ \text{Senkou Span A}_{t+26} &= \frac{\text{Tenkan-sen}_t + \text{Kijun-sen}_t}{2} \\ \text{Senkou Span B}_{t+26} &= \frac{HH_{52} + LL_{52}}{2} \\ \text{Chikou Span}_{t-26} &= C_t \end{aligned}

Procedure

For each bar take the highest high and lowest low over 9, 26 and 52 periods and average each pair to get three midpoints. Tenkan is the 9 midpoint, Kijun the 26. Average Tenkan and Kijun for Span A and take the 52 midpoint for Span B, then plot both 26 bars to the right of the bar that produced them. Plot the current close 26 bars to the left as the Chikou Span.

  5. Senkou A and B are plotted at t+26; Chikou 118 is plotted at t-26

Interpretation

The lines report where the centre of the recent range sits over three horizons, and the cloud shows what those centres were 26 periods ago. Price above the cloud says the current price is above the range centres of a month back; it is a comparison across time, not a signal.

Assumptions

  • The defaults are 9, 26 and 52 with a 26-period forward displacement. They come from a six-day Japanese trading week in which 26 sessions approximated a month, and have no derivation beyond that.
  • Every line is a midpoint of the highest high and lowest low, not an average of closes; implementations that substitute a moving average are computing something else.
  • Implementations differ by one period in the displacement, depending on whether the current bar is counted as the first of the 26. The difference is small but systematic and shows up when comparing platforms.
  • The forward displacement uses no future data. A backtest only sees future information if the code reads an undisplaced span and compares it with the price of the same bar.
Limits of the formula
  • Five correlated lines on one chart make selective interpretation easy; any rule built on them should state exactly which lines and which offsets it uses.
  • The periods carry no calendar meaning outside a six-day trading week, so on a five-day equity week or a 24/7 crypto chart they are simply three lookbacks.
  • Because the lines use extremes rather than averages, a single spike bar fixes a line for the whole lookback that follows.

Commonly confused with

Moving Average

A moving average averages closing prices. Every Ichimoku line is the midpoint between a window's highest high and lowest low, so it ignores where price closed inside that range.
