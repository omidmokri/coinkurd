Index Price

Level
Professional

Simple definition

An average price for an asset computed from several independent exchanges. It is used as a neutral reference, and you cannot trade it directly.

Professional definition

A composite reference price built from constituent venue prices, usually a weighted average with published rules for dropping outliers and stale or disconnected feeds. Derivatives venues use it as the backbone of mark price, funding and final settlement.

Technical definition

A weighted aggregate of constituent prices where weights may be equal, volume-based or liquidity-based, and where the methodology specifies exclusion of a constituent that deviates beyond a threshold, whose feed is stale, or whose venue has halted. The index is not tradeable: there is no order book for it, so closing a gap between index and venue price requires trading the constituents. Methodologies and constituent lists are published by the venue and are revised over time.

Academic definition

A rule-based composite of prices drawn from independent trading venues, constructed to estimate a consensus price more robust to single-venue dislocation than any individual constituent.

Mechanism

The point of aggregating is cost of attack. Moving one venue's book is cheap; moving a weighted basket of independent venues at the same instant is not. Outlier and staleness rules exist because the cheapest attack on an index is not the price itself but one broken or captured constituent feed.

In practice

Who uses it
Derivatives venues, risk teams, oracle designers, and anyone auditing a liquidation or an expiry settlement.
Why it matters
It is the manipulation-resistant anchor under mark price, funding and settlement, so it, not the venue print, decides most disputes.
Limitations
Composition and weights are venue decisions. A constituent outage, or a dislocation affecting several constituents at once, moves the index in ways no single chart shows.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the index as a price you can trade.
  • Assuming two venues' indices for the same asset agree.
  • Ignoring a published change to the constituent list.

Examples

market

A venue announces that one constituent exchange is suspended from its index after a withdrawal halt. Nothing changed in the asset; the reference price changed, and with it every margin ratio on that venue.

numeric

Four constituents quote 100.00, 100.20, 99.80 and 95.00. Equal weighting gives 98.75; excluding the constituent that deviates by more than two percent gives 100.00. The exclusion rule is worth 125 basis points here.

Given
  • constituent prices: 100.00, 100.20, 99.80, 95.00
  • equal weights before exclusion
  • exclusion rule: drop any constituent deviating more than 2% from the median of the set
Steps
  1. naive mean = (100.00 + 100.20 + 99.80 + 95.00) / 4 = 395.00 / 4 = 98.75
  2. median of the set = (99.80 + 100.00) / 2 = 99.90
  3. 95.00 deviates by (99.90 - 95.00) / 99.90 = 4.9%, exceeding 2%, so it is dropped
  4. remaining weights renormalised to 1/3 each: (100.00 + 100.20 + 99.80) / 3 = 100.00
index 100.00 with the exclusion rule, 98.75 without it

The 2% threshold and the use of the median as the deviation yardstick are chosen here for illustration. Real methodologies differ and are published per venue.

Mathematical foundation

P_{index} = \sum_{i \in S} w_i P_i, \qquad \sum_{i \in S} w_i = 1

Variables

Procedure

Collect each constituent's price at the same reference timestamp. Apply the published exclusion rules: drop stale feeds, halted venues and prices deviating beyond the stated threshold. Renormalise the remaining weights to sum to one, then take the weighted sum.

Worked example

Given
  • four constituents at 100.00, 100.20, 99.80, 95.00
  • equal weights of 0.25 before exclusion
  • exclusion threshold 2% from the median
Steps
  1. median = 99.90
  2. 95.00 deviates 4.9% and is excluded
  3. S has three members, w_i = 1/3
  4. P_index = (1/3)(100.00) + (1/3)(100.20) + (1/3)(99.80)
P_index = 100.00

Interpretation

The output is an estimate of a consensus price, not a price at which anything traded. Its usefulness rests entirely on the exclusion rules and on the independence of the constituents.

Assumptions

  • Weights, the deviation threshold and the staleness rule are venue choices, not a standard: two indices on the same asset can legitimately differ.
  • Equal weighting is one convention; volume-weighting and liquidity-weighting are others, and they respond differently to a thin constituent.
  • Some methodologies use a median or a trimmed mean instead of a weighted mean. Those are different statistics and are not covered by this formula.
Limits of the formula
  • If several constituents dislocate together, no exclusion rule can rescue the index.
  • Because the index is not tradeable, a gap between it and a venue price is not automatically an arbitrage: closing it requires trading each constituent.

Commonly confused with

Mark Price

The index is the external composite that feeds the venue. The mark is what the venue applies to your margin, and it may add a basis adjustment or venue data on top of the index.
