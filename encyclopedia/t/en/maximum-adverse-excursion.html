Maximum Adverse Excursion

Simple definition

The worst the price went against a trade while it was open. It is the unrealised loss at the single deepest point, whether or not the trade ended as a winner.

Professional definition

For a long, the difference between the entry price and the lowest price reached before the exit; mirrored for a short. Collected across a sample it gives the distribution of how much adverse movement each trade had to survive, which is the empirical basis for choosing a stop distance rather than guessing one.

Technical definition

Measured per trade from the intrabar extreme between entry and exit, and normally expressed in multiples of the initial risk so trades of different sizes are comparable. The distribution of MAE over winning trades is the diagnostic that matters: a stop placed inside it converts winners into losers, and a stop placed far outside it enlarges every loss for protection the sample says was never needed.

Academic definition

The maximum unrealised loss attained during the holding period of a position, evaluated as a per-trade statistic and used to characterise the adverse-excursion distribution of a trading rule.

Mechanism

A stop is a bet on the shape of this distribution. Price must be allowed to move against a position by the amount that normal noise produces, or the position is closed by noise; allow more than that, and every loss is paid at full size. MAE makes the trade-off measurable instead of intuitive, because it is recorded from trades that actually happened.

In practice

Who uses it
Systematic developers calibrating stop distance, discretionary traders reviewing a journal, and risk reviewers checking whether stops match the strategy's behaviour.
Why it matters
It answers the stop question with the strategy's own history: how far winners were allowed to go against the entry before they worked.
Limitations
It is measured on trades that were taken under one stop rule, so widening the stop changes the sample it is measured on. Intrabar extremes also depend on data resolution.

Common mistakes

  • Reading it only on losers, where it equals the stop by construction.
  • Setting a stop at the maximum observed value and calling it safe.
  • Comparing it in currency rather than in multiples of risk.

Examples

numeric

A long entered at 100 with a stop at 96 risks four. Price falls to 96.2 before rallying to a 108 exit. The adverse excursion was 3.8, which is 0.95 of the risk: the trade worked, and a stop one percent tighter would have killed it.

Given
  • entry = 100, initial stop = 96, so R = 4
  • lowest price before exit = 96.2
  • exit = 108
Steps
  1. MAE in price = 100 - 96.2 = 3.8
  2. MAE in risk units = 3.8 / 4 = 0.95R
  3. result in risk units = (108 - 100) / 4 = 2.0R
A 2.0R winner whose worst open loss reached 0.95R.

If many winners show figures near 1.0R, the stop is sitting at the edge of normal noise and small changes to it will move the win rate sharply.

market

Across 80 winning trades, the ninetieth percentile of adverse excursion is 0.7 of the risk. A desk reading that chooses to keep the current stop and reject proposals to tighten it, since tightening would have removed roughly a tenth of the winners.

Mathematical foundation

\mathrm{MAE}_{\text{long}} = P_{\text{entry}} - \min_{t \in [t_0, t_1]} L_t, \qquad \mathrm{MAE}_{\text{short}} = \max_{t \in [t_0, t_1]} H_t - P_{\text{entry}}

Variables

L_tLow of bar tprice units

Procedure

For each closed trade, find the extreme price reached against the position between the entry and exit timestamps, using the same bar resolution throughout. Subtract to get the adverse excursion in price, multiply by size for currency, and divide by the initial risk to express it in risk units. Then study the distribution separately for winners and losers.

Worked example

Given
  • long entry = 100
  • initial stop = 96, so R = 4 per unit
  • lowest low during the trade = 96.2
  • exit = 108
Steps
  1. MAE = 100 - 96.2 = 3.8
  2. in risk units = 3.8 / 4 = 0.95
  3. trade result = 108 - 100 = 8, that is 2.0R
MAE = 3.8 in price, 0.95R in risk units, on a trade that returned 2.0R.

Interpretation

A figure near zero means the trade never went against the entry; a figure near one risk unit means it nearly reached the stop. Read across winners, the distribution shows how much room the strategy needs; read across losers, it mostly reproduces the stop rule itself.

Assumptions

  • The intrabar extreme is taken from the same bar resolution for every trade. Measuring on daily bars understates the excursion that a lower timeframe would reveal.
  • Entry and exit fills are the actual executed prices, not signal prices.
  • Initial risk R is defined as the distance to the first stop and is not revised when the stop is later moved.
  • The sample comes from one unchanged rule set, including one unchanged stop rule.
Limits of the formula
  • The statistic is conditional on the stop that was in force: trades that were stopped out might have gone further, so the observed distribution is censored.
  • It is a per-trade measure and says nothing about simultaneous open risk across positions, which is what portfolio heat addresses.
  • On illiquid instruments the recorded extreme may be a single thin print that no size could have traded at.

Commonly confused with

Drawdown

Drawdown is a fall in account equity across trades. Maximum adverse excursion is the worst unrealised loss inside one trade. A string of small losses creates drawdown with no large excursion anywhere.

Flagged by the editor as uncertain on a linguistic, mathematical or regulatory point.

The measure itself is a straightforward definition and is uncontested; the practice of calibrating stops from its distribution is a professional convention, and the censoring problem stated in the limits means it cannot be read as the distance a stop should always be. Persian and Kurdish headwords are both proposals by this project; practitioners in both languages typically say MAE, which is kept in the keywords and the abbreviation. Needs native-expert review.
