Pullback

ENGLISHPullbackreference
فارسیپولبکEstablished equivalent
کوردیگەڕانەوەی کاتیProposed equivalent — not standard
Category
Technical Analysis
Level
Foundation

Simple definition

A temporary move against the trend before it carries on — a dip inside a rise, or a bounce inside a fall.

Professional definition

A counter-trend move that gives back part of the previous swing without breaking the structure that defines the trend. In the moment it is indistinguishable from the start of a reversal, which is why a pullback is always a provisional label, confirmed only by continuation.

Technical definition

A pullback and a reversal are the same price action until one of them invalidates a structural reference: a prior swing point, a trend state, or a stated maximum depth. Any mechanical definition must therefore fix the swing identification rule, the depth allowed and the confirmation required, and each of those choices changes both the number of events found and the performance measured on them.

Academic definition

A counter-trend retracement of a prior impulse that leaves the structural definition of the prevailing trend intact.

Mechanism

After a fast move some holders take profit, and participants who missed it wait for a better price rather than pay the extreme. Both reduce pressure in the trend's direction without anyone changing their view, so price drifts back toward the area the move began from, where that waiting demand sits. The trend resumes if the waiting demand outweighs the profit taking.

In practice

Who uses it
Trend traders looking for a better entry than the breakout, position builders scaling into an established move, and anyone who wants invalidation close to entry.
Why it matters
Entering on a pullback puts the entry nearer the invalidation level, so the same money risked buys a larger position than chasing the extreme does.
Limitations
Every reversal begins as a pullback, the label is retrospective, depth bounds are arbitrary, and insisting on one means missing every trend that never offers it.

Common mistakes

  • Still calling it a pullback after the prior swing low has been taken out.
  • Averaging down into a deepening move with no invalidation level.
  • Assuming a fixed depth, such as half the swing, has to be reached.

Examples

market

A trend makes a higher high, then falls for five sessions and settles just above the previous higher low. While that low holds, the move is classified as a pullback; the session it closes below, the classification changes.

numeric

A swing runs from 100 to 140 and then falls to 124. The move has given back sixteen of its forty points, a depth of forty percent, and the swing low at 100 is still intact.

Given
  • swing start = 100
  • swing extreme = 140
  • pullback low = 124
Steps
  1. swing height = 140 - 100 = 40
  2. retraced = 140 - 124 = 16
  3. depth = 16 / 40
depth = 0.40, or 40% of the swing

Depth is descriptive arithmetic. No depth value is known to separate pullbacks from reversals; the structural reference does that, not the percentage.

Commonly confused with

Break of Structure

A pullback leaves the prior swing point intact. Once that point is taken out, the same move is classified as a break of structure, and the trend label changes with it.
