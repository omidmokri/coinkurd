Vesting Schedule

Simple definition

The timetable on which tokens allocated to a team, investor or fund become transferable. A cliff is a date before which nothing releases; after it, tokens usually release gradually.

Professional definition

A contractual or contract-enforced release profile for an allocation, defined by a cliff, a total vesting period, and a release frequency. It determines when a given tranche can first be sold, and in aggregate it is the main predictable source of float growth in a token's early years.

Technical definition

Two designs with identical headline terms can release very differently depending on whether the cliff counts towards the vesting period, whether release is continuous or at discrete intervals, and whether unvested tokens can be delegated, staked or used as collateral while locked. On-chain enforcement matters too: a vesting contract that the issuer can upgrade is a disclosure, not a constraint. Aggregate schedules across all allocations are additive, so the quantity that matters for market impact is the combined release curve, not any single tranche.

Academic definition

A time-indexed release function governing when allocated but non-transferable units become transferable, typically parameterised by a cliff period, a total vesting horizon and a release frequency.

Mechanism

A lock does not reduce anyone's intention to sell, it only postpones the ability. So the schedule is a map of future supply arriving at known dates, which is why unlock dates are watched and why the largest effects are usually seen before them rather than on them. A cliff concentrates that effect: it converts a year of gradual release into one discrete event.

In practice

Who uses it
Project teams and their investors, exchanges assessing float at listing, analysts building unlock calendars, and holders sizing exposure around known dates.
Why it matters
It is the most predictable component of future supply, available from documents before it shows up in any market data.
Limitations
A schedule states capability, not behaviour: recipients may hold, sell, hedge or borrow against tokens, and terms can be amended if the contract allows.

Common mistakes

  • Reading a long total period as alignment without checking the cliff.
  • Looking at one tranche instead of the combined release curve.
  • Assuming an on-chain vesting contract cannot be changed.

Examples

numeric

An allocation of 60 million vests over 48 months with a 12-month cliff. At the cliff, 15 million unlock in one day; after that about 1.25 million unlock each month for three years.

Given
  • allocation A = 60,000,000 tokens
  • total vesting horizon T = 48 months
  • cliff c = 12 months, counted within T
  • monthly release after the cliff
Steps
  1. vested fraction at the cliff = c / T = 12 / 48 = 0.25
  2. released at the cliff = 0.25 x 60,000,000 = 15,000,000
  3. remaining = 45,000,000 over 36 months
  4. 45,000,000 / 36 = 1,250,000 per month
15,000,000 at the cliff, then 1,250,000 per month for 36 months

Hypothetical terms. If the cliff does not count within T, the cliff-day release is zero and the monthly amount is larger; the contract wording decides which applies.

comparison

Two projects both advertise four-year vesting. One starts releasing in month one; the other releases a quarter of the allocation on a single day in month twelve. The headline term is identical and the supply profile is not.

Mathematical foundation

V(t) = \begin{cases} 0, & t < c \\ A \times \dfrac{\min(t, T)}{T}, & t \geq c \end{cases}

Procedure

Confirm from the contract whether the cliff period counts inside the total horizon, then apply the ratio of elapsed to total time to the allocation. For a discrete schedule, replace the continuous ratio with the number of release events completed divided by the total number of events.

Worked example

Given
  • A = 60,000,000 tokens
  • c = 12 months
  • T = 48 months
  • t = 12 months (the cliff date), cliff counted within T
Steps
  1. t >= c, so the second branch applies
  2. min(12, 48) / 48 = 0.25
  3. V = 60,000,000 x 0.25 = 15,000,000
V(12) = 15,000,000 tokens transferable on the cliff date

Interpretation

The output is the maximum amount the recipient may transfer by that date, not an amount they will transfer. It is a constraint on supply, not a prediction of selling.

Assumptions

  • Linear vesting after the cliff, with the cliff counted inside the total horizon. Both conventions exist and the contract text decides which applies, so the same headline terms can produce different numbers.
  • Release is treated as continuous. Monthly or quarterly schedules step instead, and the step size is what the market reacts to.
  • The vesting contract is enforced and not amendable. Where it can be upgraded, the schedule is a statement of intent.
Limits of the formula
  • It describes transferability only, not whether tokens are sold, hedged, staked or pledged while locked.
  • A single tranche's curve is not the market-relevant quantity; schedules across all allocations must be summed.
  • Nothing here implies a price effect at any date: that is an empirical question the formula does not address.
