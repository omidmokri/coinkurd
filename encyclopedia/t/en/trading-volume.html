Foundation

Simple definition

How much of an asset changed hands during a period, counted in units or in money. It says how busy the market was, not which way it went.

Professional definition

The total quantity transacted over an interval on a stated venue. Volume is a flow measured per bar and reset each bar, unlike open interest, which is a stock. Its analytic value is as a weight on price information: the same move on thin and on heavy volume represents very different amounts of participation.

Technical definition

Volume is venue-specific and convention-specific. Each trade is one print with two counterparties, so what is counted differs between reporting standards; aggregated crypto figures sum across venues and can count one economic transfer several times. Volume also carries no direction — splitting it into buying and selling requires a separate classification rule, such as the aggressor flag or a tick test, and that rule is an assumption, not data.

Academic definition

The aggregate quantity of an instrument transacted within a stated interval on a stated venue; a flow variable defined only relative to that specification.

Mechanism

Volume measures participation. A price move needs someone to take the other side; when many do, the resulting price has been agreed by more capital and is harder to reverse cheaply. When few do, the same displacement can be produced by one order against a thin book. That is why two identical candles can mean very different things.

In practice

Who uses it
Anyone validating a break, execution desks measuring participation rate, exchanges and regulators monitoring market quality, and analysts comparing venue activity.
Why it matters
It is the simplest measure of how much capital stood behind a move, and it is a required input to VWAP and to participation-based execution schedules.
Limitations
Self-reported by venues, not comparable across them, and inflated by wash trading in unregulated markets. It also gives no direction without an extra classification rule.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing 24-hour volume across venues with different reporting conventions.
  • Calling a move confirmed because volume rose, with no baseline.
  • Confusing volume with open interest in derivatives markets.

Examples

crypto

An aggregator reports a token's daily volume by adding the figures from twenty venues. One market maker quoting on all twenty, and one arbitrage loop between two of them, both appear many times in that single number.

numeric

Three trades fill in one bar: two units at 100, five at 101 and three at 99. Volume is ten units, notional is 1002, and dividing one by the other gives the bar's volume-weighted average price of 100.2.

Given
  • trade 1: 2 units at 100
  • trade 2: 5 units at 101
  • trade 3: 3 units at 99
Steps
  1. V = 2 + 5 + 3 = 10 units
  2. notional = 2x100 + 5x101 + 3x99 = 200 + 505 + 297 = 1002
  3. 1002 / 10 = 100.2
volume = 10 units, notional volume = 1002, VWAP of the bar = 100.2

Unit volume and notional volume are different quantities. Venues report sometimes one and sometimes the other, which is one reason cross-venue comparisons mislead.

Mathematical foundation

V_T = \sum_{i=1}^{m} q_i, \qquad V^{N}_T = \sum_{i=1}^{m} p_i\,q_i

Variables

q_iQuantity of the i-th trade in the intervalunits of the base asset
p_iPrice of the i-th tradequote currency
mNumber of trades printed in the intervalcount
V_T, V^{N}_TUnit volume and notional volume for interval Tbase units; quote currency

Procedure

Take every trade printed in the interval on one venue and sum the quantities for unit volume, or the price times quantity products for notional volume. Count each print once, not once per counterparty, and never add volumes from different venues without stating that the result double counts.

Worked example

Given
  • trades: (2, 100), (5, 101), (3, 99) as quantity and price
Steps
  1. V = 2 + 5 + 3
  2. V_N = 200 + 505 + 297
V = 10 units, V_N = 1002 in quote currency

Interpretation

Volume is a count of activity, not a direction and not a value. A rise relative to that instrument's own recent average means more participants were involved; it says nothing about who was the aggressor.

Assumptions

  • One venue and one reporting convention. Some venues report in base units, some in quote currency, and some double count both sides of a trade.
  • Each print is a distinct trade. Self-matching, internalised flow and inflated reporting on unregulated venues all break that assumption.
  • Direction is not in the data. Any buy-versus-sell split comes from an added classification rule.
Limits of the formula
  • Volume levels are not comparable across instruments or venues; only changes relative to the same series' own history carry meaning.
  • In derivatives, volume and open interest answer different questions and can move in opposite directions on the same day.

Commonly confused with

Open Interest

Volume is a flow: how much traded in an interval, reset each bar. Open interest is a stock: how many contracts are outstanding at a moment. Heavy volume with flat open interest means positions changed hands rather than being created.
