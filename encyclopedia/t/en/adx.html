Average Directional Index

Simple definition

A 0 to 100 reading of how strongly a market is trending, in either direction. It says nothing about which direction that is.

Professional definition

Wilder's directional movement system. Two lines, +DI and -DI, measure how much of recent true range came from upward and from downward extension; ADX is the smoothed size of the gap between them. A high ADX means one side has accounted for most of the range expansion, up or down.

Technical definition

ADX is built on directional movement: in each bar only the larger of the upward and downward extension counts, and an inside bar contributes nothing to either. Each series is Wilder-smoothed and divided by smoothed true range to give +DI and -DI; the normalised difference DX is then smoothed again. The double smoothing makes ADX lag by construction, so a rising ADX confirms a trend already under way. It is direction-blind: a crash and a melt-up give the same high reading.

Academic definition

A twice-smoothed, normalised measure of the imbalance between upward and downward directional movement relative to true range, bounded on the interval [0, 100] and invariant to the sign of the trend.

Mechanism

Directional movement counts only the part of a bar that extends beyond the previous one, so a market that keeps making new extremes in one direction accumulates movement on one side only. Dividing by true range strips out the volatility scale, which lets one threshold serve many instruments. Smoothing the difference twice buys stability at the price of lag.

In practice

Who uses it
Trend-following system developers, and traders who want to filter mean-reversion rules out of trending conditions.
Why it matters
It gives a scale-free, direction-blind measure of trend strength, so one threshold can gate a rule set across many instruments.
Limitations
It lags because of the double smoothing, the usual thresholds are conventions with no statistical derivation, and a high reading describes range expansion already achieved.

Common mistakes

  • Reading ADX direction as price direction.
  • Using one fixed ADX threshold without testing it per instrument.
  • Comparing SMA-smoothed ADX values with Wilder-smoothed ones.

Examples

market

A market grinds steadily lower for six weeks. ADX rises to a high reading while -DI stays above +DI. The high ADX says the move has been one-sided; it is the DI pair, not ADX, that says the side was down.

numeric

With smoothed up-movement 14, smoothed down-movement 6 and smoothed true range 40, the two lines are 35 and 15. Their normalised gap gives DX of 40, and ADX is that series smoothed again.

Given
  • smoothed +DM over 14 periods = 14
  • smoothed -DM over 14 periods = 6
  • smoothed true range over 14 periods = 40
Steps
  1. +DI = 100 x 14 / 40 = 35
  2. -DI = 100 x 6 / 40 = 15
  3. DX = 100 x |35 - 15| / (35 + 15) = 100 x 20 / 50 = 40
+DI = 35, -DI = 15, DX = 40. ADX is the Wilder-smoothed DX series, so it reaches 40 only after the smoothing window.

DX for a single bar is not ADX. Quoting one bar's DX as an ADX value is a common implementation error.

Mathematical foundation

\begin{aligned} up_t &= H_t - H_{t-1}, \qquad dn_t = L_{t-1} - L_t \\ +DM_t &= \begin{cases} up_t & \text{if } up_t > dn_t \text{ and } up_t > 0 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \\ -DM_t &= \begin{cases} dn_t & \text{if } dn_t > up_t \text{ and } dn_t > 0 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases} \\ TR_t &= \max\left(H_t - L_t,\; |H_t - C_{t-1}|,\; |L_t - C_{t-1}|\right) \\ +DI_n &= 100 \times \frac{\widetilde{DM^{+}}_n}{\widetilde{TR}_n}, \qquad -DI_n = 100 \times \frac{\widetilde{DM^{-}}_n}{\widetilde{TR}_n} \\ DX_n &= 100 \times \frac{\left| +DI_n - (-DI_n) \right|}{+DI_n + (-DI_n)}, \qquad ADX_n = \widetilde{DX}_n \end{aligned}

Variables

+DM_t, -DM_tDirectional movement: only the larger extension is kept, the other is set to zeroprice units
TR_tTrue range, which includes any gap from the previous closeprice units
+DI_n, -DI_nDirectional indicators: smoothed directional movement as a percentage of smoothed true rangepercent
DX_nNormalised gap between the two directional indicators0 to 100

Procedure

For each bar compute the upward and downward extensions, keep only the larger as directional movement and set the other to zero, and compute true range. Wilder-smooth all three series over n periods: first value a simple sum or mean, then each later value as previous minus previous divided by n plus current. Divide the smoothed movements by smoothed true range for +DI and -DI, form DX from their normalised difference, then Wilder-smooth DX to get ADX.

Worked example

Given
  • current bar: H = 112, L = 104; previous bar: H = 110, L = 100, C = 105
  • smoothed +DM = 14, smoothed -DM = 6, smoothed TR = 40 over n = 14
Steps
  1. up = 112 - 110 = 2; dn = 100 - 104 = -4
  2. up > dn and up > 0, so +DM = 2 and -DM = 0
  3. TR = max(112 - 104, |112 - 105|, |104 - 105|) = max(8, 7, 1) = 8
  4. +DI = 100 x 14 / 40 = 35; -DI = 100 x 6 / 40 = 15
  5. DX = 100 x |35 - 15| / 50 = 40
This bar contributes +DM 2, -DM 0 and TR 8; on the smoothed sums shown, +DI is 35, -DI is 15 and DX is 40.

Interpretation

A high ADX says one side has accounted for most of the recent range expansion. The number carries no direction: +DI relative to -DI tells you which side, while ADX tells you only how one-sided it has been, and only over the window used.

Assumptions

  • Wilder's smoothing with n = 14 is the original specification. Platforms that offer SMA or EMA smoothing produce different numbers from the same price data.
  • Only the larger of the two extensions is kept in a bar, so an inside bar contributes zero to both +DM and -DM.
  • If the smoothed +DI and -DI sum to zero, DX is undefined; implementations conventionally return zero for that bar.
  • ADX is the smoothed DX series, so the first valid ADX value appears roughly two smoothing windows after the start of the data.
Limits of the formula
  • The double smoothing means ADX reports trend strength with a lag of about the smoothing length, so it cannot be used as an early signal.
  • The thresholds of 20 or 25 commonly quoted are convention; no source in this registry derives them statistically.
  • Because the measure is normalised by true range, a slow steady grind and a violent one-way move can score alike. ADX measures one-sidedness, not profitability.
