Fibonacci Retracement

Simple definition

Levels drawn at fixed fractions of a previous move — 23.6, 38.2, 50, 61.8 and 78.6 percent of it — where many traders watch for a pullback to stop.

Professional definition

A grid of retracement levels placed between two chosen swing points. 61.8 percent is the limiting ratio of the Fibonacci sequence, with 38.2 and 23.6 its square and cube; 50 percent is not a Fibonacci ratio at all but the midpoint, kept by convention, and 78.6 percent is the square root of 0.618.

Technical definition

Once two anchors are fixed the arithmetic is exact, and the anchors are a free choice: a different swing, a wick instead of a body, or a logarithmic axis moves every level. Evidence that price reacts at these particular fractions more than at arbitrary ones is weak. A competing explanation is available and is not ruled out: the grid is drawn identically by default on every major platform, so clustering may be self-fulfilling attention rather than a property of prices.

Academic definition

A set of conventional retracement proportions applied between two selected extrema, only some of which derive from the limiting ratio of the Fibonacci sequence.

Mechanism

There is nothing in the sequence that transmits to order flow. What can transmit is attention: the grid is a default tool on every major platform, so many participants see the same prices and leave orders at them. That makes clustering possible as a coordination effect, which would also explain why the levels appear to work best on the most widely watched instruments.

In practice

Who uses it
Swing traders locating where a pullback might end, analysts annotating charts for others, and anyone wanting a reference grid a colleague will reproduce identically.
Why it matters
It supplies a standard, reproducible set of prices to plan entry and invalidation around, and because it is standard, other participants are watching the same prices.
Limitations
The anchors are discretionary, so the grid is not unique. Support for these specific ratios over arbitrary fractions is weak, and a widely watched level is also a convenient place to trap late entries.

Common mistakes

  • Re-anchoring the swing after the fact until a level lines up with the low.
  • Calling 50 percent a Fibonacci ratio.
  • Treating a level as support with no confirmation from price.

Examples

chart

Two analysts annotate the same rise. One anchors the grid to the session low, the other to the body low two bars earlier. Their 61.8 levels differ by several points, and each will later call their own the one price respected.

numeric

For a swing from 100 up to 140, the grid gives 130.6, 124.7, 120, 115.3 and 108.6. The 120 level is simply the midpoint of the move, which is why calling it a Fibonacci level is wrong.

Given
  • swing start S = 100
  • swing extreme E = 140
  • height E - S = 40
  • k values: 0.236, 0.382, 0.5, 0.618, 0.786
Steps
  1. R(0.236) = 140 - 0.236 x 40 = 130.56
  2. R(0.382) = 140 - 0.382 x 40 = 124.72
  3. R(0.5) = 140 - 20 = 120
  4. R(0.618) = 140 - 24.72 = 115.28
  5. R(0.786) = 140 - 31.44 = 108.56
levels at 130.56, 124.72, 120.00, 115.28 and 108.56

0.5 is the midpoint and 0.786 is the square root of 0.618. Neither is a term of the Fibonacci sequence or a ratio of its terms.

Mathematical foundation

Procedure

Choose the swing start and the swing extreme and state whether wicks or bodies were used. Multiply the height by each fraction and subtract from the extreme for a retracement of an advance, or add to it for a decline. On a logarithmic axis apply the fractions to log prices instead, which gives different levels.

Worked example

Given
  • S = 100
  • E = 140
  • E - S = 40
Steps
  1. 0.382 x 40 = 15.28
  2. R(0.382) = 140 - 15.28
  3. 0.618 x 40 = 24.72
  4. R(0.618) = 140 - 24.72
R(0.382) = 124.72 and R(0.618) = 115.28

Interpretation

Each output is a price, derived entirely from the two anchors. The grid describes proportions of a move the analyst selected; it contains no information that was not already in those two points.

Assumptions

  • 0.618 is the limit of the ratio of consecutive Fibonacci numbers; 0.382 is its square and 0.236 its cube. 0.5 is the midpoint and 0.786 is the square root of 0.618, so neither comes from the sequence.
  • The two anchors are a discretionary choice, as is wick versus body. The grid is not unique for a given chart.
  • A linear price axis is assumed; on a log axis the same anchors produce different levels.
Limits of the formula
  • No source in this registry establishes that price reverses at these fractions more often than at arbitrary fractions of the same swing.
  • Because the levels are a platform default, any observed clustering may reflect coordinated attention rather than a property of price; the two explanations are not separated by the evidence available here.
  • Anchoring chosen after the fact is lookahead bias, and makes any backtest of the levels meaningless.

Commonly confused with

Body V (Bodyway)

Fibonacci levels are proportions of a swing and give a zone to watch. Body V is an absolute distance measured from candle bodies and gives a single projected price; the two are not variants of one method.
