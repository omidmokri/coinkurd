Trading Fee

Category
Trading Fundamentals
Level
Foundation

Simple definition

What the venue charges you for each trade, usually a percentage of the value traded. You pay it on the way in and again on the way out.

Professional definition

The venue's charge per execution, typically a rate on notional, differentiated by maker or taker role and by volume tier. It is separate from the spread and from slippage, and because it is paid on both legs it sets the minimum move a round trip must make to break even.

Technical definition

A contractual cost applied per fill, in the quote currency or in the asset received, at a rate set by role and tier and sometimes reduced by holding the venue's token. On derivatives it is charged on notional, so leverage multiplies it relative to the margin posted. Funding payments, withdrawal charges and on-chain gas are separate items frequently confused with it. Schedules are venue terms and change, so a backtest fixed to one rate drifts from reality.

Academic definition

An explicit transaction cost levied per execution which, together with the implicit costs of spread and market impact, constitutes the total cost of trading.

Mechanism

A rate on notional is paid twice per round trip, so the break-even move scales with the sum of the two rates rather than with either alone. Under leverage the same rate sits on a much larger notional and consumes margin quickly, which is why high-turnover churn at retail taker rates rarely survives the arithmetic.

In practice

Who uses it
Every trader, and critically high-turnover strategies: scalpers, grid bots, arbitrage desks, and anyone running a backtest.
Why it matters
It is the one cost known exactly in advance, and the one most often omitted from a backtest, which is how a losing strategy can look profitable on paper.
Limitations
Schedules change, tiers depend on rolling volume, and a derivatives fee on notional is not comparable with a spot fee on the amount actually controlled.

Common mistakes

  • Backtesting with no fees, or with a tier you do not qualify for.
  • Comparing a derivatives fee on notional with a spot fee.
  • Forgetting that the fee is charged on both legs.

Examples

crypto

A grid bot placing forty round trips a day at a taker rate needs each grid step to clear roughly twice that rate before any profit exists. Halving the rate by qualifying as a maker nearly halves the required step.

numeric

At 0.05% taker on both legs, a long round trip needs a gross move of about 10.0 basis points to break even. At 0.02% maker on both legs it needs about 4.0 — before any spread is crossed.

Given
  • long round trip, fee on notional both legs
  • case A: entry and exit fee 0.05%
  • case B: entry and exit fee 0.02%
  • spread and slippage excluded
Steps
  1. break-even ratio = (1 + f_in) / (1 - f_out)
  2. case A: 1.0005 / 0.9995 = 1.0010005, so 10.0 basis points
  3. case B: 1.0002 / 0.9998 = 1.0004001, so 4.0 basis points
  4. difference = 6.0 basis points of required move
the fee schedule alone moves the break-even from about 10.0 to about 4.0 basis points

Rates are illustrative. Add the spread crossed on each leg and any slippage to get the real hurdle, which is usually several times the fee.

Mathematical foundation

\frac{P_{exit}}{P_{entry}} = \frac{1 + f_{in}}{1 - f_{out}}, \qquad r_{BE} = \frac{1 + f_{in}}{1 - f_{out}} - 1

Variables

P_{entry}Execution price on the opening legquote currency
P_{exit}Execution price on the closing leg needed to break evenquote currency
f_{in}Fee rate on the opening leg, as a decimal of notionalfraction
f_{out}Fee rate on the closing leg, as a decimal of notionalfraction
r_{BE}Break-even gross return on the round tripfraction

Procedure

Write the cash paid on entry as quantity times price times one plus the entry fee, and the cash received on exit as quantity times price times one minus the exit fee. Set them equal and solve for the price ratio. Subtract one to read the result as a required return.

Worked example

Given
  • f_in = f_out = 0.0005 (0.05% taker both legs)
  • then f_in = f_out = 0.0002 (0.02% maker both legs)
Steps
  1. case A: (1 + 0.0005) / (1 - 0.0005) = 1.0005 / 0.9995 = 1.0010005
  2. r_BE = 0.0010005, i.e. 10.0 basis points
  3. case B: 1.0002 / 0.9998 = 1.0004001
  4. r_BE = 0.0004001, i.e. 4.0 basis points
10.0 basis points at the taker schedule against 4.0 at the maker schedule

Interpretation

The output is the gross move the position must make before the trader keeps anything. It is a hurdle, not a forecast, and the spread and any slippage must be added to it.

Assumptions

  • Rates apply to notional. A flat per-contract or per-trade charge changes the arithmetic and does not scale with price.
  • The expression is written for a long round trip. For a short, the entry is the sale and the exit the purchase, so the roles of the two rates swap.
  • Spread, market impact, funding and withdrawal costs are excluded; each raises the real hurdle.
  • A maker rebate enters as a negative fee rate, which lowers the break-even and can in principle make it negative.
Limits of the formula
  • Fee schedules, volume tiers and token discounts are venue terms and are revised without notice, so a figure computed today is not durable.
  • On leveraged products the rate applies to notional, so the cost measured against posted margin is multiplied by the leverage used.

Commonly confused with

Bid-Ask Spread

The spread is an implicit cost paid to the other side of the market. The fee is an explicit charge paid to the venue. They are additive, and a cheap fee on a wide spread is not cheap execution.
