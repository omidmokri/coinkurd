Limit Order

Foundation

Simple definition

An instruction to trade only at your price or better. You control what you pay; you get no promise that the trade happens at all.

Professional definition

A priced instruction that rests in the book until it is matched, cancelled or expires. It guarantees the price boundary and not execution: if the market never reaches your limit, or reaches it with a long queue ahead of you, you simply do not trade.

Technical definition

A limit order joins the queue at its price level and ranks by price first, then by arrival time. It supplies liquidity while resting and takes it when priced through the opposite quote, which is why a buy limit above the best ask executes immediately as a taker. Queue position decides whether a touch of the level actually fills you: being last at the best bid in a one-tick move usually means no fill at all.

Academic definition

A conditionally executable instruction specifying a worst acceptable price, which supplies liquidity to the book and bears both non-execution risk and the risk of adverse selection by better-informed counterparties.

Mechanism

A resting limit order is a free option handed to the rest of the market: others trade against it whenever that is advantageous to them. The compensation is the spread and any maker rebate; the hazard is that the fills arrive mostly when price is about to continue straight past you.

In practice

Who uses it
Market makers, accumulation and distribution desks, fee-sensitive high-frequency strategies, and any trader with a defined entry price.
Why it matters
It fixes the worst acceptable price and converts execution cost into queue risk and waiting risk, which is usually cheaper in a calm book.
Limitations
Non-execution is a real cost: an unfilled entry is a missed trade, and an unfilled exit means the position stays on through the move.

Common mistakes

  • Treating a resting limit as a guaranteed entry.
  • Ignoring queue position and assuming a touch means a fill.
  • Leaving stale limit orders in the book across a news event.

Examples

market

A buyer rests a bid one tick under the market. In the morning price trades through the level and fills it; in the afternoon the same level is touched and never fills, because dozens of earlier orders sat ahead in the queue.

numeric

Taking the 100.00 offer at a 0.05% taker fee costs 100.05 per unit. Resting a bid at 99.90 at a 0.02% maker fee costs 99.92 — thirteen basis points better, if it fills.

Given
  • best ask 100.00, best bid 99.90
  • taker fee 0.05%, maker fee 0.02%
  • one unit bought
Steps
  1. taker cost = 100.00 x (1 + 0.0005) = 100.05
  2. maker cost = 99.90 x (1 + 0.0002) = 99.9200
  3. difference = 100.05 - 99.92 = 0.13, i.e. 0.13%
the passive route is about 13 basis points cheaper per unit when it fills

The saving is conditional. Weight it by the probability of being filled, and remember that fills cluster on the moves that go against the maker.

Commonly confused with

Stop-Limit Order

A limit order is live in the book from the moment it is sent. A stop-limit order is dormant until a trigger price is touched, and only then becomes a limit order.
