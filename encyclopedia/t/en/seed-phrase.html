Simple definition

A list of ordinary words, usually twelve or twenty-four, that is a readable form of the master secret of a wallet. Whoever has the words has the coins.

Professional definition

A mnemonic encoding of wallet entropy. The words map back to a number, the number seeds a key tree, and every key the wallet will ever use is derived from it. It is a full backup and a full bearer secret at the same time.

Technical definition

Under BIP-39, 128 to 256 bits of entropy plus a short checksum are split into eleven-bit indices into a fixed 2048-word list, giving 12 to 24 words. The phrase, optionally with a passphrase, is stretched into a seed, which BIP-32 expands into a hierarchical key tree. Word order is part of the secret, and the checksum detects a mistyped word but cannot repair a forgotten one.

Academic definition

A human-transcribable mnemonic representation of wallet entropy, standardised with a checksum, from which a deterministic hierarchy of key pairs is derived.

Mechanism

The phrase is not a password that unlocks an account somewhere. It is the number the keys are computed from, so anyone who learns it can recompute every key on their own hardware, immediately and without permission. That is why a phrase typed into a website, pasted into a chat or photographed must be treated as already spent: there is nothing to revoke.

In practice

Who uses it
Every self-custodian, and every team writing a custody or inheritance procedure.
Why it matters
It is the one artefact that restores a wallet after a lost, broken or wiped device, and the one artefact whose disclosure ends ownership.
Limitations
A phrase alone cannot prove which wallet software or derivation path produced it, so a backup should record the wallet type and whether a passphrase was used.

Common mistakes

  • Entering the phrase into any website, form, chat or support request.
  • Storing it as a photo, screenshot or cloud note.
  • Keeping the only copy in one place that one fire can reach.

Examples

numeric

A twelve-word phrase carries 128 bits of entropy plus a four-bit checksum: 2048 to the power of twelve equals 2 to the power of 132 combinations. Guessing is not the threat. Copying the words is.

Given
  • BIP-39 word list = 2048 words
  • Each word carries 11 bits
  • 12 words = 132 bits total
  • Of those, 128 bits are entropy and 4 bits are the checksum
Steps
  1. 2048 = 2^11
  2. 12 x 11 = 132 bits encoded
  3. 132 - 4 = 128 bits of entropy
  4. Search space = 2^128
2^128 possible 12-word secrets, about 3.4 x 10^38

The number only bounds brute force. It says nothing about phishing, screenshots or a compromised device, which is how phrases are actually lost.

security

A support account offers to restore a stuck wallet if the user sends the twelve words. No legitimate wallet support ever needs them. Any request for the phrase is the attack, whatever the explanation offered.
