Simple definition

The number of derivative contracts currently open and not yet closed or settled. It counts live positions, not how many trades have happened.

Professional definition

The total of outstanding contracts counted on one side of the market, so each contract pairs one long with one short and is counted once. It rises when a new buyer and a new seller create a contract, and falls when an existing long and short close against each other.

Technical definition

A stock, not a flow. A trade changes open interest only according to whether each side is opening or closing: new long against new short increases it by one, closing long against closing short decreases it by one, and a transfer between a new and an existing holder leaves it unchanged. Read alongside volume and price, it separates fresh commitment from position rotation.

Academic definition

The aggregate number of outstanding derivative contracts not yet offset by an opposing transaction nor extinguished by delivery or cash settlement.

Mechanism

Open interest changes only when contracts are created or destroyed, because every contract needs a willing long and a willing short. That is why rising price with rising open interest is read as new capital taking the move, while rising price with falling open interest points to shorts closing rather than buyers committing.

In practice

Who uses it
Futures and perpetual traders, exchange risk teams, and analysts gauging how much leveraged commitment sits behind a price move.
Why it matters
It separates a move driven by new positions from one driven by existing positions closing, which volume alone cannot do.
Limitations
Crypto open interest is fragmented across venues and quoted sometimes in contracts, sometimes in coins, sometimes in notional currency, so totals are not comparable across sources.

Common mistakes

  • Adding longs and shorts together and reporting double the real figure.
  • Treating a jump in open interest as directional on its own.
  • Comparing notional open interest across venues with different contract sizes.

Examples

market

Price breaks higher and open interest rises with it: new longs and new shorts are both being created, so the move carries fresh commitment. The same break with open interest falling is shorts covering, and it often stalls once they are done.

numeric

Two new traders open against each other: open interest becomes 1. A third buys from the original long, so the contract transfers and open interest stays 1. Finally long and short close against each other and it returns to 0. Volume over the three trades was 3.

Given
  • Start: open interest = 0
  • Trade 1: new buyer against new seller, 1 contract
  • Trade 2: new buyer against the existing long, 1 contract
  • Trade 3: the existing long against the existing short, 1 contract
Steps
  1. After trade 1: a contract is created, OI = 1
  2. After trade 2: the position is transferred, OI = 1
  3. After trade 3: the contract is extinguished, OI = 0
Open interest 0, cumulative volume 3

Volume counts transactions; open interest counts surviving contracts. They answer different questions, and either can move without the other.

Commonly confused with

Trading Volume

Volume counts how many contracts changed hands in a period and resets each period. Open interest counts how many contracts are still alive and carries over.
