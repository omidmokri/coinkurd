Strike Price

ENGLISHStrike Pricereference
فارسیقیمت اعمالEstablished equivalent

Category
Derivatives
Level
Foundation

Simple definition

The fixed price written into an option at which the holder may buy or sell the underlying. It does not change for the life of the contract.

Professional definition

The price at which an option's right is exercised, set when the contract is listed and fixed thereafter. It is the reference against which the underlying is measured, so it determines intrinsic value, moneyness and the expiry breakeven of the position.

Technical definition

A contract parameter, not a market variable. Venues list strikes on a fixed grid whose spacing narrows near the prevailing price, so the available strikes are themselves a venue decision. Because the strike is constant while the underlying moves, every moneyness label, every intrinsic-value calculation and the sign and size of the Greeks are all stated relative to it.

Academic definition

The predetermined exchange price specified in a contingent claim, at which the holder's right to transact in the underlying may be exercised.

Mechanism

The strike is the only price in the structure that is contractually frozen, and that is what creates optionality: value appears from the gap between a fixed number and a moving one. Moving the strike away from the current price buys cheaper exposure to a larger move, and surrenders the intrinsic value that a nearer strike would already hold.

In practice

Who uses it
Anyone selecting a contract from a chain, hedgers choosing where to place a floor or a cap, and desks constructing spreads from two or more strikes.
Why it matters
Strike selection, not direction alone, decides how much has to happen before the position pays, and how much capital is at risk to find out.
Limitations
Only the strikes the venue lists are available, and in thin option markets the quoted spread on an unusual strike can exceed any edge in choosing it.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing the cheapest strike without checking how far price must travel.
  • Comparing premiums across different strikes as if they were the same product.
  • Ignoring that strike spacing widens far from the current price.

Examples

market

Two calls on the same underlying, same expiry, strikes 100 and 120. The 120 costs far less, but needs a much larger move before it has any intrinsic value at all. Cheaper is not more conservative.

numeric

A call at strike 100 bought for 6 breaks even at 106. A call at strike 120 bought for 1.5 breaks even at 121.5 — a much smaller outlay, but the underlying must travel far further before the position is level.

Given
  • underlying at 100
  • call A: K = 100, premium 6
  • call B: K = 120, premium 1.5
  • same expiry, fees ignored
Steps
  1. breakeven A = 100 + 6 = 106, a required move of 6%
  2. breakeven B = 120 + 1.5 = 121.5, a required move of 21.5%
A needs a 6% move, B needs 21.5%, for outlays of 6 and 1.5

The premiums are illustrative numbers chosen to show the arithmetic, not quotes from any market.

Mathematical foundation

\text{Breakeven}_{\text{long call}} = K + c, \qquad \text{Breakeven}_{\text{long put}} = K - p

Variables

KStrike price fixed in the contractprice units
cPremium paid for the callprice units
pPremium paid for the putprice units

Procedure

Add the premium to the strike for a long call, or subtract it for a long put. The result is the underlying price at which the position returns exactly what it cost, at expiry and ignoring fees.

Worked example

Given
  • K = 100
  • c = 6
Steps
  1. breakeven = 100 + 6
106 at expiry, before fees

Interpretation

Breakeven turns a strike into a decision: it is the move the underlying must make for the trade to have been worth entering, which is a different question from whether the direction was right.

Assumptions

  • Breakeven as written is an expiry quantity and ignores fees, the contract multiplier and the financing cost of the premium.
  • Before expiry a position can be profitable below its expiry breakeven, because the remaining time value is still in the premium.
  • For written options the same arithmetic gives the point beyond which the premium received is exhausted.
Limits of the formula
  • Breakeven says nothing about the probability of reaching it; that requires a distributional assumption and a model.
  • Strike grids and spacing are set by each venue, so the comparison between two strikes is only as meaningful as the liquidity at both.

Commonly confused with

Mark Price

The strike is a fixed contract term. The mark price is the venue's running valuation of the underlying or position, and it changes continuously.
