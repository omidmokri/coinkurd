Simple definition

The different instructions you can attach to a buy or sell. Some say take whatever price is available now; others say only trade at my price, or only if this level is reached.

Professional definition

The execution instructions a venue accepts. Each one trades certainty of execution against certainty of price: a market order guarantees execution and not price, a limit order guarantees price and not execution, and the conditional and flag types restrict when or how an order may rest or fill.

Technical definition

Three separable choices: a trigger condition, an execution style, and flags. Market and limit set the style; stop and stop-limit add a price trigger ahead of it; post-only and reduce-only are flags that constrain behaviour, the first rejecting an order that would take liquidity, the second preventing an order from increasing net position. Availability and exact semantics are venue-specific.

Academic definition

The set of execution instructions defining how an order interacts with the book, each imposing a distinct trade-off between execution certainty, price certainty and the liquidity role the order takes.

Mechanism

Every order either takes liquidity from the book or adds it, and that choice sets the guarantee. Taking crosses the spread and fills now at whatever is resting; adding waits in the queue and fills only if someone comes to it. Conditional types do not escape this: a triggered stop becomes a taker, and a stop-limit becomes a maker that may never trade.

In practice

Who uses it
Every trader placing an order, execution desks measuring cost against a benchmark, and anyone automating entries and exits through an API.
Why it matters
Choosing the wrong type is one of the few errors that costs money even when the market view is right, because it decides the price actually paid.
Limitations
Names and behaviour differ by venue, and some flags exist only on some platforms. Semantics for triggers, partial fills and rejections must be read from the venue's own documentation.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming a stop order guarantees a price as well as an exit.
  • Using a market order in a thin book and reading the result as slippage bad luck.
  • Relying on a post-only or reduce-only flag the venue does not implement as expected.

Examples

market

A trader wants out of a position before a data release. A market order gets them out at whatever the book offers. A limit order at the current bid may sit unfilled through the release, which is the one outcome they were trying to avoid.

numeric

The book offers 10 units at 100.05, 30 at 100.10 and 100 at 100.20. A market buy for 50 units fills across three levels at an average of 100.11. A limit buy at 100.10 fills only 40 units and leaves 10 resting.

Given
  • asks: 10 units at 100.05, 30 units at 100.10, 100 units at 100.20
  • order: buy 50 units
  • fees excluded; no other participants act in between
Steps
  1. market order fills 10 at 100.05 = 1,000.50
  2. then 30 at 100.10 = 3,003.00
  3. then 10 at 100.20 = 1,002.00
  4. total = 5,005.50 for 50 units
  5. average fill = 5,005.50 / 50 = 100.11
  6. limit buy at 100.10 fills 10 + 30 = 40 units and the last 10 rest at 100.10
market order: 50 units at an average of 100.11. Limit order at 100.10: 40 units filled, 10 unfilled

The market order paid 0.06 above the best ask on average. The limit order paid no more than 100.10 but ended the moment with an unfilled remainder, which is the same trade-off in both directions.

Commonly confused with

Stop-Loss

A stop order is a mechanism; a stop-loss is a purpose. The same stop order can cut a loss, protect a profit or enter a breakout, and the venue treats all three identically.

Editorial status

Draftindustry-conventionv1.02026-11-03

Written, not yet reviewed.

Order-type names and semantics are venue conventions, not standards. Market, limit, stop and stop-limit behave broadly alike across regulated futures venues and crypto exchanges, but post-only and reduce-only are exchange features whose exact handling of rejections, partial fills and position netting differs, and some venues do not offer them at all. There is no math block because the concept is a taxonomy of instructions rather than a quantity. The Kurdish wording follows the project lexicon's recommended term for the order book and needs native-expert confirmation.
