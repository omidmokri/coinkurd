Hindsight Bias

Simple definition

Once you know how it turned out, it feels like it was always obvious. Last month's chart looks readable in a way it never did at the time.

Professional definition

The retrospective sense that an outcome was predictable, and the inability to recover the uncertainty one actually felt beforehand. Its cost in trading is measurement: a review written after the result cannot distinguish a good decision from a lucky one.

Technical definition

The bias operates on memory of a prior probability estimate, not on the estimate itself. Knowing the outcome reorganises which evidence feels relevant, so the earlier state of knowledge becomes inaccessible. This makes any post-hoc evaluation of a decision unreliable unless the forecast was recorded in advance, which is the entire reason a trade journal records the reasoning and not just the fill.

Academic definition

A systematic distortion in which knowledge of an outcome inflates the recalled or imputed ex-ante probability of that outcome, rendering unrecorded retrospective self-assessment unreliable.

Mechanism

Learning the outcome updates the whole picture, including the parts that record what was known before. Evidence consistent with the result becomes salient and the alternatives that were live at the time quietly drop out of the story. Nothing is suppressed deliberately; the earlier uncertainty simply has nowhere left to sit.

In practice

Who uses it
Anyone reviewing their own trades, grading a strategy after the fact, or reading a post-mortem of a market event.
Why it matters
It is the reason a decision log written in advance is worth more than any amount of careful reflection afterwards.
Limitations
Hindsight bias says retrospective confidence is inflated. It does not say the past is unlearnable; it says the record has to pre-date the result.

Common mistakes

  • Grading an entry by whether the trade worked.
  • Rewriting the plan after the outcome and calling it a lesson.
  • Reading an explained crash as a predicted crash.

Examples

market

After a crash, commentary converges on one cause and the chart acquires an obvious topping pattern. The same pattern appeared several times that year without a crash following; those instances are no longer part of the story.

everyday

A trader writes down before a decision: sixty percent chance the level holds. The level breaks. Without that note, the memory becomes I knew it would break; with it, the note was simply a sixty-forty call that landed on the forty.

Commonly confused with

Lookahead Bias

Lookahead bias is a defect in a backtest: future data leaks into a past decision in code. Hindsight bias is the same leak in memory, and it has no log file to inspect.
