Counterparty Risk

Simple definition

The risk that the other side of your trade, or the platform holding it, does not pay what it owes. Your position can be right and the money still not arrive.

Professional definition

The exposure to non-performance by any party to a contract or an intermediary in the chain: a trading venue, a broker, a clearing house, a lending protocol or a stablecoin issuer. It is separate from market risk, and it is not reduced by being right about price.

Technical definition

Exposure has two components: current exposure, the replacement value of what the counterparty owes now, and potential future exposure, how large that could become before settlement. Mitigants are structural rather than analytical: margin posted to a central counterparty, segregation of client assets, netting agreements, default funds and position limits. Where none of these applies, the exposure is an unsecured claim whose recovery depends on the insolvency law of the relevant jurisdiction.

Academic definition

The risk of economic loss arising from the failure of a contractual counterparty or intermediary to perform, comprising current replacement cost and potential future exposure, mitigated by collateralisation, netting and asset segregation.

Mechanism

A contract is only as good as the ability and obligation of the other side to perform. Central clearing changes the question by replacing many bilateral exposures with one exposure to a clearing house that collects margin daily and holds a default fund. Without such a structure, an account balance is a claim on the operator, and claims rank behind whatever the law gives priority to.

In practice

Who uses it
Anyone holding a balance or an open contract at a venue, treasury and operations teams selecting intermediaries, and risk teams setting per-venue exposure limits.
Why it matters
It is the risk that is invisible on a chart and unaffected by a stop: no exit level protects against the venue itself failing to settle.
Limitations
Disclosure is uneven and the protections differ by jurisdiction and by product, so the exposure usually cannot be quantified from outside the institution.

Common mistakes

  • Treating an exchange balance as equivalent to holding the asset.
  • Assuming client funds are segregated without checking the terms.
  • Concentrating every venue exposure in one place because fees are lower.

Examples

market

On a centrally cleared futures exchange, the trader's exposure is to the clearing house rather than to whoever took the other side. Daily variation margin and a default fund stand between a member's failure and the rest of the participants.

crypto

A perpetual contract with a solvent-looking venue still carries three distinct counterparty exposures: the margin balance held by the venue, the settlement of unrealised profit, and the insurance fund that absorbs losses from under-margined liquidations before auto-deleveraging reaches other traders.

Commonly confused with

Credit risk

Credit risk is the risk a borrower does not repay. Counterparty risk is wider: it covers any party to a contract or any intermediary in the chain failing to perform, including a venue holding a balance where no loan was ever made.
