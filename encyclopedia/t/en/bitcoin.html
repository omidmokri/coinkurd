Simple definition

A digital money with no issuer, no bank and no administrator. Anyone can hold it with a key and send it over an open network, and new coins appear on a fixed published schedule.

Professional definition

The first proof-of-work blockchain and its native asset, BTC. Miners compete on hash power to group transactions into blocks and the heaviest valid chain wins. New coins arrive only as a block subsidy that halves every 210,000 blocks, so issuance is scheduled rather than discretionary.

Technical definition

A UTXO ledger whose state transitions are checked independently by every full node, with proof-of-work ordering transactions and difficulty retargeting holding the average block interval near ten minutes. Settlement is probabilistic: a tip deepens towards finality with each confirmation rather than becoming final at once. Scarcity is a consensus rule enforced by node operators, so it is a social commitment with a mathematical form, not a physical law.

Academic definition

A permissionless distributed ledger secured by proof-of-work, in which a native bearer asset is issued on a predetermined, geometrically declining schedule and transferred by digital signature without a trusted intermediary.

Mechanism

Miners spend real resources for a chance to extend the chain and are paid in the asset whose ledger they secure. That ties the cost of rewriting history to the value of the reward, which is why security and price are coupled. Nodes, not miners, enforce the rules: a block that breaks them is simply rejected, however much work it carries.

In practice

Who uses it
Long-term holders, miners and mining pools, exchanges and custodians, treasury teams, and analysts who use it as the crypto market's reference asset.
Why it matters
It is the deepest and most widely quoted cryptoasset, so it sets the benchmark against which other tokens are priced and dominance is measured.
Limitations
Base-layer settlement is slow and fees rise with demand; it carries no general smart-contract layer of its own, and self-custody means full exposure to key loss.

Common mistakes

  • Treating a seen transaction as settled before confirmations accumulate.
  • Reading the 21 million cap as a price floor.
  • Confusing the network with a company or exchange built on top of it.

Examples

market

A payment visible in the mempool is not yet settled. Merchants accepting large amounts usually wait for several blocks, because a shallow chain tip can still be reorganised away.

numeric

At a 3.125 BTC subsidy and a ten-minute target, about 144 blocks a day create roughly 450 new BTC. The next halving cuts that to about 225 a day before demand changes at all.

Given
  • block subsidy = 3.125 BTC (era that began at block 840,000, April 2024)
  • target block interval = 10 minutes
  • blocks per day = 24 x 60 / 10 = 144
Steps
  1. 144 x 3.125 = 450 BTC per day
  2. after the next halving the subsidy is 1.5625 BTC
  3. 144 x 1.5625 = 225 BTC per day
about 450 new BTC per day in the current era, about 225 in the next

144 blocks a day is an average produced by difficulty retargeting; realised intervals vary, so daily issuance fluctuates around this figure.

Commonly confused with

Token versus Coin

Bitcoin is a coin: the native asset of its own chain, created by that chain's consensus rules. A token lives inside a smart contract on someone else's chain and is issued by that contract.
