Inducement

Simple definition

A small, obvious swing that attracts early entries and stops, placed between price and the level a trader actually wants to reach. Taking it out is treated as a precondition for the real move.

Professional definition

In retail smart-money teaching, the minor swing point whose stops must be collected before a larger zone becomes valid. A demand zone is said to be unmitigated until the inducement below it has been taken, so the term functions as a sequencing condition on entries rather than as a signal in itself.

Technical definition

Codeable only after three arbitrary choices: which minor swing counts as the inducement, usually the first pullback inside the leg that created the zone; a minimum size for that pullback, since any noise qualifies otherwise; and whether a wick or a body close counts as taking it. Different choices make different zones valid, so the rule cannot be compared between traders unless all three are stated.

Academic definition

A practitioner sequencing condition requiring that a designated minor extreme be violated before a larger zone is treated as actionable; specified informally and lacking an established empirical validation.

Mechanism

Two things in it are ordinary: early entrants do cluster their stops at the nearest obvious swing, and a move through those stops adds one-directional orders. What the term adds is the claim that this clearing is required before the larger zone works, and that claim is not observable from price. Stated loosely it is also unfalsifiable: any failed zone can be explained by an inducement that was missed.

In practice

Who uses it
Advanced smart-money traders adding a sequencing filter to zone entries, and developers trying to make that filter explicit in code.
Why it matters
It discourages entering at the first touch of a zone, which is where a shallow stop is most likely to be taken out before the intended move.
Limitations
The most loosely defined term in this family. Its choices are discretionary, it is easy to apply after the fact, and no peer-reviewed evidence base exists.

Common mistakes

  • Identifying the inducement only after the trade failed.
  • Accepting any minor wiggle as one.
  • Entering before the level is taken while calling the setup valid.

Examples

market

Price leaves a demand zone at 100 and rises, pausing at a minor low of 104 on the way. Traders using the term will not buy the 104 area: they wait for price to trade below 104 and only then treat the 100 zone as the entry.

numeric

Entering at 104 with a stop at 103 risks one unit; waiting for 100 with a stop at 99 risks one unit but from four lower. If a 102 target is hit either way, the first trade loses a unit and the second never opened.

Given
  • inducement low = 104, zone = 100
  • stop one unit below each entry
  • price trades to 102 then reverses up
Steps
  1. entry at 104, stop 103: price at 102 passes through 103, so the trade is stopped for one unit
  2. entry at 100: price never reached 100, so no fill and no loss
  3. the sequencing filter avoided one loss and also missed the move
The filter removed a losing trade and a participation at the same time.

That trade-off is the whole of it: fewer entries, each with a better location, at the cost of the moves that never return to the zone. Only a fixed, pre-written rule can measure which side wins.

Commonly confused with

Liquidity Sweep

A liquidity sweep is the event of trading through a cluster of stops. Inducement names the cluster that is supposed to be swept first, and asserts an order of events. One is an observation, the other a precondition.

Flagged by the editor as uncertain on a linguistic, mathematical or regulatory point.

The microstructure point that predictable stop placement is costly is supported by the cited work. The term itself, and the claim that a designated minor swing must be taken before a larger zone becomes valid, belong to the retail smart-money tradition, which no registry source documents and for which no peer-reviewed evidence base exists. Of the terms in this family this is the least precisely defined and the easiest to apply retrospectively, which is stated plainly in the entry. Persian and Kurdish both keep the English term, which is what practitioners say.
