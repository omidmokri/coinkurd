Simple definition

In the Mokri method, a trendline is anchored at a pivot: a rising support line starts at a down pivot, and a falling resistance line starts at an up pivot. Breaking the line is what confirms it.

Professional definition

A trendline whose origin is a defined pivot rather than any convenient low or high. An ascending support line forms at a descending pivot; a descending resistance line forms at an ascending pivot. The break confirms the line and reverses the level's role.

Technical definition

A construction rule that makes trendline drawing less arbitrary by fixing the anchor to a pivot and making confirmation retrospective: the line is treated as valid once broken, at which point support is reclassified as resistance, or resistance as support, for subsequent interaction.

Academic definition

A trendline construction in which the anchor point is constrained to a swing pivot and validity is established by the break rather than by prior respect of the line.

Mechanism

Anchoring to a pivot removes most of the freedom that makes trendlines unfalsifiable, because the starting point is defined by structure rather than chosen for a good fit. Treating the break as the confirmation accepts that a line's informational value appears when it fails, not while it holds.

In practice

Who uses it
Price-action traders following the Mokri framework, and anyone trying to make trendline rules precise enough to code.
Why it matters
A pivot-anchored line can be generated programmatically, which is the difference between a rule that can be tested and a drawing that cannot.
Limitations
The author's own framework. The definition of a pivot (how many bars either side) is not fixed here, and different pivot definitions produce different lines from the same data.

Common mistakes

  • Anchoring to a convenient low instead of a structural pivot.
  • Keeping a line after price has made a new extreme instead of redrawing it.
  • Treating an unbroken line as confirmed.

Examples

scenario

Price makes a down pivot, then a series of higher lows. A rising support line drawn from that pivot is broken downward. From that break on, the same line is treated as resistance when price returns to it.

market

Two analysts draw different trendlines on the same chart and disagree about a break. Fixing the anchor to a pivot and requiring a body close beyond the line removes most of that disagreement.
