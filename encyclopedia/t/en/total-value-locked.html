Total Value Locked

Simple definition

The market value of all the assets sitting in a protocol's contracts, added up. It is a size figure, not earnings, and it moves when prices move even if nobody deposits anything.

Professional definition

The summed market value of deposited assets across a protocol or a whole ecosystem, quoted in a reference currency. Widely used to rank protocols, but it is a stock of balances valued at spot, so it rises and falls with token prices independently of usage, fees or revenue.

Technical definition

Three properties limit it as a metric. It double-counts: a token deposited in one protocol, wrapped and redeposited in another is counted in both, so ecosystem totals exceed the underlying assets. It is price-elastic: the same unchanged balances report a different figure after a market move. And it is not revenue: a protocol can hold a large balance and earn almost nothing, or earn well on a small one. Because the definition of what counts as locked varies between data providers, figures are not directly comparable across sources.

Academic definition

An aggregate stock measure of assets held in smart contracts, valued at prevailing market prices, subject to double counting under composability and to valuation effects independent of protocol activity.

Mechanism

The figure is a price times a quantity, and almost all of its movement usually comes from the price. Composability then adds the same underlying asset back in at each layer it passes through, so an ecosystem total measures how many times value was redeposited as much as how much value exists. Neither is an arithmetic flaw; both are reasons the number cannot bear the weight placed on it.

In practice

Who uses it
Analysts ranking protocols, treasuries screening where capital sits, and data providers publishing ecosystem aggregates.
Why it matters
It is the quickest available read on how much capital a contract currently holds, which bounds how large a position can be entered or exited there.
Limitations
It is not revenue, not users and not growth; it double-counts across layers, moves with prices, and is defined differently by each provider.

Common mistakes

  • Reading a rising figure as adoption when the token price did the work.
  • Summing protocol figures across an ecosystem as if there were no double counting.
  • Comparing two providers' numbers without checking what each counts.

Examples

numeric

A protocol holds 2,000 units of a token priced at 500, so its figure is 1,000,000. The price falls to 400 with every balance unchanged and the figure reads 800,000 — a twenty percent drop with no deposits or withdrawals at all.

Given
  • quantity held = 2,000 units, unchanged
  • price before = 500
  • price after = 400
Steps
  1. before: 2,000 x 500 = 1,000,000
  2. after: 2,000 x 400 = 800,000
  3. change = -200,000, i.e. -20%, entirely from price
The figure fell 20% while protocol usage was unchanged.

The same arithmetic in reverse explains most reported growth in a rising market.

crypto

A token is supplied to a lending protocol, the receipt token is deposited in a pool, and the pool token is staked in a third protocol. One underlying asset is now reported three times in an ecosystem total.

Mathematical foundation

TVL = \sum_{i} q_i \, p_i

Variables

q_iQuantity of asset i held in the protocol's contractstoken units
p_iMarket price of asset i in the reference currencyquote currency per unit
TVLTotal value locked in the reference currencyquote currency

Procedure

List every asset balance held by the contracts in scope, price each at a market rate in one reference currency, and sum. Decide and state explicitly whether staked receipt tokens, borrowed balances and bridged representations are included, because those choices change the result more than any pricing detail.

Worked example

Given
  • asset A: 2,000 units at 500
  • asset B: 1,500,000 units of a pegged asset at 1.00
Steps
  1. A: 2,000 x 500 = 1,000,000
  2. B: 1,500,000 x 1.00 = 1,500,000
  3. TVL = 1,000,000 + 1,500,000 = 2,500,000
TVL = 2,500,000 in the reference currency, on this definition of scope.

Interpretation

The output is the current market value of balances held, nothing more. It bounds the size of positions a protocol can support, and it is a poor proxy for usage, profitability or safety. Changes should always be decomposed into price and quantity before being described as growth.

Assumptions

  • A stated scope: which contracts, which assets, and whether receipt tokens, borrowed balances and bridged assets are counted. Data providers differ on all four.
  • A single reference currency and a single pricing source. Thinly traded assets priced at a pool quote can inflate the total.
  • Within an ecosystem aggregate, no adjustment for double counting unless the provider says otherwise.
Limits of the formula
  • The figure is a stock valued at spot, so most of its variation over short horizons is price, not activity.
  • Composability makes ecosystem totals exceed the value of the underlying assets, by an amount nobody can observe directly.
  • It is unrelated to fees earned, users served or solvency; a protocol with a large figure can still be loss-making or insolvent.

Commonly confused with

Protocol Revenue

Value locked is a balance held. Revenue is fees actually earned over a period; a large balance can produce almost none.

Commonly confused with

Market Capitalisation

Market capitalisation values a token's supply. Value locked values assets deposited in contracts, which may include none of that token.
