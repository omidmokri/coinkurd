Simple definition

A market belief that after bitcoin rises, money rotates into smaller coins and they outperform for a while. It is a widely repeated pattern, not a measured or reliable one.

Professional definition

A folk description of a phase in which altcoins outperform bitcoin, usually identified after the fact by falling bitcoin dominance. There is no agreed definition of when one starts or ends, no agreed sample of past episodes, and no registry source establishing it as a repeatable phenomenon — so it functions as narrative, not as a signal.

Technical definition

The claim is untestable as usually stated, because every term in it is chosen after the outcome: which assets count as alts, what threshold of outperformance qualifies, and over what window. Dominance falling is an arithmetic identity when altcoin values rise faster, so citing it as evidence is circular. Rotation stories are also vulnerable to survivorship: the assets remembered as the season's winners are selected from the ones that survived, while the segment's failures leave the sample.

Academic definition

A market-folklore construct describing episodic outperformance of non-bitcoin crypto-assets, lacking an agreed operational definition, an ex ante identification rule, or published evidence of recurrence sufficient to treat it as an empirical regularity.

Mechanism

The usual story has a plausible core — capital that has gained in a large liquid asset may rotate into smaller ones, and thin markets move further for the same flow — but plausibility is not evidence of recurrence. The pattern feels reliable because it is identified only once it is visible, which guarantees a clean example every time and no record of the times rotation never came.

In practice

Who uses it
Retail traders and social-media commentators, rotation-based discretionary traders, and analysts who have to explain what a claim about rotation would need in order to be testable.
Why it matters
It is in common use, so understanding what it does and does not assert is necessary to read market commentary critically.
Limitations
There is no agreed start rule, no agreed asset universe and no registry evidence of recurrence. Treating it as a tradable signal substitutes a story for a tested edge.

Common mistakes

  • Citing falling bitcoin dominance as proof, when that is the same fact restated.
  • Deciding a season has begun from a few days of outperformance.
  • Judging past seasons only from the assets that survived them.

Examples

market

Commentary declares a season under way because dominance has fallen. But dominance falls precisely when altcoin values rise faster than bitcoin's, so the observation and the claim are the same statement twice.

comparison

A testable version would fix the asset list, the outperformance threshold and the window in advance, then count how often the condition was followed by the claimed phase. No such published test is available in this encyclopedia's sources.

Commonly confused with

Bitcoin Dominance

Dominance is a computed ratio with a definition you can check. Altseason is a claim about what that ratio's movement implies for the future. The first is a measurement, the second an interpretation of it.

Deliberately framed as contested. No source in the closed registry establishes altseason as a measured or recurring phenomenon, and this entry says so explicitly rather than describing it as a market cycle stage. No episode is dated, no asset is named and no return figure is quoted, because the registry holds no dataset that would support one. The circularity of citing bitcoin dominance as evidence is an argument from the definition of that ratio, cross-referenced to the existing bitcoin-dominance entry rather than repeated here. The survivorship point is a general methodological caution, not a measured finding.
