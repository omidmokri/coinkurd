Working capital

ENGLISHWorking capitalreference

Category
Financial Foundations
Level
Professional

Simple definition

What a business has available in the short term after covering what it owes in the short term. It is the cushion that keeps day-to-day operations running.

Professional definition

Current assets less current liabilities. It measures short-term financial slack: a positive figure means short-dated resources exceed short-dated obligations, while a persistent deficit means the firm depends on refinancing or supplier credit to keep operating.

Technical definition

A balance-sheet difference, highly sensitive to definition. Operating variants exclude cash and short-term debt to isolate inventory, receivables and payables, because including them mixes financing decisions with operating ones. The level matters less than the direction and the cash conversion cycle behind it: a rising figure can mean growth or it can mean inventory nobody wants.

Academic definition

The difference between an entity's current assets and current liabilities, representing the net short-term resources available to fund continuing operations.

Mechanism

Cash leaves the business to buy inventory and returns only after goods are sold and customers pay. Working capital measures how much is trapped in that gap. A longer gap ties up more cash, which is why fast-growing firms can be profitable on paper and still run out of money.

In practice

Who uses it
Finance directors managing liquidity, credit analysts assessing short-term resilience, and investors reading cash-flow statements.
Why it matters
It links the income statement to actual cash: profit that sits in receivables and inventory does not pay wages.
Limitations
A single balance-sheet date can be managed; comparisons need the same definition, the same seasonality and ideally the cash conversion cycle alongside.

Common mistakes

  • Reading a higher figure as unambiguously better.
  • Comparing firms using different working-capital definitions.
  • Ignoring seasonal timing of the balance-sheet date.

Examples

market

A retailer reports record profit while cash falls. Inventory was built ahead of a season that never came, so the profit is sitting on shelves and working capital has swallowed the cash.

numeric

Current assets 480,000 against current liabilities 350,000 gives working capital of 130,000 and a current ratio of 1.37. If receivables of 120,000 prove uncollectible, the cushion nearly disappears.

Given
  • current assets = 480,000
  • current liabilities = 350,000
  • receivables included = 120,000
Steps
  1. WC = 480,000 - 350,000 = 130,000
  2. current ratio = 480,000 / 350,000 = 1.37
  3. if receivables fail: 360,000 - 350,000 = 10,000
WC = 130,000; falls to 10,000 if those receivables are written off

Illustrative figures. Quality of current assets matters as much as their total.

Mathematical foundation

WC = CA - CL, \qquad WC_{op} = (AR + INV) - AP

Procedure

Take current assets and current liabilities from the same balance sheet date and subtract. For the operating version, strip out cash and interest-bearing short-term debt first, and state which definition you used before comparing anything.

Worked example

Given
  • CA = 480,000
  • CL = 350,000
Steps
  1. 480,000 - 350,000
WC = 130,000

Interpretation

A positive figure says short-term resources currently exceed short-term claims, nothing more. Whether that is comfortable depends on how quickly inventory sells and receivables convert, which the difference alone cannot show.

Assumptions

  • Headline and operating definitions give different numbers; neither is more correct, but mixing them across companies invalidates the comparison.
  • Current assets are assumed convertible at carrying value, which fails for slow inventory and doubtful receivables.
  • A balance-sheet date is a snapshot and can be influenced by timing of payments around period end.
Limits of the formula
  • A high figure can reflect poor collection and excess stock rather than strength.
  • Negative working capital is normal and healthy in some business models where customers pay before suppliers are due.

Commonly confused with

Current Ratio

Working capital is a difference measured in currency; the current ratio is the same two numbers as a ratio. The difference shows size of cushion, the ratio shows proportion.
