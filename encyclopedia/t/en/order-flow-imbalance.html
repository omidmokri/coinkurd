Order Flow Imbalance

Simple definition

A measure of whether buyers or sellers were the ones pushing. If far more volume was bought at the ask than sold at the bid, flow was imbalanced to the buy side.

Professional definition

The signed net of aggressive buying and aggressive selling over a window, usually normalised by total volume so it sits between minus one and plus one. It is the standard short-horizon input in price-impact models, because net flow, not volume, is what moves quotes.

Technical definition

A family of related statistics, not a single quantity. The trade-based version nets buyer- and seller-initiated volume; the book-based version nets signed changes in queue sizes at the best bid and ask, so cancellations and new limit orders count even when nothing trades. The two correlate but are different objects, and either requires a rule assigning a side to each event.

Academic definition

A signed aggregate of liquidity-consuming activity over a time or event window, used as the explanatory variable in linear price-impact specifications.

Mechanism

A quote sits where the market maker is indifferent between the two sides. Persistent one-sided aggression drains depth on that side and loads inventory the wrong way, so the maker reprices to restore balance. Flow therefore moves price mechanically, before any news is interpreted — which is also why imbalance predicts the next tick far better than it predicts the next hour.

In practice

Who uses it
Market makers, short-horizon systematic traders, execution algorithms deciding when to post versus take, and researchers estimating price impact.
Why it matters
It compresses the raw event stream into one signed number that has a measured, if short-lived, relationship with the next price change.
Limitations
The relationship decays within seconds to minutes on liquid venues, and the sign convention, window length and normalisation all change the number.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing OFI figures from two venues computed under different definitions.
  • Reading a large imbalance as a directional forecast over hours.
  • Using exchange taker flags without checking how that venue assigns them.

Examples

market

During a fast move, prints arrive almost entirely at the ask. Volume looks heavy on both sides of the tape, but the signed net is strongly positive, and the quote ratchets upward without any single large order appearing.

numeric

In a one-minute window, 7.2 million of volume lifted the ask and 4.8 million hit the bid. Normalised imbalance is plus 0.20 — a modest tilt, not an extreme one, and it says nothing about the hour ahead.

Given
  • window = 60 seconds
  • buyer-initiated volume V_b = 7,200,000
  • seller-initiated volume V_s = 4,800,000
Steps
  1. net = 7,200,000 - 4,800,000 = 2,400,000
  2. total = 7,200,000 + 4,800,000 = 12,000,000
  3. OFI = 2,400,000 / 12,000,000 = 0.20
OFI = +0.20

Same raw data, book-based definition: had 2.4 million of that buying been met by fresh limit sells arriving at the ask, the queue-change version of OFI could be near zero. The definition must travel with the number.

Mathematical foundation

OFI_t = \frac{V^{b}_t - V^{s}_t}{V^{b}_t + V^{s}_t}

Variables

OFI_tNormalised order flow imbalance over window t, bounded in [-1, +1]ratio (dimensionless)
V^{b}_tBuyer-initiated (ask-side) volume in the windowvolume units
V^{s}_tSeller-initiated (bid-side) volume in the windowvolume units

Procedure

Fix a window, by clock time or by event count. Assign a side to every trade in it, using the venue's aggressor flag where one exists. Sum each side's volume, take the difference, and divide by the sum to normalise. Report the window length and the signing rule with the figure.

Worked example

Given
  • V_b = 7,200,000
  • V_s = 4,800,000
Steps
  1. numerator = 7,200,000 - 4,800,000 = 2,400,000
  2. denominator = 12,000,000
  3. OFI = 0.20
OFI = +0.20 for that window

Interpretation

Zero means aggression was balanced; the sign names the side that was paying for immediacy. Magnitude is comparable only within one instrument, one window length and one signing rule.

Assumptions

  • Competing definitions exist. The trade-based form above nets executed volume; the order-book form nets signed changes in depth at the best quotes, which also captures cancellations. They are not interchangeable and the literature uses both under the same name.
  • Every trade must be assigned an initiating side. Where the venue publishes no aggressor flag, the side is inferred, and inference rules disagree most on trades priced at the mid.
  • Normalising by total volume bounds the measure but discards scale; unnormalised net flow is the form used in linear impact regressions.
Limits of the formula
  • Short-horizon only: measured predictive content decays quickly, and a high reading is a description of what just happened rather than a forecast.
  • Wash trading and self-matching inflate both sides on venues without credible trade reporting, which can leave the ratio intact while making the underlying volumes meaningless.

Commonly confused with

Imbalance

In trading-desk usage, imbalance often means a visible gap in resting depth on the two sides of the book. Order flow imbalance is about executed or consumed flow over time, not a static snapshot.
