Bitcoin Dominance

Simple definition

Bitcoin's share of the total value of all cryptocurrencies, shown as a percentage. It rises when bitcoin grows faster than the rest, or shrinks slower.

Professional definition

The ratio of bitcoin's market capitalisation to the capitalisation of a chosen crypto universe, quoted as a percentage. Because both terms move, the ratio alone cannot say whether bitcoin rose or the rest fell, and the denominator differs by data provider.

Technical definition

A ratio of two estimates, each built from a marginal price and a provider-specific circulating supply. The denominator's composition drives the level: including stablecoins, including long-tail tokens, or adding one large new listing shifts the series without any change in bitcoin. Short-horizon moves in dominance are therefore partly an artefact of index maintenance, which is why levels are not comparable across sources or across years.

Academic definition

The proportion of aggregate cryptoasset market capitalisation attributable to bitcoin, a composite statistic whose level is conditional on the constituent universe and on the supply conventions used for each constituent.

Mechanism

The series is a quotient, so its direction reflects relative speed, not absolute moves. Dominance can rise in a falling market if bitcoin falls less, and fall in a rising market if the rest rise more. Reading it needs the two capitalisations separately; the ratio has already discarded the information that distinguishes those cases.

In practice

Who uses it
Allocators deciding between bitcoin and the rest of the market, index and product designers, and chartists who trade the dominance series itself.
Why it matters
It compresses a two-asset-group allocation question into one series, which makes regime talk easy and makes relative strength visible at a glance.
Limitations
It is a ratio, so it hides which side moved, and its level depends on an inclusion rule the chart rarely displays.

Common mistakes

  • Reading falling dominance as bitcoin weakness without checking its capitalisation.
  • Comparing dominance levels from two providers with different universes.
  • Treating a dominance chart as a tradable price series.

Examples

numeric

In an illustrative universe worth 1,000 units where bitcoin accounts for 600, dominance is 60 percent. If the rest of the market doubles to 800 while bitcoin is unchanged, dominance falls to 42.9 percent with no move in bitcoin at all.

Given
  • bitcoin capitalisation = 600 (illustrative units)
  • rest of the universe = 400
  • then: rest doubles to 800, bitcoin unchanged at 600
Steps
  1. start: 600 / (600 + 400) = 0.600
  2. after: 600 / (600 + 800) = 600 / 1,400 = 0.4286
dominance falls from 60.0% to 42.9% while bitcoin's own capitalisation never changes

Illustrative units, not observed values. The example exists to show that the numerator need not move for the ratio to move.

market

When a large new token is listed and included in a provider's index, dominance steps down on that day. Nothing happened to bitcoin; the denominator was redefined, which is why index-maintenance notes matter when reading the series.

Mathematical foundation

D = \frac{MC_{BTC}}{MC_{BTC} + \sum_{i} MC_{i}} \times 100

Variables

DDominance, in percentpercent
MC_{BTC}Bitcoin's market capitalisationcurrency
MC_{i}Market capitalisation of each other constituent in the chosen universecurrency

Procedure

Compute each constituent's capitalisation from one price and one supply figure at the same instant. Sum them to get the universe. Divide bitcoin's capitalisation by that sum and multiply by one hundred. State the inclusion rule, because the result is meaningless without it.

Worked example

Given
  • MC_BTC = 600 (illustrative units)
  • sum of all other constituents = 400
Steps
  1. universe = 600 + 400 = 1,000
  2. D = 600 / 1,000 = 0.60
  3. 0.60 x 100 = 60
D = 60 percent

Interpretation

The output is a share, not a price. A change in it means the two capitalisations grew at different rates; it does not identify which one moved, and it carries no directional information about either.

Assumptions

  • All constituent capitalisations are measured at the same instant with each provider's own circulating-supply definition.
  • The universe is a provider choice: whether stablecoins, wrapped assets and long-tail tokens are included changes the level, and providers disagree.
  • Index additions and removals are treated as redefinitions of the denominator, not as market moves.
Limits of the formula
  • The ratio cannot be decomposed after the fact: the same reading is consistent with bitcoin rising, the rest falling, or both.
  • Levels are not comparable across providers or across long spans, because the universe has grown and its composition has changed.
  • Illustrative values above are not observed market figures.

Commonly confused with

Market Capitalisation

Capitalisation is a level in currency; dominance is a share with no units. A rising dominance tells you nothing about whether the capitalisation behind it grew or shrank.
