Cash versus Physical Settlement

Simple definition

Two ways a contract can end. Cash settlement pays the difference in money. Physical settlement means the actual asset is delivered and paid for.

Professional definition

The contract's stated discharge method. Under cash settlement the final price is compared with the position's price and only the difference moves, so no asset changes hands. Under physical settlement the short delivers the specified grade and quantity and the long pays the settlement price for it, with venue procedures governing notices, locations and acceptable quality.

Technical definition

A specification choice with different residual risks. Cash settlement replaces delivery with a reference price, which removes logistics but makes the contract only as good as that reference and its manipulation safeguards. Physical settlement anchors the contract to the deliverable itself, which keeps the convergence honest but creates squeeze risk when deliverable supply is tight, plus storage, grade and location obligations a financial trader cannot meet.

Academic definition

The contractual mechanism of discharge at maturity: monetary exchange of the difference against a reference price, or transfer of title to the specified underlying against payment of the settlement price.

Mechanism

The choice decides who must be able to handle the asset. Cash settlement opens a commodity market to traders with no warehouse, which is why financial participation concentrates there. Physical settlement keeps a hard link to the deliverable, so the price cannot drift far from where the goods actually trade — the discipline comes from the obligation, not from the quote.

In practice

Who uses it
Commodity producers and consumers who want the goods, financial traders who do not, and operations teams that must know which obligation they hold.
Why it matters
It determines whether holding past the last trading day creates a payment or a lorry, and it governs which contract a hedger can actually use.
Limitations
Cash settlement inherits every weakness of its reference price; physical settlement inherits warehouse, grade, location and squeeze risk. Neither is safer in general.

Common mistakes

  • Holding a deliverable contract into delivery without the capacity to deliver or receive.
  • Assuming a crypto contract settles in the coin rather than in cash.
  • Ignoring which reference index a cash-settled contract uses.

Examples

market

A refinery holds a deliverable crude contract to take the barrels it genuinely needs. A fund holding the same contract must close or roll before delivery, because it has nowhere to put them. Same price exposure, entirely different obligation.

numeric

A short of two contracts covering 10 units each, entered at 80, runs to settlement at 76. Cash settled, the short receives 80 for the period. Physically settled, the short must instead deliver 20 units and receive 1,520.

Given
  • Short 2 contracts
  • Q_c = 10 units per contract
  • Entry price = 80
  • Final settlement price = 76
Steps
  1. Units = 2 x 10 = 20
  2. Cash settlement: (80 - 76) x 20 = 80 received
  3. Physical settlement: deliver 20 units, invoice = 76 x 20 = 1,520
Cash: a net receipt of 80. Physical: delivery of 20 units against 1,520, with the same 80 economic gain relative to selling at 76

The economics match only if the short already holds the deliverable. Acquiring it late, in a tight market, is where the two paths diverge.

Commonly confused with

Settlement

Settlement is the general act of completing a trade. Cash versus physical is the narrower question of what a dated contract delivers at maturity: a difference in money, or the asset itself.
