Mitigation Block

Simple definition

The candle zone at a swing that failed: price turned there, then went the other way through it. The zone is marked on the chart and watched if price comes back.

Professional definition

In smart-money teaching, the block formed at a swing point that was subsequently broken, drawn from the last candle before the failed move. Unlike an ordinary order block it carries a history: the structure at that swing was invalidated, and the zone is treated as where positions opened there would close at little or no loss.

Technical definition

Codeable as: detect a confirmed swing point; require that the structure it defined was later broken by a body close; then mark the last opposite-colour candle at that swing as the zone. Teachings disagree on body versus high-to-low boundaries, on whether a displacement filter is needed on the breaking leg, and on whether this differs at all from a breaker block, so the detected set depends on the configuration chosen.

Academic definition

A practitioner construct designating the candle region at an invalidated local extreme as a prospective reaction zone, motivated by an unobservable account of position management and lacking an established empirical validation.

Mechanism

Geometrically the zone is the edge of the region that traded just before a move which later failed, so it is the first area a return revisits after the structure flipped. That is a statement about the chart. The mitigation story — that someone trapped there closes at break-even and that this is why price reacts — describes intentions that no candle can show.

In practice

Who uses it
Advanced smart-money traders who want a zone on the side the structure just flipped to, and developers extending order-block code with a prior-invalidation condition.
Why it matters
It restricts zones to those that come with a structural event attached, which cuts the number of marked areas on a chart substantially.
Limitations
The naming overlaps with breaker block, boundaries and filters vary by teacher, the mitigation account is unobservable, and no peer-reviewed evidence base exists.

Common mistakes

  • Treating it as a stronger zone because it has a longer story.
  • Marking it without the required prior break.
  • Using mitigation block and breaker block as if both sides agreed on the definition.

Examples

market

A swing high forms at 120 and the market turns down. Weeks later price closes above 120, invalidating that swing. The last up-candle at the old high is marked, and the first pullback into it after the break is watched for a reaction.

numeric

The reference candle at the failed swing opens at 118.0, closes at 119.6, with a high of 120.0 and a low of 117.4. The body zone is 1.6 wide, the high-to-low zone 2.6. A stop beyond each differs by 0.6, which is 38 percent of the smaller risk.

Given
  • reference candle: open 118.0, close 119.6, high 120.0, low 117.4
  • stop placed just beyond the far edge of the zone
Steps
  1. body zone width = 119.6 - 118.0 = 1.6
  2. high-to-low zone width = 120.0 - 117.4 = 2.6
  3. difference in stop distance = 2.6 - 1.6 = 1.0 at the far edge, 0.6 at the near edge
  4. 0.6 / 1.6 = 0.375
The two zone conventions differ by up to 1.0 in stop distance from one candle.

Position size is computed from stop distance, so the convention changes the size of every trade. It has to be fixed before any backtest.

Commonly confused with

Order Block

An order block only needs an impulsive move away from a candle. A mitigation block additionally requires that the swing at that candle was later invalidated. Every mitigation block is a block with a history; not every block has one.

Flagged by the editor as uncertain on a linguistic, mathematical or regulatory point.

The classical observation that a broken level often reverses role is cited and is the only part of this entry a registry source supports. The term, its zone boundaries, the displacement filters some teachers add and the distinction from breaker block all come from the retail smart-money tradition, which no registry source documents and for which no peer-reviewed evidence base exists. The mitigation account describes position management that cannot be observed from price data. Persian and Kurdish both keep the English term, which is what practitioners say.
