Protocol Revenue

Simple definition

The part of the fees a protocol keeps for itself, after paying the people who supplied the capital. It is much smaller than the total fees users pay.

Professional definition

Fees retained by the protocol or its treasury, as distinct from total fees paid by users, most of which go to liquidity providers or suppliers. The retained share is set by a parameter — a fee switch, a reserve factor, a performance cut — and can be changed by governance.

Technical definition

The distinction matters because the two figures answer different questions. Total fees measure how much activity the protocol intermediates; retained fees measure what accrues to the protocol itself and is therefore the only candidate for a valuation input. Even then it is gross: contributor costs, audits, incentive emissions and bad debt are not deducted, so retained fees are not profit. Where revenue is denominated in the protocol's own token, it is also partly a circular measure.

Academic definition

The share of transaction fees accruing to a protocol's treasury rather than to supply-side participants, determined by governance-set parameters and distinct from both gross fee volume and economic profit.

Mechanism

Most of what users pay is a payment for capital, not for the protocol. Setting the retained share high enough to be meaningful makes the venue less attractive to the providers whose capital creates the service in the first place, so the parameter is a genuine trade-off rather than a free lever. That is why fee-switch votes are contested and why a large fee figure says little on its own.

In practice

Who uses it
Analysts valuing a governance token, treasury managers planning spending, and holders voting on whether to turn a fee switch on.
Why it matters
It separates how much business a protocol does from how much of it the protocol actually keeps, which is the distinction most headline fee figures blur.
Limitations
It is gross, not profit; it depends on parameters governance can change; and token-denominated revenue co-moves with the token being valued.

Common mistakes

  • Quoting total user fees as the protocol's revenue.
  • Annualising a single strong month of fees into a run rate.
  • Treating retained fees as cash available to holders when they sit in a treasury.

Examples

numeric

Users pay 4,000,000 in fees over a quarter. Liquidity providers keep 85 percent, so protocol revenue is 600,000 — fifteen percent of the figure most commonly quoted as the protocol's earnings.

Given
  • total fees paid by users over the quarter = 4,000,000
  • supply-side share = 85%
  • protocol take rate = 15%
Steps
  1. supply-side payout = 4,000,000 x 0.85 = 3,400,000
  2. protocol revenue = 4,000,000 - 3,400,000 = 600,000
  3. equivalently 4,000,000 x 0.15 = 600,000
Protocol revenue 600,000 against 4,000,000 of total fees.

Still gross: contributor costs, audits, emissions and bad debt are not deducted, so this is not profit.

market

A governance vote raises the retained share. Revenue rises immediately, and over the following weeks some liquidity migrates to a cheaper venue, so volume and therefore total fees fall. The net effect is not knowable from the revenue line alone.

Mathematical foundation

R_{\text{protocol}} = F_{\text{total}} - F_{\text{supply}} = \tau \, F_{\text{total}}

Variables

R_{\text{protocol}}Fees retained by the protocol or its treasuryquote currency
F_{\text{total}}All fees paid by users over the periodquote currency
F_{\text{supply}}Fees paid out to liquidity providers or suppliersquote currency
\tauTake rate: the governance-set share the protocol retainsfraction

Procedure

Measure total fees over a stated period from on-chain data. Subtract the portion routed to suppliers under the protocol's own distribution rules, or equivalently apply the take rate. State the denomination: revenue earned in the protocol's own token should be reported separately from revenue in a pegged asset, because the two carry different risk.

Worked example

Given
  • F_total = 4,000,000 over one quarter
  • take rate tau = 0.15
Steps
  1. F_supply = 4,000,000 x 0.85 = 3,400,000
  2. R_protocol = 4,000,000 - 3,400,000 = 600,000
Protocol revenue = 600,000 for the quarter.

Interpretation

The figure is what reached the protocol, not what the protocol earned. Read beside total fees it shows how much of the business the protocol is able to keep; read alone it is easily mistaken for profit, which it is not until costs and emissions are deducted.

Assumptions

  • A stated measurement period and a stated set of contracts. Protocols with multiple deployments and chains are often aggregated inconsistently between data sources.
  • The take rate is a governance parameter and may differ by pool, asset or market within one protocol.
  • Revenue is reported gross of contributor costs, audits, incentive emissions and bad debt.
Limits of the formula
  • It is not profit and should never be annualised from a single period without saying so.
  • Revenue denominated in the protocol's own token is partly circular when used to value that token.
  • Comparisons across protocols require identical treatment of supply-side payouts, which data providers do not guarantee.
