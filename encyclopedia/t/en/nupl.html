Net Unrealised Profit/Loss

Simple definition

An on-chain estimate of how much of the market is sitting on paper gains. It compares what the coins are worth now with what they last moved at, as a share of market value.

Professional definition

Market capitalisation minus realised capitalisation, divided by market capitalisation. Realised cap values every unit of supply at the price of its last on-chain movement, so the difference is the aggregate paper profit or loss of the holder base, expressed as a fraction of market value.

Technical definition

A normalised spread between two supply valuations: one at the current price, one at each unit's last-moved price. The quotient is bounded above by 1 and unbounded below, and it is the same quantity MVRV reports in ratio form. Its inputs inherit every limitation of realised capitalisation: coins whose keys are lost keep an ancient cost basis, transfers between addresses of one exchange reset cost bases without any economic sale, and the metric exists for only a handful of market cycles, so its historical bands rest on very few observations.

Academic definition

A normalised measure of aggregate unrealised profit in a cryptoasset, defined as the difference between market and realised capitalisation scaled by market capitalisation.

Mechanism

Realised capitalisation is a cost-basis proxy built from last-movement prices, so NUPL is the gap between what holders paid, as the chain records it, and what the market now says. It widens in advances because the current price outruns old cost bases, and turns negative when price sits below the average last-moved price of supply.

In practice

Who uses it
On-chain analysts, cycle researchers, and desks using holder cost basis as one input among several.
Why it matters
It puts price in the context of what the holder base paid, which price alone cannot show and which order-book data does not contain.
Limitations
It inherits realised cap's distortions: lost coins, exchange internal transfers that reset cost bases without a sale, and only a handful of cycles of history. Vendor supply and clustering choices change the series.

Common mistakes

  • Treating historical bands as cycle tops and bottoms.
  • Comparing NUPL across assets with different chain histories.
  • Reading an exchange-driven cost-basis reset as real selling.

Examples

crypto

A long sideways stretch lets supply change hands near the current price. Cost bases reset upward, realised cap rises, and NUPL falls without price falling at all.

numeric

With a market capitalisation of 800 and a realised capitalisation of 500, NUPL is 0.375: about 37.5 percent of market value is aggregate paper profit at that moment.

Given
  • market capitalisation = 800 (illustrative units)
  • realised capitalisation = 500
  • same supply definition used in both terms
Steps
  1. 800 - 500 = 300
  2. 300 / 800 = 0.375
NUPL = 0.375

Illustrative units, not observed values. The equivalent MVRV ratio here is 800/500 = 1.6, which is the same information in a different form.

Mathematical foundation

Procedure

Value circulating supply at the current price to get market capitalisation. Value the same supply at each unit's last-moved price to get realised capitalisation. Subtract, then divide by market capitalisation so the result is a fraction of market value rather than a currency amount.

Worked example

Given
  • MC = 800
  • RC = 500
Steps
  1. MC - RC = 300
  2. 300 / 800 = 0.375
  3. check: 1 - 1/(800/500) = 1 - 0.625 = 0.375
NUPL = 0.375

Interpretation

A positive value means supply is held above its recorded cost basis in aggregate; a negative value means below. The number is a state description of the holder base, not a signal, and its magnitude depends on how the vendor defines supply.

Assumptions

  • Realised capitalisation treats the price at a unit's last on-chain movement as its cost basis, which equates movement with acquisition.
  • Both terms must use the same supply definition; excluding or including provably lost or long-dormant coins changes the result.
  • Off-chain trading inside custodians is invisible to the chain, so cost bases for custodial balances are not observed.
Limits of the formula
  • Coins with lost keys retain an ancient cost basis that the metric cannot distinguish from a patient holder, which inflates aggregate unrealised profit.
  • Internal transfers between a single exchange's addresses reset cost bases without any economic sale, moving realised cap for non-economic reasons.
  • Only a handful of market cycles exist for any asset, so historical bands are descriptions of very few observations, not estimated thresholds.

MVRV

MVRV is the ratio of market to realised capitalisation; NUPL is the same comparison expressed as a share of market value. They are algebraically linked, so they cannot disagree.
