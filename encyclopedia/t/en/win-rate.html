Win Rate

ENGLISHWin Ratereference

Category
Risk Management
Level
Foundation

Simple definition

The share of trades that end in profit. Forty wins out of a hundred trades is a win rate of forty percent, and it says nothing about how big the wins were.

Professional definition

Winning trades divided by total closed trades over a defined sample. On its own it ranks nothing: paired with average win and average loss it produces expectancy, which is the figure that decides whether a strategy makes money.

Technical definition

A sample proportion, with the sampling error that implies: a run of thirty trades gives a very wide confidence interval, so small samples cannot distinguish a real edge from noise. It is also sensitive to definitional choices, since scratch trades, partial exits and fees all change what counts as a win.

Academic definition

The empirical frequency of favourable outcomes in a sample of closed positions, an estimator of success probability whose standard error scales with the inverse square root of sample size.

Mechanism

A win rate is the frequency half of expected value; reward-to-risk is the size half. Because the two multiply, they trade off: tight targets raise the win rate and shrink the average win, wide targets do the reverse. Strategies at opposite ends of that trade-off can share the same expectancy and feel completely different to run.

In practice

Who uses it
Strategy developers reviewing a trade log, prop desks setting evaluation rules, and traders comparing two rule sets on the same data.
Why it matters
It is the frequency input to expectancy, and it describes the psychological load of a strategy: how many losses in a row the trader must sit through.
Limitations
It is meaningless without average win and average loss, and unreliable on small samples. Marketing figures are usually quoted on the sample that flatters them.

Common mistakes

  • Judging a strategy on its win rate alone.
  • Reading a win rate from fewer than a few dozen trades.
  • Excluding break-even or fee-only trades to lift the number.

Examples

market

A trend strategy wins three trades in ten and still compounds, because the three winners run far. A mean-reversion strategy wins eight in ten and can still lose money if the two losers are large enough.

numeric

Out of 100 closed trades, 40 are winners, so the win rate is 40 percent. If the average win is 2.5 times the average loss, expectancy is positive at 0.4 units of risk per trade despite the majority of trades losing.

Given
  • closed trades n = 100
  • winning trades n_w = 40
  • average win = 2.5 units of risk
  • average loss = 1.0 unit of risk
  • fees already inside the trade results
Steps
  1. W = 40 / 100 = 0.40
  2. expectancy = W x average win - (1 - W) x average loss
  3. = 0.40 x 2.5 - 0.60 x 1.0
  4. = 1.00 - 0.60
W = 40 percent and expectancy = +0.40 units of risk per trade

The break-even win rate at a reward-to-risk of 2.5 is 1 / 3.5 = 28.6 percent, so 40 percent clears it. Below 28.6 percent the same strategy loses money.

Mathematical foundation

W = \frac{n_w}{n}, \qquad \mathbb{E}[\text{trade}] = W\,\overline{G} - (1 - W)\,\overline{L}

Variables

WWin rate: the proportion of closed trades that were profitablefraction
n_wNumber of winning tradescount
nTotal number of closed trades in the samplecount
\overline{G}Average size of a winning tradeaccount currency or units of risk
\overline{L}Average size of a losing trade, as a positive magnitudeaccount currency or units of risk

Procedure

Count closed trades and the subset that closed in profit, then divide. For expectancy, compute the average winning result and the average losing result over the same sample, weight each by its frequency and subtract. Express both averages in the same unit, either account currency or multiples of the risk taken.

Worked example

Given
  • n = 100
  • n_w = 40
  • average win = 2.5 units of risk
  • average loss = 1.0 unit of risk
Steps
  1. W = 40 / 100 = 0.40
  2. W x average win = 0.40 x 2.5 = 1.00
  3. (1 - W) x average loss = 0.60 x 1.0 = 0.60
  4. expectancy = 1.00 - 0.60
W = 0.40 and expectancy = +0.40 units of risk per trade

Interpretation

The win rate alone says how often, never how much. Expectancy combines the two into the average result per trade, which is the only one of the three numbers that can be multiplied by trade count to project an outcome.

Assumptions

  • Trades are counted consistently: the same treatment of scratch trades, partial exits and fees throughout the sample.
  • The expectancy form assumes results split cleanly into wins and losses, so break-even trades must either be assigned or excluded explicitly.
  • Averages are taken over the same sample as the win rate; mixing periods invalidates the product.
Limits of the formula
  • As a sample proportion it carries standard error of roughly the square root of W(1-W)/n, so forty trades cannot establish an edge of a few percentage points.
  • It is unstable under changes to exit rules, so win rates from differently exited strategies are not comparable.

Commonly confused with

Expected Value

Win rate is a frequency; expected value is a frequency multiplied by sizes. A ninety percent win rate with one catastrophic loser has a negative expected value.
