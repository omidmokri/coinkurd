Simple definition

A stretch of chart that price crossed so fast that one side barely traded there. On a candle chart it looks like a long body with little or no overlap with its neighbours.

Professional definition

In price-action teaching, a region traversed by a one-sided move where little two-way trade took place. It is identified on candles as non-overlapping bodies or wick gaps between consecutive bars, and the region is watched for a later return that trades through it more evenly.

Technical definition

Candle-based detection compares consecutive bars: an up-imbalance exists over the interval between the high of bar i-1 and the low of bar i+1 when that interval is above the body of bar i, which is the three-candle construction formalised as a fair value gap. Volume-profile practitioners instead define it from the profile, as price levels with very little traded volume. The two definitions do not mark the same places, and neither is derived from observed order flow.

Academic definition

A region of the price domain traversed with little two-sided transaction, identified either from the geometry of consecutive bars or from low traded volume in a price-distribution profile.

Mechanism

A fast one-sided leg consumes resting orders faster than they are replaced, so some prices are crossed with almost no opposing volume. That much is a real statement about execution speed and book depth. The further claim that price must come back to such a region to balance it is not a mechanism, it is an expectation, and it is routinely violated.

In practice

Who uses it
Price-action traders marking regions for a possible return, volume-profile traders reading low-volume nodes, and developers coding gap or profile detection.
Why it matters
It gives an objective, codeable way to mark where a move was one-sided, which can be used as a target zone or as evidence that a leg was impulsive.
Limitations
Candle and volume-profile definitions disagree, fill rates are not established by any peer-reviewed study, and the balancing story is an expectation.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming every imbalance must be filled.
  • Mixing candle imbalances with low-volume profile nodes as one concept.
  • Marking them on one timeframe and trading another.

Examples

market

After a scheduled data release one candle covers more ground than the previous twenty, and its body sits entirely above the prior candle's high. The untraded strip between them is marked, and a pullback a day later trades back through it slowly.

numeric

Bar i-1 has a high of 101.0, bar i runs from 101.5 to 106.0, bar i+1 has a low of 102.4. The untraded strip is 101.0 to 102.4, so 1.4 wide. Shifting the rule to bodies only would move both edges and change the width.

Given
  • bar i-1 high = 101.0
  • bar i range = 101.5 to 106.0
  • bar i+1 low = 102.4
Steps
  1. lower edge of the untraded strip = high of bar i-1 = 101.0
  2. upper edge = low of bar i+1 = 102.4
  3. width = 102.4 - 101.0 = 1.4
  4. midpoint = (101.0 + 102.4) / 2 = 101.7
A 1.4-wide imbalance between 101.0 and 102.4, with a midpoint at 101.7.

Some teachings treat the midpoint as the level that counts as filled, others require the full strip. State which before measuring fill rates.

Commonly confused with

Order Flow Imbalance

Order-flow imbalance is measured from trade and quote data as a difference between buy and sell pressure. Chart imbalance is inferred from candle geometry. One is a measurement of flow, the other a description of shape.

Commonly confused with

Fair Value Gap

Fair value gap is the specific three-candle formalisation of a chart imbalance. Imbalance is the broader word, and also covers low-volume nodes on a volume profile, which the three-candle rule does not detect.

Flagged by the editor as uncertain on a linguistic, mathematical or regulatory point.

The classical treatment of gaps and candle windows is cited and supports the geometric observation only. The smart-money vocabulary of imbalance and inefficiency, and the claim that such regions must be revisited, come from the retail tradition, which no registry source documents and for which no peer-reviewed evidence base exists. The candle definition and the volume-profile definition genuinely mark different places. Kurdish headword follows the project's own wording in the category list and is a proposal needing native-expert review.
