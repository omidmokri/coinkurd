Keltner Channel

Simple definition

A band drawn above and below a moving average, set a multiple of average true range away from it.

Professional definition

A volatility envelope. The centre line is usually a 20-period exponential moving average of the close, and the two outer lines sit a fixed multiple of average true range above and below it, commonly two. Because the width comes from true range, it responds to gaps and to the whole high-low span, not only to closes.

Technical definition

The modern construction is centre plus or minus m times ATR. Chester Keltner's original was different: a ten-day simple average of typical price with bands set from the ten-day average daily range, which is why descriptions in circulation disagree and the parameters must always be stated. The difference from Bollinger Bands is the width measure — standard deviation of closes against average true range of bars. One extreme close widens Bollinger more; a run of wide bars widens Keltner more.

Academic definition

A volatility envelope about a moving average whose half-width is a fixed multiple of average true range.

Mechanism

True range measures how much ground each bar covered, including any gap from the previous close, so an ATR-scaled band widens when bars get large regardless of where they close. Standard deviation measures how far closes scatter about their mean. The two can diverge: a market that swings widely inside each bar but closes near its average has high ATR and low close dispersion.

In practice

Who uses it
Volatility-breakout and trend developers, and traders who want a band that reacts to bar range rather than to closing dispersion.
Why it matters
It scales the band to how far price actually travels per bar, which makes one multiplier usable across instruments and across volatility regimes.
Limitations
The centre type, the ATR length and the multiplier are all free parameters, the original and modern definitions differ, and a band is not a boundary: price can walk along it.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing Keltner results across studies without stating the multiplier and ATR length.
  • Treating a touch of the upper band as an overbought signal.
  • Assuming Keltner and Bollinger widths are interchangeable.

Examples

chart

A market opens with a large gap and then trades quietly all day. The Keltner band widens, because true range includes the gap. Bollinger Bands barely move, because the closes did not scatter.

numeric

With the average at 100, a ten-period ATR of 2.5 and a multiplier of two, the band runs 95 to 105. Two standard deviations of closes in the same window measure 1.2, so Bollinger Bands would run 97.6 to 102.4.

Given
  • EMA(20) of close = 100
  • ATR(10) = 2.5
  • multiplier m = 2
  • standard deviation of closes over the same window = 1.2
Steps
  1. Keltner upper = 100 + 2 x 2.5 = 105
  2. Keltner lower = 100 - 2 x 2.5 = 95
  3. Keltner width = 10 points
  4. Bollinger at 2 sigma = 100 +/- 2 x 1.2, that is 97.6 to 102.4, a width of 4.8
On the same data the ATR band is 10 points wide and the standard-deviation band 4.8.

Neither width is more correct. They measure different things: distance travelled per bar against scatter of closing prices.

Mathematical foundation

KC^{mid}_t = EMA_n(C), \qquad KC^{upper}_t = EMA_n(C) + m \times ATR_p, \qquad KC^{lower}_t = EMA_n(C) - m \times ATR_p

Variables

EMA_n(C)Exponential moving average of the close over n periods; 20 is the common defaultprice units
ATR_pAverage true range over p periods, Wilder-smoothed in its original formprice units
mBand multiplier; commonly between 1.5 and 2.5ratio (dimensionless)
n, pLookback lengths for the centre line and for the range measure; they need not be equalperiods

Procedure

Compute the centre line as an exponential moving average of the close over n periods. Compute average true range over p periods, where true range for each bar is the largest of the high-low span, the distance from the previous close to the high, and the distance from the previous close to the low. Add and subtract m times that ATR from the centre line.

Worked example

Given
  • EMA(20) of close = 100
  • ATR(10) = 2.5
  • m = 2
Steps
  1. upper = 100 + 2 x 2.5 = 105
  2. lower = 100 - 2 x 2.5 = 95
  3. width = 105 - 95 = 10, which is 10% of the centre line
Keltner channel 95 to 105 about a centre of 100.

Interpretation

The two lines say how far price has moved relative to how far it has lately been moving per bar. A close outside the band means the move was large by the standard of recent bar ranges; it does not mean the move is finished.

Assumptions

  • The modern convention is an exponential centre and an ATR half-width, with m usually between 1.5 and 2.5 and the ATR length 10 or 20. There is no single standard, so every published result must name its parameters.
  • Chester Keltner's original construction used a simple average of typical price with bands drawn from the average daily range, which produces different values from the same data. Descriptions in circulation mix the two.
  • ATR in its original form is Wilder-smoothed. Platforms offering a simple or exponential average of true range instead give different band widths.
Limits of the formula
  • Three free parameters make it easy to fit a channel to a sample; a result quoted without the centre type, ATR length and multiplier is not reproducible.
  • The band describes recent range, so price can walk along the upper line through an entire trend without the band meaning anything is stretched.
  • Because true range includes gaps, a single gap widens the band sharply even when closes barely move.

Related terminology

related
Bollinger Bands Average True Range Moving Average Channel Volatility Breakout Standard Deviation
Commonly confused with

Bollinger Bands

Both are envelopes around a moving average. Bollinger width is a multiple of the standard deviation of closes; Keltner width is a multiple of average true range, which includes gaps and intrabar range.
