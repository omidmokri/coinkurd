Money Flow Index

Simple definition

A 0 to 100 reading much like RSI, except each period is weighted by how much money changed hands, not only by the price move.

Professional definition

A volume-weighted momentum oscillator. Typical price times volume gives each period's raw money flow; flows from periods where typical price rose are summed as positive and those where it fell as negative over a lookback, usually 14, and the ratio is mapped onto 0 to 100 exactly as RSI maps its own ratio.

Technical definition

MFI is RSI with money flow in place of price change: the same bounded mapping, so the same saturation in a trend. Two differences matter. Periods where typical price is unchanged are excluded from both sums by convention, so the effective window can be shorter than n. And the weighting is by notional turnover, so one outsized bar can set the reading for the whole window, which makes MFI noisier than RSI on thin instruments.

Academic definition

A bounded oscillator formed by mapping the ratio of positive to negative volume-weighted typical-price flows over a fixed lookback onto the interval [0, 100].

Mechanism

Weighting each period by turnover makes the oscillator report where the money went rather than only where the price went. That helps when a price move happened on negligible size, and hurts when one oversized bar sets the reading for an entire window. Because the output is bounded, it saturates in a trend for exactly the reason RSI does.

In practice

Who uses it
Volume-aware oscillator users, divergence traders, and anyone screening for price moves unsupported by turnover.
Why it matters
It puts price direction and traded size on one bounded scale, so a move on thin volume scores lower than the same move on heavy volume.
Limitations
Crypto volume is venue-specific and can be inflated, periods with an unchanged typical price are dropped from the window, and the 80 and 20 levels are convention just as RSI's 70 and 30 are.

Common mistakes

  • Using exchange-reported volume without checking how it is measured.
  • Reading 80 as a sell signal in a strong trend.
  • Comparing MFI across instruments with very different turnover.

Examples

market

Two instruments rise the same four per cent over a week. One does it on steady heavy turnover and its MFI climbs; the other does it on fading volume and its MFI lags behind the price, which is the comparison the measure exists to make.

numeric

Over fourteen periods positive money flow totals 1.2 million and negative money flow 0.8 million. The ratio is 1.5 and MFI is 60 — the same arithmetic RSI performs on average gain and average loss.

Given
  • n = 14
  • positive money flow = 1,200,000
  • negative money flow = 800,000
  • periods with an unchanged typical price are excluded from both sums
Steps
  1. money flow ratio = 1,200,000 / 800,000 = 1.5
  2. MFI = 100 - 100 / (1 + 1.5) = 100 - 40 = 60
  3. equivalently MFI = 100 x 1,200,000 / 2,000,000 = 60
MFI = 60.

A reading of 60 describes the balance of volume-weighted flows over the window. It is not a probability of any future move.

Mathematical foundation

MFI_n = 100 - \frac{100}{1 + MFR} = 100 \times \frac{PMF}{PMF + NMF}, \qquad MFR = \frac{PMF}{NMF}, \qquad TP_t = \frac{H_t + L_t + C_t}{3}, \qquad RMF_t = TP_t \times V_t

Procedure

For each period compute typical price and multiply it by volume to get raw money flow. Compare typical price with the previous period's: classify the flow as positive if it rose, negative if it fell, and discard the period if it is unchanged. Sum each class over the last n periods, divide the positive sum by the negative sum, and map the ratio onto 0 to 100 with the formula above.

Worked example

Given
  • n = 14
  • positive money flow over the window = 1,200,000
  • negative money flow over the window = 800,000
Steps
  1. MFR = 1,200,000 / 800,000 = 1.5
  2. 1 + MFR = 2.5
  3. 100 / 2.5 = 40
  4. MFI = 100 - 40 = 60
MFI = 60.

Interpretation

The output is a position on a fixed 0 to 100 scale where 50 means positive and negative volume-weighted flows were equal over the window. It describes the recent balance of turnover by direction and carries no forecast.

Assumptions

  • Typical price, the mean of high, low and close, is the standard input; implementations that use the close give different sums.
  • Periods where typical price equals the previous typical price are excluded from both sums by convention, so the effective window can hold fewer than n observations.
  • If negative money flow over the window is zero, the ratio is undefined and MFI is set to 100 by convention, exactly as RSI is.
  • Volume units must be consistent. In crypto, volume is reported in base units on some feeds and in quote currency on others; multiplying typical price by quote-denominated volume counts the price twice and silently corrupts every value.
Limits of the formula
  • Bounded at 0 and 100, so it saturates and can sit above 80 for long stretches in a trend without a reversal.
  • Turnover weighting makes one unusually large bar dominate the window, which is more damaging on thin instruments than on liquid ones.
  • Volume in crypto markets is venue-specific and some reported figures are unreliable, so the same pair can produce different MFI values on different data feeds.

Commonly confused with

Relative Strength Index

They share the same 0 to 100 mapping. RSI weights each period by the size of its price change; MFI weights it by typical price times volume.
