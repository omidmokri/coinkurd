Foundation

Simple definition

Price moving decisively past a level that had been holding it back, such as a previous high, the edge of a range, or a trendline.

Professional definition

The resolution of a level: price trades and closes beyond a zone that had been absorbing order flow, often with a rise in volume. It reports that the resting orders on one side have been exhausted; whether it leads anywhere is a separate question answered only afterwards.

Technical definition

An event defined entirely by its filter. Beyond the level can mean an intraday touch, a close, a close by some multiple of ATR, or a close that holds for k bars, and each rule selects a different population of events with a different base rate. Without a stated filter the concept has no measurable properties, so published breakout statistics describe a code definition rather than a market regularity.

Academic definition

The passage of price through a previously absorbing region, operationalised by an explicit threshold and confirmation rule.

Mechanism

A level holds because resting orders sit on it. Price can only pass once those orders are filled, so passage is evidence that one side's patience has run out. Stops parked just beyond then fire as market orders, adding flow in a single direction. That is the mechanical reason breaks often move fast and then stall once the stop pocket is empty.

In practice

Who uses it
Momentum and trend-following traders, systematic strategy developers, and market makers who must widen quotes when a level gives way.
Why it matters
It supplies a trigger that can be coded exactly and an invalidation just back inside the level, so the risk is small relative to the move being chased.
Limitations
False breaks are common, in thin books especially, and the entry is the worst price of the move so far. Every published statistic depends on the filter used.

Common mistakes

  • Entering on a wick that pokes through and closes back inside the level.
  • Using one filter on a liquid pair and an illiquid one alike.
  • Treating a thin weekend break as equivalent to a full-session break.

Examples

market

An asset ranges between 95 and 100 for three weeks, then closes at 102 on heavy volume. The range edge is now the invalidation level: a return and a close back under 100 says the break failed.

numeric

With resistance at 100 and ATR at 2.0, a filter of half an ATR needs a close above 101. A close at 100.4 is above the level but inside the filter, so under this rule no breakout has occurred.

Given
  • resistance level = 100
  • ATR(14) = 2.0
  • filter = 0.5 x ATR
  • candidate close = 100.4
Steps
  1. threshold = 100 + 0.5 x 2.0 = 101
  2. 100.4 < 101
no breakout under a half-ATR close filter

Change the filter to a bare touch and the same bar counts as a breakout. The event rate is a property of the rule.

Commonly confused with

Break of Structure

A breakout passes a chosen level of any kind. A break of structure specifically passes the swing point that defined the prevailing market structure, so it is a statement about structure, not about a drawn level.
