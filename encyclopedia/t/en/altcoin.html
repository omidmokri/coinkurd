Simple definition

Any cryptocurrency other than bitcoin. The word says what a coin is not, so it covers everything from large settlement networks to tokens with no users at all.

Professional definition

A residual category: every cryptoasset except bitcoin, and in some desks' usage except ether too. It carries no information about design, supply or purpose — only about not being the reference asset. In practice it is shorthand for the higher-beta end of the market.

Technical definition

Because the class is defined by exclusion, statistics about it are artefacts of the chosen universe. Most altcoins are quoted against bitcoin or a stablecoin, so their dollar performance mixes two exposures and an exit prices in the quote asset's own move. Liquidity is concentrated in a handful of pairs, supply is often concentrated among few holders, and any historical study of altcoin returns is severely survivorship-biased.

Academic definition

A residual classification denoting any cryptoasset other than bitcoin; being defined by exclusion, it has no intrinsic technical or economic content and its aggregate statistics depend wholly on the sampling universe.

Mechanism

Most altcoin liquidity routes through bitcoin and stablecoin pairs, so flows reach them second-hand. That ordering, plus smaller free float, is why the same shift in risk appetite moves altcoins further in both directions than it moves the reference asset. Nothing about the label causes this; the market plumbing does.

In practice

Who uses it
Rotation traders, listing and research teams screening new assets, and anyone whose portfolio is denominated in something other than the quote asset.
Why it matters
The label is a rough risk bucket: it separates the asset most of the market quotes against from everything priced relative to it.
Limitations
It groups assets with nothing in common, so any statement about altcoins as a class is almost always too broad to act on.

Common mistakes

  • Treating a large network and a dormant token as one asset class.
  • Measuring altcoin performance in dollars while funding the trade in bitcoin.
  • Reading altcoin index history without adjusting for delisted and dead tokens.

Examples

numeric

A token rises 20 percent in dollars while bitcoin rises 30 percent. Measured against bitcoin, the token lost about 7.7 percent. A trader funded in bitcoin made a loss on a position that looked green on a dollar chart.

Given
  • token return in quote currency = +20%
  • bitcoin return in the same quote currency = +30%
  • position funded in bitcoin
Steps
  1. relative factor = 1.20 / 1.30 = 0.9231
  2. 0.9231 - 1 = -0.0769
about -7.7% measured in bitcoin terms

Illustrative returns. The denominator is a choice, and it changes the sign of the answer, not just its size.

market

Periods when altcoins outperform are often described as rotation out of bitcoin. The flows are rarely observable; what is observable is that the ratio between the two capitalisations changed, which has at least two possible causes.

Commonly confused with

Stablecoin

A stablecoin is an altcoin by the usual definition, yet it is built to do the opposite of what the altcoin label implies: hold value rather than move with risk appetite.
