Volume-Weighted Average Price

Simple definition

The average price traded over a session, weighted by how much changed hands at each price. It tells you whether your fills beat the day's typical price.

Professional definition

Total traded value divided by total traded quantity over a defined window, almost always one session. Buy-side desks use it as an execution benchmark: buying below the session VWAP is a better-than-average outcome. Plotted on a chart it resets at each session boundary, so it is not a moving average.

Technical definition

Computed from trade prints, or approximated on bar data as the typical price (high plus low plus close, divided by three) weighted by bar volume. It is cumulative within its window: early trades keep their full weight all session, so VWAP grows steadily less responsive as the session matures, and the reset is what keeps it meaningful. On 24-hour crypto venues there is no natural session, so the anchor must be stated; an anchored VWAP from a chosen date is a different statistic.

Academic definition

The quantity-weighted mean transaction price over a specified interval and venue, used principally as a benchmark for execution quality.

Mechanism

Weighting by volume makes the figure represent where capital actually traded rather than where price merely visited. A price touched on one small print barely moves it; the price at which most of the day's size changed hands dominates it. That is precisely the property an execution benchmark needs, and the reason a VWAP line does not behave like a time-weighted average.

In practice

Who uses it
Execution traders and brokers measured against it, scheduling algorithms that slice an order to track it, and intraday traders using it as a reference level.
Why it matters
It is the standard auditable measure of whether a large order was filled near the market's own volume-weighted price over the chosen period.
Limitations
Backward-looking and path-dependent, influenced by the very order being benchmarked, and comparable only when window, venue and price proxy are identical.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing a session VWAP with an anchored VWAP as the same line.
  • Averaging VWAP values across bars instead of recomputing from value and volume.
  • Benchmarking a multi-venue fill against one venue's VWAP.

Examples

institutional

A desk must buy a large position across one session. It slices the order to follow the volume profile and reports an average fill price against the session VWAP; a fill below it is recorded as positive execution performance.

numeric

Three bars with typical prices of 100, 102 and 101 and volumes of 1000, 3000 and 1000 give a VWAP of 101.4. The middle bar carries three fifths of the weight because that is where the size traded.

Given
  • bar typical prices: 100, 102, 101
  • bar volumes: 1000, 3000, 1000
  • window: one session, no reset inside it
Steps
  1. sum of price x volume = 100000 + 306000 + 101000 = 507000
  2. sum of volume = 5000
  3. VWAP = 507000 / 5000
VWAP = 101.4

The simple mean of the three typical prices is 101.0. The difference is the whole point: VWAP answers where the size traded, not where the bars sat.

Mathematical foundation

VWAP = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{m} p_i\,q_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{m} q_i}, \qquad \tilde{p}_j = \frac{H_j + L_j + C_j}{3}

Variables

p_i, q_iPrice and quantity of the i-th trade inside the windowquote currency; base units
mNumber of trades, or of bars in the approximationcount
\tilde{p}_jTypical price of bar j, used when trade data is unavailablequote currency
H_j, L_j, C_jHigh, low and close of bar jquote currency

Procedure

Fix the window and its reset rule. From the window's start, accumulate price times quantity and accumulate quantity, then divide the two running totals at every point. With bar data, substitute the typical price for each bar and the bar's volume for the quantity, and state that the result is an approximation.

Worked example

Given
  • typical prices 100, 102, 101
  • volumes 1000, 3000, 1000
Steps
  1. 100 x 1000 = 100000
  2. 102 x 3000 = 306000
  3. 101 x 1000 = 101000
  4. total value = 507000, total volume = 5000
VWAP = 101.4

Interpretation

The output is a single price: the volume-weighted centre of trading in the window. Comparing a fill price with it measures execution against the market's own activity, not against a forecast.

Assumptions

  • The window and its reset are declared. Session VWAP, anchored VWAP and rolling VWAP are different statistics and are not interchangeable.
  • Trade-level data is preferred; the typical-price approximation assumes volume was distributed evenly across each bar's range, which is false in detail.
  • One venue, one price convention. Opening and closing auction prints, and whether block trades are included, change the figure.
Limits of the formula
  • It is not an average of averages: VWAP values from sub-periods cannot be averaged, only recomputed from accumulated value and volume.
  • Late in a session the accumulated denominator is large, so the line barely responds to new trades.
  • As a benchmark it can be influenced by the order being measured, especially in a thin book.

Commonly confused with

Moving Average

A moving average weights by time over a rolling window. VWAP weights by volume and accumulates from a fixed anchor, so it has no fixed length and resets instead of rolling.
