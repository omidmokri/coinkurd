Simple definition

A jump with no trading in between: one period closes at one price and the next opens away from it, leaving a blank band on the chart.

Professional definition

A discontinuity between one interval's range and the next, so that no trades printed in the band between them. Classical charting sorts gaps into common, breakaway, runaway and exhaustion types by where in a move they appear, and treats an unfilled gap as an open reference level.

Technical definition

A gap is a property of session structure rather than of price itself: it appears when a venue is closed while information arrives, and the reopening auction clears at a new price. Continuously traded venues therefore gap only on halts, outages or thin-book jumps. The classical taxonomy is assigned after the move it claims to anticipate, and any gap-fill rate depends entirely on the time window allowed for the fill.

Academic definition

A discontinuity in the observed price path between consecutive trading intervals, arising where information accumulates while a venue is not open for continuous trading.

Mechanism

Between a close and the next open, orders accumulate but cannot execute. The reopening auction clears them at whatever price balances the built-up imbalance, so the jump measures the information that arrived while the book was shut. Nothing in that mechanism obliges price to come back: a gap is filled only if later supply and demand happen to trade through the band again.

In practice

Who uses it
Equity and futures traders working the session open, watchers of CME bitcoin futures across the weekend, and risk managers sizing overnight exposure.
Why it matters
It marks a band in which no trading occurred, so there are no reference prices inside it and the level structure there is thinner than elsewhere on the chart.
Limitations
The fill idea has no agreed horizon, the gap labels are applied with hindsight, and on continuously traded spot pairs gaps are rare enough that the concept carries much less weight.

Common mistakes

  • Calling a gap a breakaway gap only after the trend continued.
  • Quoting a fill rate without stating the time window allowed.
  • Expecting weekend gaps in a continuously traded spot pair.

Examples

market

CME bitcoin futures close for the weekend while spot keeps trading. If spot moves over Saturday, the futures chart shows a Monday gap that the spot chart does not. The same asset, two session structures, one gap.

numeric

Friday closes at 100 and Monday opens at 95 with nothing traded in between. The gap is five points, five per cent of the prior close, and stays open until price trades at 100 again — whenever that may be.

Given
  • Friday close = 100
  • Monday open = 95
  • no trades printed between 95 and 100
Steps
  1. gap size = 100 - 95 = 5
  2. gap as a share of the prior close = 5 / 100 = 5%
  3. the gap is filled only when a trade prints at 100 or above
A five-point downward gap, 5% of the prior close, open until price trades back through 100.

No source in this registry supports a fill probability or a typical fill time for a gap of any size, and none is claimed here.

Commonly confused with

Fair Value Gap

A gap is a real discontinuity with no trades inside it. A fair-value gap is a three-candle imbalance inside a continuous series, where trading did occur throughout.
