Foundation

Simple definition

The live list of all the buy and sell orders waiting at each price, with the highest bid and the lowest ask facing each other at the top.

Professional definition

A venue's record of resting limit orders, aggregated by price into two sides. Orders match by price and then by time of arrival, and a market order consumes the best levels first. What the book shows is intent that can be cancelled, not committed capital.

Technical definition

A limit order book holds price levels, each with an aggregate quantity and a time-ordered queue. Matching is price-time priority on most venues: a better price always executes first, equal prices in arrival order. Level 1 shows the best bid and ask with their sizes, level 2 aggregated depth per level, level 3 individual orders. Because a resting order is cancellable at any moment, reading displayed size as committed supply and demand overstates what is really there.

Academic definition

The centralised data structure recording outstanding limit orders by side and price level, from which quotes, depth and the execution outcome of incoming orders are determined under the venue's priority rules.

Mechanism

A limit order is an option granted free to the rest of the market: it offers a fixed price and can be withdrawn before anyone takes it. That asymmetry shapes the book, because liquidity suppliers post close to the quote while adverse selection is cheap and pull back when it is not. The visible shape is an equilibrium of what suppliers are willing to show, not a census of demand.

In practice

Who uses it
Execution traders, market makers, scalpers, and anyone sizing an order or building microstructure and market-making tooling.
Why it matters
It is where price is actually formed, and the only direct view of what a given order size would cost at this moment.
Limitations
It shows displayed orders only: iceberg and hidden size are invisible, displayed size can vanish in milliseconds, and aggregated feeds hide queue position.

Common mistakes

  • Reading a large resting order as a commitment.
  • Sizing a position from the chart instead of from visible depth.
  • Treating one venue's book as the whole market's liquidity.

Examples

market

A large bid sits two ticks under the market all morning and several traders size up because of it. The moment a sell order arrives the bid is cancelled, and the fill lands at levels that were hidden behind it.

numeric

Bids rest at 100.00 for 50 units and 99.95 for 120; asks at 100.10 for 40 and 100.15 for 200. The quote is 100.00 by 100.10, a spread of 0.10, and the top of book is 56 percent bid-side by size.

Given
  • bids: 50 at 100.00, 120 at 99.95
  • asks: 40 at 100.10, 200 at 100.15
Steps
  1. best bid = 100.00, best ask = 100.10
  2. spread = 0.10
  3. top-of-book imbalance = 50 / (50 + 40) = 0.556
Quote 100.00 / 100.10, top-of-book size imbalance 56 percent to the bid.

Imbalance is a snapshot of displayed size, which can be cancelled. One level deeper the book is 200 against 120 the other way.

Commonly confused with

Market Depth

The book is the data structure that holds the resting orders. Depth is a quantity read out of it: how much size sits within a chosen distance of the quote.
