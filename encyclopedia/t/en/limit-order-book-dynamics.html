Simple definition

How the list of resting buy and sell orders changes from moment to moment as orders arrive, get cancelled, and get filled — and what that churn does to the price.

Professional definition

The study of the order book as an evolving queue system. Three event types drive it: arrivals of new limit orders, cancellations, and market orders that consume depth. Spread, depth and resiliency are outputs of the relative rates of those three, not independent properties.

Technical definition

A modelling tradition treating the book as a multi-queue process with a precedence rule, typically price first and then time. Most events are cancellations, most depth never trades, and the queue at the best quote is where the economics sit: time priority makes queue position a real asset, since being early means a higher chance of filling without crossing the spread, at the cost of being filled exactly when the quote is about to move against you.

Academic definition

The analysis of limit order book evolution as a point process over arrivals, cancellations and executions under a stated precedence rule, from which spread, depth and resiliency are derived rather than assumed.

Mechanism

The book is a stack of options the queue has written. A resting limit order promises a price to whoever wants it most, so it fills when the other side is in a hurry — often because that side knows something. Makers manage this by keeping queue position cheap to abandon: cancellation is the main tool, which is why visible depth is a statement of intent, not a commitment.

In practice

Who uses it
Market makers, execution algorithm designers choosing between posting and taking, exchange engineers setting matching rules, and microstructure researchers.
Why it matters
Fill probability and adverse fill risk depend on queue position and event rates, so a passive strategy's economics cannot be read off the price chart at all.
Limitations
Snapshots of the visible book exclude hidden and iceberg quantity, and on fragmented venues the aggregate book is a reconstruction with its own latency errors.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming posted depth will still be there when your order arrives.
  • Modelling fills as certain at a touched price, ignoring queue position.
  • Fitting a book model on one venue's rules and reusing it elsewhere.

Examples

market

A venue switches the best-price queue from price-time priority to pro-rata allocation. Posted size at the touch grows sharply, because size now buys allocation, while the economic depth behind it barely changes. The same book looks deeper under a different rule.

numeric

You join the best bid with 500 lots resting ahead of you under price-time priority. Before the quote moves, 180 lots trade and 90 are cancelled ahead of you. Your remaining queue ahead is 230 lots — you have advanced without trading at all.

Given
  • queue ahead at entry = 500 lots
  • price-time priority
  • executed ahead = 180 lots
  • cancelled ahead = 90 lots
  • price has not moved
Steps
  1. cleared ahead = 180 + 90 = 270
  2. queue ahead = 500 - 270 = 230
230 lots remain ahead of you

Two thirds of the progress came from cancellations, not trades. A fill model that counts only executed volume would badly misestimate the wait.

Commonly confused with

Order Book

The order book is the object: the current list of resting orders. Limit order book dynamics is the study of how that object changes, which is where fill probability and resiliency come from.
