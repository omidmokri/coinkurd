Body V (Bodyway)

Level
Expert

Simple definition

A price target used in the Mokri method. You measure the bodies of the few candles just before a trendline breaks, then project that distance from the break point to the other side of the line.

Professional definition

A measured-move target built from candle bodies only. The aggregated body range of the candles immediately preceding the break candle, measured from the body edge to the broken trendline level, is projected from the break point in the break direction to give a single absolute target.

Technical definition

A projection rule within one practitioner's framework. Three conditions narrow it: wicks are excluded entirely; only the last few candles before the break candle are aggregated, not the whole preceding wave; and any part of that body range already consumed as the target of an earlier Body V is subtracted, so the same price territory is not counted twice.

Academic definition

A discretionary measured-move projection, defined on candle-body range rather than swing range, within a named practitioner's private trading framework.

Mechanism

The rule rests on treating bodies as the record of accepted price and wicks as rejected price. The compressed body range before a break is read as stored energy, and the break releases it symmetrically on the other side of the line. This is the framework's own reasoning, not an established market mechanism.

In practice

Who uses it
Followers of the Mokri method taught in the Coin_Kurd channel and the author's workshops.
Why it matters
It converts a trendline break into one specific numeric target instead of an open-ended expectation, which makes the trade plan testable.
Limitations
This is the author's own framework. It has not been independently validated or published in the peer-reviewed technical-analysis literature, and the choice of how many candles to aggregate is left to the analyst, so two analysts can derive two targets from the same chart.

Common mistakes

  • Including wicks in the measurement, which breaks the rule's own logic.
  • Measuring the whole preceding wave instead of the last few bodies.
  • Forgetting to subtract body range already used by an earlier Body V.

Examples

scenario

A descending resistance line is broken upward. The three candles before the break candle have bodies spanning 1,000 units down from the line. The target is the break level plus 1,000 units, projected above the line.

numeric

Break level 60,000. Lowest body edge of the aggregated candles 58,500. 400 of that range was already the target of an earlier Body V. Effective height is 1,100, so the target is 61,100.

Given
  • Break is upward
  • Trendline level at the break candle L = 60,000
  • Far body edge of the aggregated candles B = 58,500
  • Range already consumed by a previous Body V, C = 400
Steps
  1. |L - B| = 60,000 - 58,500 = 1,500
  2. Effective height = 1,500 - 400 = 1,100
  3. T = 60,000 + 1,100
Target T = 61,100

Units are whatever the chart is quoted in. The subtraction of C is what distinguishes this rule from an ordinary measured move.

Mathematical foundation

T = L \pm \Big( \big| L - B \big| - C \Big)

Procedure

Confirm the break is a body break, not a wick. Take the k candles immediately before the break candle and find the body edge furthest from the line on the pre-break side. Measure to the trendline level at the break candle. Subtract any range already used by an earlier Body V. Add the result above the break level for an upward break, or subtract it below for a downward break.

Worked example

Given
  • Upward break
  • L = 60,000
  • B = 58,500
  • C = 400
Steps
  1. |L - B| = 1,500
  2. 1,500 - 400 = 1,100
  3. T = 60,000 + 1,100
T = 61,100

Interpretation

The output is one price, not a zone and not a probability. Reaching it is a hypothesis to be tested against a record of outcomes, which is the only way to learn whether the rule holds on a given market and timeframe.

Assumptions

  • The trendline itself is valid under the method's own rules: at least one real touch between the two anchor points, and the line redrawn from each new high or low.
  • The break is confirmed by a candle body closing beyond the line, not by a wick.
  • k, the number of aggregated pre-break candles, is chosen by the analyst. The rule as taught does not fix it, so the target is partly discretionary.
  • This formalisation was written from the author's published teaching summary and should be confirmed against his workshop material before being treated as definitive.
Limits of the formula
  • A backtest of this rule must derive the trendline programmatically from pivot points. Feeding hand-drawn lines into a backtest imports the analyst's hindsight and will overstate the result.
  • Because k is discretionary, the same chart admits several valid targets, so hit-rate statistics are only meaningful once k and the pivot definition are both fixed in code.
  • No published out-of-sample study of this rule exists.

Commonly confused with

Fibonacci Retracement

Fibonacci levels are ratios of a swing and give a zone. Body V is an absolute distance taken from candle bodies and gives one price.
