Premium and Discount

Simple definition

Split a price range in half. Above the midpoint is called premium, below it discount. Buyers are taught to prefer the lower half and sellers the upper half.

Professional definition

A normalisation of position inside a chosen swing range. The midpoint between the range high and low is called equilibrium; price above it sits at a premium, below it at a discount. The whole content of the label is the choice of which high and which low define the range.

Technical definition

With range low L and high H, equilibrium is the arithmetic mean of the two and the normalised position of price P is (P - L) / (H - L), so premium is p above 0.5 and discount below it. The arithmetic is identical to a 50 percent retracement and to the range-position input of a stochastic oscillator. It is defined only while the range holds: a new extreme redefines H or L and so re-labels every past bar.

Academic definition

A min-max normalisation of price within a selected interval, with the midpoint used as a reference threshold separating the upper and lower halves of the range.

Mechanism

The arithmetic is trivially true, so any content has to come from the range selection. Buying the lower half of a range is a statement about reward relative to the range, not about probability: it improves the ratio of distance-to-target over distance-to-stop whenever the range holds, and loses exactly that advantage when the range breaks.

In practice

Who uses it
Price-action and smart-money traders choosing where inside a range to act, and developers normalising price position for a filter or a signal.
Why it matters
It turns expensive and cheap into one number between zero and one, which is comparable across instruments and codeable in a single line.
Limitations
The range endpoints are a discretionary choice and change retroactively on a new extreme. No peer-reviewed evidence supports the levels as thresholds.

Common mistakes

  • Redrawing the range after entry so the trade looks like a discount buy.
  • Applying it inside a trend with no defined range.
  • Mixing ranges from different timeframes.

Examples

market

A market trades between a swing low at 100 and a swing high at 140 for three weeks. Traders using the label wait for a return under 120 before buying and treat anything above 120 as the half they would rather sell into.

numeric

Range low 100, range high 140. Equilibrium is 120. Price at 132 gives a normalised position of 0.80, so it sits deep in premium. If price later prints 150, the range high becomes 150 and the same 132 becomes a discount at 0.64.

Given
  • range low L = 100
  • range high H = 140
  • price P = 132
  • later new high H = 150
Steps
  1. equilibrium = (100 + 140) / 2 = 120
  2. p = (132 - 100) / (140 - 100) = 32 / 40 = 0.80
  3. after the new high: p = (132 - 100) / (150 - 100) = 32 / 50 = 0.64
The same price is premium at 0.80 and then discount at 0.64.

The label is a function of the range, not of the price. Any backtest must fix the range rule in advance, because the range updates after the fact.

Procedure

State the swing rule and pick the range: usually the confirmed swing low and swing high that bound the current leg. Compute the midpoint as the mean of the two. For any price, subtract the range low and divide by the range width. Re-run the whole calculation whenever a new extreme extends the range.

Worked example

Given
  • L = 100
  • H = 140
  • P = 132
Steps
  1. E = (100 + 140) / 2 = 120
  2. H - L = 40
  3. P - L = 32
  4. p = 32 / 40
E = 120 and p = 0.80, so price is in premium.

Interpretation

The output says where price sits inside one chosen interval and nothing else. A reading of 0.80 is not a probability of a fall; it is a statement that four fifths of the range lies below the current price.

Assumptions

  • The range endpoints are chosen, not given. The choice of swing rule and of which leg to measure is the entire discretionary content of the method.
  • Equilibrium is the arithmetic midpoint. Some teachings add sub-zones such as the 0.705 to 0.79 band; those thresholds are conventions with no standard derivation.
  • The range is assumed to hold. The normalisation is undefined in a trend that keeps making new extremes, because H or L keeps moving.
Limits of the formula
  • Because H and L update, labels are revised retroactively. A past bar can change from premium to discount with no new information about that bar.
  • The arithmetic is the same as a 50 percent retracement and as the range position used by oscillators, so the label adds vocabulary rather than a new measurement.
  • A favourable normalised position improves reward relative to the range only on the condition that the range survives, which the measure itself cannot assess.

Commonly confused with

Fibonacci Retracement

The 50 percent retracement level and equilibrium are the same number computed the same way. Premium and discount only renames the two halves and attaches a buy-low sell-high instruction to them.

Flagged by the editor as uncertain on a linguistic, mathematical or regulatory point.

The midpoint arithmetic is elementary and the fifty percent retracement is classical and cited; the premium and discount vocabulary, the sub-zones some teachers add and the trading instruction attached to them belong to the retail smart-money tradition, which no source in this registry documents and for which no peer-reviewed evidence base exists. Range selection is discretionary and updates retroactively, which is the main practical hazard. Persian and Kurdish both keep the English terms, which is what practitioners say.
