Double Top and Double Bottom

Simple definition

Two peaks at a similar price with a dip between them, or two troughs at a similar price with a bounce between them.

Professional definition

A reversal shape built from two extremes at approximately the same level, separated by a counter-move. Confirmation is conventionally a close beyond the extreme between them, and the measured target is the height from the pair to that level projected from the break.

Technical definition

The pattern requires a tolerance on approximately the same level and a minimum separation between the two extremes. Without both, a detector finds a double top in almost any range, and those two parameters rather than the shape drive every count and every statistic. What is observable is that a second failure at a level leaves stops and resting orders stacked around it; the reversal claim is the contested part.

Academic definition

A reversal configuration formed by two local extrema at approximately equal price separated by a counter-move, confirmed by a breach of the intervening extremum.

Mechanism

A level that rejects price twice has absorbed two attempts, and each failure leaves participants who bought near it holding a loss. The move between the two extremes defines one level that those participants either defend or abandon together. Concentrated order flow at a single price is the observable part; that a trend must therefore reverse is not.

In practice

Who uses it
Chart analysts marking levels that have already failed twice, and detector authors who must set an equality tolerance.
Why it matters
It names a level with two documented rejections and supplies a single confirmation price between the two extremes.
Limitations
The equality tolerance and the minimum separation decide how many patterns exist, out-of-sample evidence is weak, and the measured target is geometry rather than an expectation.

Common mistakes

  • Calling any two nearby highs a double top with no tolerance rule.
  • Entering before the level between the extremes has been breached.
  • Quoting a hit rate measured with different parameters.

Examples

chart

Price tops at 120, pulls back to 100, rallies again and stalls at 119. Two failures at one band, one shared level at 100, and every stop above the highs now sits in the same place.

numeric

Peaks at 120 and 119 differ by 0.83 per cent. Under a two-per-cent equality tolerance they count as a pair; under a half-per-cent tolerance they do not, and the same chart contains no double top at all.

Given
  • peak 1 = 120
  • peak 2 = 119
  • intervening trough = 100
  • tolerance rule A: peaks within 2% count as equal
  • tolerance rule B: within 0.5%
Steps
  1. difference between peaks = 1 / 120 = 0.83%
  2. 0.83% <= 2%, so rule A accepts the pair
  3. 0.83% > 0.5%, so rule B rejects it
  4. pattern height = 120 - 100 = 20
  5. measured target on a break below 100 = 100 - 20 = 80
A double top with target 80 under rule A; no pattern at all under rule B.

The tolerance is the pattern. Quoting statistics without it describes nothing reproducible.

Commonly confused with

Support and Resistance

Support and resistance names a level that has held. A double top is a specific two-rejection shape plus a claim that the trend into it is ending.
