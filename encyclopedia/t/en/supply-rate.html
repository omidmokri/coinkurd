Supply Rate

ENGLISHSupply Ratereference
فارسینرخ تأمینRecommended equivalent

Category
Decentralised Finance
Level
Professional

Simple definition

What a depositor earns in a lending pool. It is the borrowers' interest shared out across everyone who supplied, which is why it is always below the borrow rate.

Professional definition

The return paid to suppliers of an asset, derived from the borrow rate scaled by utilisation and reduced by the protocol's reserve cut. Idle cash earns nothing, so the same interest is spread over the whole supplied balance rather than only the lent part.

Technical definition

Because the rate is the borrow rate times utilisation times one minus the reserve factor, it depends on the same single input as the borrow side and inherits all of its weaknesses. Two consequences matter. The spread between the two sides is not a credit premium but a mechanical dilution plus a protocol fee. And a high supply rate signals a heavily drawn pool, which is precisely when withdrawal may be constrained — the rate is highest when the exit is narrowest.

Academic definition

The supply-side return in a pooled lending market, equal to the borrow rate scaled by utilisation net of the protocol reserve factor, and therefore jointly determined with liquidity availability rather than independently of it.

Mechanism

Interest is collected only on the lent portion but owed to the whole supplied balance, so utilisation acts as a dilution factor. The protocol then keeps a slice as reserves. That makes the supply rate a residual: it rises with borrowing demand, falls with idle cash, and can be squeezed by a governance change to the reserve factor without the borrow rate moving at all.

In practice

Who uses it
Suppliers comparing on-chain returns, treasuries parking stablecoin balances, and yield aggregators routing between pools.
Why it matters
It is the base return on supplied capital before any token incentives, and the honest benchmark against which an advertised yield should be read.
Limitations
It is variable, uncapped and unguaranteed, it earns nothing if borrowing stops, and principal is exposed to contract, oracle and bad-debt risk.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing it with a bank deposit rate as though principal were protected.
  • Chasing the highest rate without checking how drawn the pool is.
  • Adding incentive tokens to the figure and still calling it interest.

Examples

numeric

At 80 percent utilisation with a borrow rate of 7 percent and a reserve factor of 10 percent, suppliers earn about 5.04 percent. The 1.96-point gap is dilution by idle cash plus the protocol's cut, not a credit premium.

Given
  • borrow rate r_b = 7%
  • utilisation U = 80%
  • reserve factor R = 10%
Steps
  1. gross interest to the supply side = 0.07 x 0.80 = 0.056
  2. net of reserves = 0.056 x (1 - 0.10) = 0.0504
  3. spread versus the borrow rate = 7.00% - 5.04% = 1.96 points
Supply rate 5.04% against a 7% borrow rate.

Reserve factors are governance parameters and differ by asset; 10% here is illustrative.

market

Two pools of the same asset quote 3 percent and 11 percent. The second is not paying more for the same thing: it is far closer to fully utilised, so the higher figure also describes a thinner exit.

Mathematical foundation

r_s = r_b \; U \; (1 - R)

Variables

r_sSupply rate paid to depositors, annualised% per year
r_bBorrow rate charged on outstanding debt% per year
UUtilisation of the poolfraction
RReserve factor: the share of interest the protocol retainsfraction

Procedure

Start from the borrow rate for that asset. Multiply by utilisation, because interest accrues only on the borrowed portion while being shared across the whole supplied balance. Then multiply by one minus the reserve factor to remove the protocol's retained share. The result is the annualised rate credited to suppliers.

Worked example

Given
  • r_b = 0.07
  • U = 0.80
  • R = 0.10
Steps
  1. r_b x U = 0.07 x 0.80 = 0.056
  2. 1 - R = 0.90
  3. r_s = 0.056 x 0.90 = 0.0504
Supply rate = 5.04% annualised.

Interpretation

The figure is what the pool is paying right now for capital that is at risk, with no term and no guarantee. Read next to utilisation it says two things at once: how much is being earned and how little of the pool is free to leave.

Assumptions

  • Interest accrues only on borrowed balances and is shared pro rata across all supplied balances. Protocols with tranches or isolated markets split this differently.
  • The reserve factor is a governance parameter set per asset; the 10% used here is illustrative.
  • Figures are annualised nominal rates. Per-block accrual compounds, so a quoted APY will exceed the simple annualised rate.
Limits of the formula
  • The identity gives the base interest only. Incentive emissions are a separate, token-denominated return and should never be added into this figure without saying so.
  • It says nothing about the probability of loss: the same 5% can sit on sound collateral or on parameters that are too loose.
  • Because both inputs move every block, a quoted rate is a snapshot and not an achievable forward return.
