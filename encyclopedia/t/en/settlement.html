Simple definition

The moment ownership and money actually change hands. The trade is agreed on one day; the asset and the cash often move later.

Professional definition

The completion of a trade: delivery of the asset against payment. Trade date and settlement date are different, and in between each side carries counterparty risk. Securities markets settle on a published cycle; on-chain transfers settle when the network treats the transaction as final.

Technical definition

Two distinct models. In traditional securities markets settlement runs through a central counterparty and a central securities depository on a published cycle, written T+n for n business days after the trade date, with netting, fail procedures and clearing-house margin in between. On-chain, settlement is the state transition itself and is probabilistic rather than contractual: the asset moves when the transaction is included and confirmed, with finality defined by the consensus rules. Cycles and finality definitions differ by market and by chain.

Academic definition

The discharge of a trade's obligations through transfer of the asset against payment, completing the legal and economic transfer of ownership.

Mechanism

A settlement cycle exists because matching trades and moving assets were historically separate operations, and netting many trades before moving anything is far cheaper than moving each one. The price is a window of counterparty risk, bridged by clearing-house margin. On a ledger the two operations are one event, so the window closes but the residual risk becomes technical rather than credit: reorganisation, not default.

In practice

Who uses it
Operations and back-office teams, custodians, anyone trading across a settlement boundary, and anyone moving crypto between venues.
Why it matters
Until settlement the position is a claim rather than an asset, so fails, recalls and chain reorganisations are live risks and the funds are not yet usable.
Limitations
Cycles are set by each market's own rules and differ by jurisdiction and instrument; on-chain finality differs by chain and is often probabilistic rather than absolute.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the trade date as the date funds are usable.
  • Assuming one confirmation is final settlement on every chain.
  • Planning a transfer between venues without allowing for settlement and crediting times.

Examples

market

Shares sold on Monday appear as a trade immediately, but the cash is available only after the market's settlement cycle has run. The seller has priced the risk and owns a receivable until then.

numeric

Twenty trades gross 2,600,000 of buys and 2,450,000 of sells. Netted, only 150,000 has to move — about three percent of the gross, which is why cycles with netting survived so long.

Given
  • gross buys 2,600,000 and gross sells 2,450,000 in one instrument on one settlement date
  • bilateral netting applied before delivery
  • fees excluded
Steps
  1. gross flow = 2,600,000 + 2,450,000 = 5,050,000
  2. net obligation = 2,600,000 - 2,450,000 = 150,000
  3. net as a share of gross = 150,000 / 5,050,000 = 0.0297
150,000 settles instead of 5,050,000 moving, about 2.97% of the gross

Illustrative figures. Real netting is multilateral through a central counterparty and the ratio depends entirely on the flow being netted.

Commonly confused with

Finality

Finality is the property that a transfer can no longer be reversed. Settlement is the act of transferring. In securities markets finality is contractual and dated; on a chain it is probabilistic and strengthens with each confirmation.
