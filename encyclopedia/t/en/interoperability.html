Simple definition

The ability of separate chains to move assets or messages between one another, instead of each staying a closed island.

Professional definition

Mechanisms for cross-chain value and message transfer: bridges that lock on one side and mint on the other, message-passing protocols, and light-client or proof-based verification of one chain inside another. Each design replaces one trust assumption with a different one rather than removing it.

Technical definition

No chain can natively verify another's state, so every crossing rests on some verifier: a multisignature set, an external validator committee, an optimistic challenge window, or an on-chain light client checking the counterparty's consensus. A bridged asset is a claim against that verifier, not the native asset, so the weakest verifier in a path sets the risk for everything held through it, and composed routes accumulate assumptions instead of averaging them.

Academic definition

The capacity of distinct ledgers to transfer value or messages across trust boundaries, mediated by verification mechanisms whose assumptions bound the security of every dependent claim.

Mechanism

A chain's security comes from its own validation, and that validation stops at its border. Any crossing therefore needs either a third party or a proof the destination chain can check itself. That is why bridges concentrate value and risk: they hold the collateral backing every claim they have ever issued.

In practice

Who uses it
Users of bridges and wrapped assets, protocol integrators, treasury and risk teams, and exchanges supporting several networks.
Why it matters
It is what lets capital and data move between ecosystems instead of being stranded on whichever chain they were issued on.
Limitations
Trust-minimised designs are young and costly, and a bridge failure can make a wrapped asset worthless while the native asset it referenced is untouched.

Common mistakes

  • Treating a wrapped token as identical to the native asset.
  • Not knowing which verifier secures a given bridge route.
  • Assuming an audit removes bridge custody risk.

Examples

crypto

A wrapped asset on a second chain is backed by holdings the bridge controls on the first. Its peg holds while the bridge's keys and code hold, which is a different question from whether the underlying chain is sound.

everyday

It works like correspondent banking: two systems that cannot read each other's books settle through an intermediary who holds balances on both sides. The intermediary's solvency becomes part of your risk.

Commonly confused with

Bridged Asset

Interoperability is the capability. A bridged asset is one output of exercising it: a token on one chain representing a claim held on another, and only as good as the mechanism holding it.
