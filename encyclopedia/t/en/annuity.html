Annuity

Simple definition

A series of equal payments made at regular intervals — a loan instalment plan or a fixed pension are both annuities.

Professional definition

A finite stream of identical cash flows at equal intervals. Its present and future values have closed forms, which is why instalment loans, leases and pension promises can be priced with a single expression instead of a cash-flow table.

Technical definition

A level stream valued as a geometric series. The ordinary form assumes end-of-period payments; the annuity-due form shifts every payment one period earlier and multiplies the value by (1+r). As n grows without bound and r stays positive, the present value converges to C/r, the perpetuity. The closed forms break down if payments vary or the rate is not constant.

Academic definition

A finite sequence of equal periodic cash flows whose present and future values admit closed-form expressions derived from the sum of a geometric series.

Mechanism

Each payment is discounted by one more factor than the one before, so the discounted payments form a geometric sequence and collapse to a short formula. That is also why later instalments contribute little: the final payment of a long loan is worth a small fraction of the first.

In practice

Who uses it
Lenders building instalment schedules, lessees, pension actuaries, and anyone pricing a fixed stream against a lump sum.
Why it matters
It converts a payment plan into one comparable number, which is the only way to judge whether an instalment offer is cheap.
Limitations
Requires a constant payment, a constant rate and fixed intervals; indexed, variable-rate or irregular streams need a full cash-flow model.

Common mistakes

  • Applying the ordinary formula to payments made at the start of each period.
  • Using an annual rate with monthly instalments.
  • Ignoring fees that sit outside the payment stream.

Examples

everyday

A shop offers a phone for 12 monthly payments of 100, or 1,050 in cash. The instalment plan totals 1,200 in name; whether it is actually worse depends on the rate used to compare the two.

numeric

Twelve monthly payments of 100 at 1% per month have a present value of 1,125.51. Against a cash price of 1,050 the plan is dearer; at 2% per month its present value falls to 1,057.53 and the gap nearly closes.

Given
  • C = 100 per month
  • n = 12 monthly payments, end of period
  • case A: r = 1% per month; case B: r = 2% per month
Steps
  1. A: 100 x (1 - 1.01^-12) / 0.01 = 1,125.51
  2. B: 100 x (1 - 1.02^-12) / 0.02 = 1,057.53
PV = 1,125.51 at 1%/month; 1,057.53 at 2%/month

Ordinary annuity assumed. If each payment were made at the start of the month, multiply each result by (1+r).

Mathematical foundation

PV = C \times \frac{1-(1+r)^{-n}}{r}, \qquad FV = C \times \frac{(1+r)^{n}-1}{r}, \qquad PV_{due} = PV \times (1+r)

Procedure

Match the rate to the payment interval, count the payments, then apply the present-value form for a value today or the future-value form for a value at the end. For start-of-period payments multiply by (1+r). For a perpetual stream use C/r.

Worked example

Given
  • C = 100
  • r = 1% per month
  • n = 12
Steps
  1. (1.01)^-12 = 0.887449
  2. (1 - 0.887449) / 0.01 = 11.2551
  3. PV = 100 x 11.2551
PV = 1,125.51

Interpretation

The factor 11.2551 is the price, in today's money, of one unit per period for twelve periods at this rate. It is an exchange rate between a stream and a lump sum, not a forecast of either.

Assumptions

  • Payments are identical, the interval is constant, and the rate is constant for the whole term.
  • The ordinary form places payments at the end of each period; the due form is the same series shifted one period and is explicitly separate.
  • r must be expressed per payment interval, not per year, whenever payments are not annual.
Limits of the formula
  • Gives no information about default: a promised stream and a paid stream are the same in this formula.
  • Indexed or floating payments, and insurance products sold under the same name with mortality features, are outside these closed forms.

Commonly confused with

Amortisation

An annuity is the level payment stream. Amortisation is how each of those payments splits into interest and principal over the life of a loan.
