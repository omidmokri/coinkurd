On-Balance Volume

ENGLISHOn-Balance Volumereference

Category
Technical Analysis
Level
Professional

Simple definition

A running total of volume that adds the whole bar's volume when price closes up and subtracts it when price closes down.

Professional definition

A cumulative signed-volume line. Each period's total volume is added if the close is above the previous close and subtracted if it is below; an unchanged close conventionally contributes nothing. Only the slope and any disagreement with price carry information, because the starting value is arbitrary.

Technical definition

OBV assigns an entire period's volume to one direction from the sign of a single close-to-close change, so a bar that closed one tick higher after a wide two-way session is credited with all of its volume as buying. The level of the line is meaningless: it depends on where the cumulation started. What is interpretable is the slope, and its disagreement with price, which is why OBV is read almost exclusively as a divergence tool.

Academic definition

A cumulative sum of period volume signed by the direction of the close-to-close price change, interpreted through its first difference rather than its level.

Mechanism

The construction assumes volume on an up-close was net buying and volume on a down-close was net selling. That is a simplification: every trade has a buyer and a seller, and the sign only records which side was more willing to move the price. Because a whole period's volume is signed by one number, flow that went the other way inside the period is lost.

In practice

Who uses it
Volume analysts, divergence traders, and developers who want a flow proxy with no parameters to tune.
Why it matters
It needs no lookback and no smoothing, so there is nothing to optimise, and it combines price direction with traded size in one line.
Limitations
The level is arbitrary, a whole bar's volume is signed by one close-to-close change, unchanged closes are handled inconsistently across platforms, and crypto volume is venue-specific.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing OBV levels between two instruments or two data histories.
  • Treating signed volume as measured buying pressure.
  • Reading venue volume as if it were market-wide.

Examples

market

Price grinds to a new high while the OBV line fails to exceed its own previous peak. The new high was made on less signed volume than the one before it — the disagreement that practitioners call volume divergence.

numeric

Starting from zero: an up-close on 1,000 units takes the line to 1,000; a down-close on 800 takes it to 200; an unchanged close on 500 leaves it at 200; an up-close on 600 lifts it to 800.

Given
  • OBV seeded at 0
  • bar 1: close up, volume 1000
  • bar 2: close down, volume 800
  • bar 3: close unchanged, volume 500
  • bar 4: close up, volume 600
Steps
  1. 0 + 1000 = 1000
  2. 1000 - 800 = 200
  3. 200 + 0 = 200
  4. 200 + 600 = 800
OBV = 800 after four bars.

Seeding at 0 was a choice. Seeding at the first bar's volume gives 1,800 for the same four bars, which is why only the shape of the line is read.

Mathematical foundation

OBV_t = OBV_{t-1} + \operatorname{sgn}(C_t - C_{t-1}) \times V_t, \qquad \operatorname{sgn}(x) = \begin{cases} +1 & x > 0 \\ 0 & x = 0 \\ -1 & x < 0 \end{cases}

Variables

OBV_tCumulative signed volume after period tvolume units
C_t, C_{t-1}Current and previous closing priceprice units
V_tTotal volume traded in period tvolume units

Procedure

Pick a starting value, conventionally zero. For each period compare the close with the previous close: add the full period volume if it is higher, subtract it if lower, and add nothing if equal. Carry the running total forward. Read the slope of the resulting line and compare it with price; never read its level.

Worked example

Given
  • OBV seeded at 0
  • up-close volume 1000, down-close volume 800, unchanged-close volume 500, up-close volume 600
Steps
  1. 0 + 1000 = 1000
  2. 1000 - 800 = 200
  3. 200 + 0 = 200
  4. 200 + 600 = 800
OBV = 800, from a seed that was chosen rather than measured.

Interpretation

A rising line says up-closes have carried more volume than down-closes since the cumulation began. The only readable content is direction and the comparison with price; the number itself has no units that mean anything across instruments.

Assumptions

  • The unchanged-close rule is a genuine source of disagreement: Granville's original adds nothing, while some platforms carry the previous direction forward and others add the full volume. The three produce different lines from the same data.
  • The starting value is arbitrary, so the level of the series is not a quantity. Only its changes are.
  • Volume must come from a consistent source. In crypto it is reported per venue and can be measured in base or quote units, so two data feeds can give two different OBV lines for the same pair.
Limits of the formula
  • A single tick of close-to-close change decides the sign of an entire period's volume, so the measure is coarse by construction.
  • The series is not normalised, so OBV values cannot be compared between instruments or across data histories of different length.
  • It is an inference about flow, not a measurement of it: order-flow data, not signed bar volume, measures which side was aggressive.

Commonly confused with

Money Flow Index

OBV signs a whole bar's volume by the close and cumulates without bound. The money flow index weights volume by typical price and maps the result onto 0 to 100 over a fixed lookback.
