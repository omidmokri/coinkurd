Simple definition

The money you must put up and keep on deposit to hold a borrowed or derivative position. If it falls too low you are asked to add more, or the position is closed.

Professional definition

Collateral posted against a position's potential loss. Initial margin is required to open it, maintenance margin is the minimum that must remain, and a shortfall triggers a call or a forced close. It is a credit-risk control for the venue, not a measure of the trader's own risk.

Technical definition

A requirement set by rule rather than by the holder: regulators impose initial requirements, clearing houses and venues set and revise maintenance rates, and both can be raised intraday during stress. Margin is computed on notional, so a position's requirement grows as the market moves against it at the same time as the equity available to meet it is shrinking.

Academic definition

Collateral held against counterparty performance on a leveraged or derivative position, specified as an initial requirement and a maintenance threshold below which the position is subject to a call or compulsory liquidation.

Mechanism

Margin exists to protect the party extending credit, so its logic runs from the lender's exposure, not the borrower's plan. The requirement is a function of notional and of assessed volatility, which means it tightens exactly when markets are disorderly. That timing is the mechanism behind margin-driven selling: calls arrive together, and meeting them forces sales into the same thin market that caused them.

In practice

Who uses it
Brokers and clearing houses setting requirements, risk teams monitoring utilisation, and every holder of a futures, perpetual or margin-equity position.
Why it matters
It is the binding constraint on how long a position can be held, so it determines survivability independently of whether the view is correct.
Limitations
Requirements are venue and jurisdiction specific, tiered by position size on many crypto venues, and subject to change without the holder's consent, including intraday.

Common mistakes

  • Planning a position around the initial requirement and ignoring the maintenance one.
  • Counting unrealised profit on one position as collateral for another.
  • Assuming requirements stay constant through a volatile week.

Examples

market

A venue raises maintenance rates for a contract ahead of an event. Positions that were comfortably collateralised the previous day are suddenly short of margin, and holders must either add collateral or reduce size, regardless of their view.

numeric

Under United States rules a 10,000 stock purchase may be opened with 5,000 of cash under Regulation T, leaving a 5,000 debit. At the FINRA minimum maintenance level of 25 percent, a fall to 6,667 brings equity to the threshold, a decline of about 33 percent.

Given
  • market value at purchase = 10,000 (account currency)
  • initial margin posted = 5,000, so the debit balance is 5,000
  • maintenance requirement = 25 percent of market value
  • no dividends, interest or fees
Steps
  1. equity at any market value MV = MV - 5,000
  2. requirement: MV - 5,000 >= 0.25 x MV
  3. 0.75 x MV >= 5,000
  4. MV >= 6,666.67
  5. decline from 10,000 to 6,666.67 = 33.3 percent
a fall of about 33 percent takes the account to the 25 percent maintenance threshold

These rates are United States equity-margin rules and are cited as an example of how the arithmetic works. Futures, crypto derivatives and other jurisdictions use entirely different requirements, and brokers may impose stricter house rules than the regulatory minimum.

Procedure

Multiply notional by the initial margin rate to get what must be posted to open, which is equivalently notional divided by the leverage. Track equity as collateral plus unrealised profit and loss, revalued at the venue's mark. Compare equity with the maintenance rate times current notional: below it, a call or an automatic reduction follows under the venue's rules.

Worked example

Given
  • N = 10,000 account currency at the mark
  • m_i = 0.10, so L = 10
  • m_m = 0.005
  • collateral C = 1,000, no unrealised profit or loss yet
Steps
  1. IM = 0.10 x 10,000 = 1,000, equal to 10,000 / 10
  2. maintenance requirement = 0.005 x 10,000 = 50
  3. equity = 1,000 + 0 = 1,000, which is well above 50
  4. after an unrealised loss of 950, equity = 50 and the maintenance threshold is reached
IM 1,000, maintenance requirement 50, and the position is at the threshold once unrealised loss reaches 950

Interpretation

Initial margin answers what it costs to open; maintenance margin answers how long it can be held. The gap between them is the whole buffer, and on a high-leverage position that buffer is a small fraction of the price move needed to be wrong.

Assumptions

  • Rates are constant. In practice venues tier maintenance rates by position size and revise them, including intraday during stress.
  • Notional is revalued at the venue's mark or index price, which can differ from the last traded price.
  • Isolated collateral for a single position. Under cross margin the whole account's equity and all positions enter one calculation.
  • Fees, funding payments and borrowing interest are excluded and all reduce equity against the same requirement.
Limits of the formula
  • The requirement is set by rule and can change without the holder's agreement, so it is not a parameter a trader controls.
  • United States equity margin follows Regulation T initial requirements and FINRA maintenance minimums, with brokers free to impose stricter house requirements; futures, crypto derivatives and other jurisdictions are governed by different regimes entirely.
  • Meeting a call by liquidating other holdings transmits the stress across a portfolio, which the single-position formula cannot show.

Commonly confused with

Liquidation

Margin is the requirement and the collateral held against it. Liquidation is what happens when the requirement is no longer met. One is a continuous condition, the other a discrete event.
