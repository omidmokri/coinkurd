Simple definition

Reading a project's own technical document to see whether what it says is specific, internally consistent, and matched by the code and the chain.

Professional definition

A verification exercise on a self-published document: separating testable statements from marketing, checking the token mechanics in the text against the deployed contract, and resolving every citation, named person and audit reference.

Technical definition

The document has no independent status. It is marketing material in which the issuer describes its own design, with no filing requirement, no auditor and no liability in most jurisdictions. Analysis therefore treats it as a source of claims to be tested elsewhere: supply and allocation figures against the contract, performance claims against measurable behaviour, cited work against the real literature, and the team section against public registers.

Academic definition

The structured appraisal of a self-published project document, evaluating the specificity, internal consistency and external verifiability of its technical and economic claims.

Mechanism

A claim can be written faster than it can be built, so a document and a system drift apart unless someone compares them. Every testable claim has a place it can be resolved: the contract, the explorer, the register, the cited paper. The analysis works by moving each claim to the place that settles it, and by recording the claims with no such place.

In practice

Who uses it
Diligence analysts, exchange listing and grant reviewers, technical journalists, and participants who want to know what a project has actually committed to in writing.
Why it matters
The document is where a project's claims are most explicit, so it is the cheapest place to find contradictions with the deployed system.
Limitations
A well-written document is not evidence of a working system, and a poor one is not evidence of fraud. The analysis can only report what the text claims and whether each claim resolves.

Common mistakes

  • Judging the project by the document's polish.
  • Accepting supply figures from the text without reading the contract.
  • Skipping the references section, where plagiarism and invented citations surface.

Examples

procedure

A verification pass over the document: list every numeric claim and resolve each against the contract or the chain; check the supply table sums to the stated total; open every cited reference; search distinctive sentences for prior publication; confirm each named author and advisor independently; note any guaranteed-return or no-risk language.

numeric

The document states a fixed maximum supply of 1,000 million tokens, and the allocation table lists 400, 300, 250 and 100 million — summing to 1,050. The deployed contract also exposes an owner-callable mint function, so the stated cap is not enforced in code.

Given
  • Stated maximum supply = 1,000 million
  • Allocation table: 400 + 300 + 250 + 100 million
  • Contract contains an owner-callable mint function with no cap
Steps
  1. Allocation sum = 400 + 300 + 250 + 100 = 1,050 million
  2. Excess over stated cap = 1,050 - 1,000 = 50 million, a 5% inconsistency
  3. Code check: no on-chain enforcement of the 1,000 million figure
An internal arithmetic contradiction of 5 per cent and a stated cap that the code does not enforce

Illustrative figures. The arithmetic error is a reading finding; the uncapped mint function is a code finding, and the second matters more.

Commonly confused with

Due Diligence

Whitepaper analysis examines one self-published document. Diligence is the wider process that also covers identity, registration, custody, distribution and whether funds can be withdrawn. The document is a source of claims, not the review itself.

The verification checklist is this encyclopedia's own assembly of steps, which is why the claim type is editorial-recommendation; the failure patterns it looks for are those documented by the cited regulators. Whitepapers carry no filing or liability requirement in most jurisdictions, but MiCA and some national regimes now impose disclosure duties on certain offers, so the legal status is jurisdiction-dependent.
