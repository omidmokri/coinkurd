Consumer Price Index

Level
Foundation

Simple definition

A measure of what a fixed basket of consumer goods and services costs over time. Its yearly change is the inflation number most news reports quote.

Professional definition

An index of the average price of a representative consumption basket, compiled monthly by a national statistical agency. Headline CPI covers the whole basket; core CPI excludes food and energy to show the slower-moving part. The year-over-year change is the inflation rate.

Technical definition

A Laspeyres-type price index built from a weighted basket whose weights come from household expenditure surveys and are revised periodically. Coverage, weights, seasonal adjustment and the treatment of housing differ by country, so CPI inflation is not directly comparable across jurisdictions. The Federal Reserve's 2 percent objective is defined on PCE inflation, not CPI; the two series use different weights and differ persistently.

Academic definition

A statistical index measuring change over time in the cost of a fixed or periodically re-weighted basket of consumption goods and services faced by households in a given economy.

Mechanism

Prices are collected for specified items, turned into price relatives and aggregated with expenditure weights. Because weights are held fixed between revisions, the index answers one narrow question: what the same pattern of spending costs now against then. Substitution, quality change and new goods are handled by adjustment rules, and that is where measurement disputes live.

In practice

Who uses it
Central banks, statistical agencies, bond and rate traders, wage negotiators, and anyone indexing a contract to inflation.
Why it matters
It anchors real returns: a nominal gain below CPI inflation is a loss of purchasing power, and rate expectations move on each release.
Limitations
Jurisdiction-specific and revised. Housing treatment, basket weights and seasonal adjustment all shift the number, and CPI is not the series the Federal Reserve targets.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing one country's CPI inflation with another's as if the baskets matched.
  • Treating CPI as the Federal Reserve's 2 percent target measure, which is PCE.
  • Reading a single monthly print as a change in trend.

Examples

everyday

A household whose spending looks nothing like the national basket — heavy rent, little travel — can face personal inflation well above or below the published CPI rate.

numeric

If the index reads 315.6 and read 304.0 twelve months earlier, year-over-year CPI inflation is about 3.8 percent. The level itself means nothing; only its change against a base period does.

Given
  • CPI index, current month = 315.6 (illustrative, not a published figure)
  • CPI index, same month one year earlier = 304.0
  • No seasonal adjustment applied
Steps
  1. 315.6 / 304.0 = 1.0382
  2. 1.0382 - 1 = 0.0382
  3. 0.0382 x 100 = 3.82
year-over-year CPI inflation of about 3.8 percent

Mathematical foundation

\pi_t = \left( \frac{CPI_t}{CPI_{t-12}} - 1 \right) \times 100

Variables

\pi_tYear-over-year CPI inflation rate in month tpercent per year
CPI_tIndex level in month tindex points
CPI_{t-12}Index level twelve months earlierindex points

Procedure

Divide the current index level by the level twelve months earlier, subtract one, and multiply by 100. The month-over-month rate replaces t-12 with t-1 and is usually computed on seasonally adjusted data, often annualised afterwards.

Worked example

Given
  • CPI_t = 315.6
  • CPI_{t-12} = 304.0
Steps
  1. 315.6 / 304.0 = 1.0382
  2. 1.0382 - 1 = 0.0382
  3. 0.0382 x 100 = 3.82
about 3.8 percent

Interpretation

The result is the percentage change in the cost of the basket over a year. It says nothing about which items moved, and nothing about where prices go next.

Assumptions

  • The same basket and weighting structure apply in both months; a re-weighting break makes the comparison approximate.
  • The year-over-year form removes most seasonality, so month-over-month comparisons need seasonally adjusted data instead.
  • The index level is relative to an arbitrary base period and carries no meaning on its own.
Limits of the formula
  • Basket, weights, housing treatment and adjustment rules are national choices, so the same formula produces series that are not comparable across jurisdictions.
  • Core CPI applies the same formula to a reduced basket; it is a different coverage, not a different kind of measure.

Commonly confused with

Interest Rates and the Federal Reserve

CPI is a price statistic; the Federal Reserve's 2 percent objective is stated on PCE inflation. A CPI print is information about the target, not the target itself.
