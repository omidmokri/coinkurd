Quantitative tightening

Simple definition

The reverse of quantitative easing: the central bank lets its bond holdings shrink, or sells them, so the money it created earlier drains back out.

Professional definition

A deliberate reduction of the central bank's securities portfolio, usually by not reinvesting maturing holdings — runoff — rather than by outright sales. Reserves fall as the portfolio shrinks, and the private sector absorbs the duration the central bank is no longer holding.

Technical definition

QT is a quantity operation running alongside, not instead of, the policy rate. Runoff caps make the pace passive and dependent on the maturity profile of the portfolio, so the drain is uneven over time. Because reserves are a bank liability of the central bank, QT interacts with the demand for reserves, and the point at which reserves stop being ample is not observable in advance — which is why money-market spreads are watched as a constraint on how far it can go.

Academic definition

A contractionary balance-sheet policy that reduces central-bank asset holdings and reserve liabilities, operating through term premia and reserve scarcity rather than through the policy rate.

Mechanism

When a holding matures and is not replaced, the issuer repays the central bank and reserves are extinguished; the private sector must then hold the new issuance it would otherwise not have bought. The intended effect is a higher term premium and tighter financial conditions. How large that effect is, and whether it is symmetric with easing, is disputed.

In practice

Who uses it
Central-bank balance-sheet desks, repo and money-market traders, bank treasurers, and macro investors tracking the supply of duration.
Why it matters
It changes the net supply of government duration the private sector must absorb, and the quantity of reserves in the banking system, both of which affect funding markets.
Limitations
The pace is set administratively and the transmission is weakly identified; estimates of the yield impact per unit of runoff vary widely and are not settled.

Common mistakes

  • Assuming QT is the mirror image of easing in size and speed.
  • Reading a shrinking balance sheet as an immediate equity signal.
  • Ignoring money-market spreads as the binding constraint.

Examples

numeric

A portfolio has 60 of holdings maturing in a month against a runoff cap of 40. Twenty is reinvested, so the balance sheet shrinks by 40, not 60: the cap, not the maturity schedule, sets the pace that month.

Given
  • maturing holdings this month = 60
  • monthly runoff cap = 40
  • no outright sales
Steps
  1. amount allowed to roll off = min(60, 40) = 40
  2. reinvested = 60 - 40 = 20
  3. balance-sheet reduction = 40
reserves drain by 40 this month; 20 is reinvested

Figures are invented. Actual caps, portfolio maturity profiles and reinvestment rules are set by each central bank and change over time.

market

Repo rates begin printing above the central bank's administered floor as reserves fall. That spread, not the stock of assets, is the practical signal that reserve scarcity is approaching.

Commonly confused with

Interest Rates and the Federal Reserve

Raising the policy rate changes the price of overnight money; QT changes the quantity of reserves and the duration the market must hold. They are separate instruments and can move at different speeds.

Editorial status

Needs expert reviewcontestedv1.02026-11-05

Flagged by the editor as uncertain on a linguistic, mathematical or regulatory point.

The transmission from balance-sheet shrinkage to risk-asset prices is genuinely contested: one reading treats reserve drain as a direct withdrawal of liquidity that weighs on risk assets, another holds that only the term-premium channel matters and that reserves held by banks are not a claim on equity markets. Neither is endorsed here, and no magnitude is quoted because this registry contains no estimate. Runoff caps, reinvestment rules and portfolio composition are jurisdiction-specific and change; all figures in the examples are invented. The Kurdish headword توندکردنی چەندایەتی is a proposal by this project, formed to mirror the existing ئاسانکاری چەندایەتی, and needs native-expert review.
