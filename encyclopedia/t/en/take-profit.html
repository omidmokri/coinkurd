Take-Profit

Category
Risk Management
Level
Foundation

Simple definition

A pre-set exit that closes a winning position once price reaches a level you chose in advance, turning a gain on screen into a gain in the account.

Professional definition

A resting order, usually a limit, placed in the profitable direction of an open position so the exit happens at a planned level rather than in a chosen mood. It fills only if price trades there, so it guarantees a price, not an exit.

Technical definition

The target leg of a planned trade. As a limit order it guarantees the price or better but not execution, and it can be partially filled when depth at the level is thin. Its distance from entry, measured against the stop distance, fixes the trade's reward-to-risk ratio and therefore the win rate the strategy must clear to break even; moving the target moves both.

Academic definition

A pre-committed liquidation level in the favourable direction of an open position, specified ex ante so that realisation of gains follows a rule rather than discretion.

Mechanism

A target converts a floating gain into cash at a chosen price, in exchange for giving up whatever the move does afterwards. Because it is a limit order, the queue matters: price must trade through the level, or leave enough size resting there, for the whole quantity to fill. A target that price never reaches offers no protection at all.

In practice

Who uses it
Traders with rule-based exits, systematic strategy developers, and desks that scale out of a position in tranches.
Why it matters
It removes the decision to sell from the moment of greatest emotional pressure and makes the reward side of the trade measurable in advance.
Limitations
It is a price guarantee, not an exit guarantee. Unreached targets, partial fills and a trend that runs far past the level are all normal outcomes.

Common mistakes

  • Moving the target further away while price approaches it.
  • Setting a target no realistic move in that timeframe reaches.
  • Comparing results across trades whose targets were set inconsistently.

Examples

market

A target is set at a prior swing high where sellers were last active. Price stalls two ticks short of it, reverses, and the stop closes the trade. The target was a good read of the level and still produced no fill.

numeric

Fifty units are long from 100 with a target at 112 and a stop at 96. Only 30 units fill at the target before price turns and the rest exit at the stop: 360 gained against 80 lost, so 280 instead of the 600 the plan assumed.

Given
  • Q = 50 units long
  • E = 100
  • T = 112
  • S = 96
  • 30 units filled at T, 20 units exited at S
Steps
  1. filled leg = 30 x (112 - 100) = 30 x 12 = 360
  2. stopped leg = 20 x (96 - 100) = 20 x (-4) = -80
  3. net = 360 - 80 = 280
  4. full-fill plan was 50 x 12 = 600
realised 280, against a planned 600

Partial fills are the normal case at a level with thin resting size. Fees are excluded here and would reduce both legs.

Mathematical foundation

G_{\text{target}} = Q\,(T - E)\ \text{(long)}, \qquad G_{\text{target}} = Q\,(E - T)\ \text{(short)}

Procedure

Take the distance from entry to target in price units, in the direction the position profits, and multiply by size. For a partial fill, apply the same product to the quantity actually filled and treat the remainder as a separate exit at whatever price it leaves.

Worked example

Given
  • Q = 50 units
  • E = 100
  • T = 112
Steps
  1. T - E = 112 - 100 = 12
  2. G = 50 x 12
G_target = 600 in account currency

Interpretation

The output is the gain the trade is designed to produce if the target is reached and fully filled. It is a plan, not an expectation: the expected value of the trade also depends on how often the target is reached.

Assumptions

  • The entire quantity fills at T. Partial fills at a thin level are common and must be accounted separately.
  • Fees, funding and any currency conversion are excluded.
  • A linear instrument quoted in the account currency.
Limits of the formula
  • The formula says nothing about the probability of reaching T, which is where the expected value of the trade actually lives.
  • On a limit target, price trading exactly at T does not guarantee a fill; queue position decides.

Commonly confused with

Order Types

Take-profit names the purpose of an exit; order type names the mechanism. The same target can be worked as a limit, a stop-market or a reduce-only order, with different guarantees.
