Token Velocity

Simple definition

How many times the same token changes hands in a period. A token used once a year and one used weekly need very different supply to settle the same value.

Professional definition

Transaction value settled over a period divided by the average value of the token supply in the same period. Borrowed from the monetary identity in which money supply times velocity equals the value of transactions, and used in token-valuation arguments to argue that high velocity suppresses price.

Technical definition

There is no agreed construction. Numerator candidates include raw on-chain transfer value, entity-adjusted transfer value and exchange trading volume; denominator candidates include circulating market capitalisation, free-float supply and total supply. The underlying identity is a definition rather than a behavioural law, so velocity is whatever the chosen numerator divided by the chosen denominator happens to be, and it cannot by itself establish a causal claim about price.

Academic definition

The ratio of transaction value settled in a token over an interval to the average market value of its supply, an application of the monetary velocity identity to cryptoasset networks.

Mechanism

The identity is arithmetic: if a network settles a given value and the supply is small, each unit must have turned over many times. Interpreting that as a price mechanism adds an assumption the identity does not contain — that settled value is exogenous. Where holders are also speculators, causation can run from price to velocity instead, and the identity cannot distinguish the two.

In practice

Who uses it
Token-economics researchers, protocol designers arguing for fee capture or locking mechanisms, and analysts comparing a token's turnover with its own history.
Why it matters
It makes explicit that a medium-of-exchange token needs demand to hold it, not just demand to use it, which is a real design constraint.
Limitations
The construction is unsettled, both inputs are contaminated by exchange and internal transfers, and the identity supports no causal inference on its own.

Common mistakes

  • Quoting a velocity figure without naming numerator and denominator.
  • Treating the monetary identity as a pricing model.
  • Comparing velocity across networks with different settlement architectures.

Examples

numeric

A network settles 180,000 units of value in a year on an average supply value of 45,000, so velocity is 4. If the same value were settled on a supply worth 90,000, velocity would be 2 and each unit would have to be held twice as long.

Given
  • Annual settled transaction value = 180,000 illustrative units
  • Average market value of the circulating supply = 45,000
  • Alternative case: average supply value = 90,000
Steps
  1. Velocity = 180,000 / 45,000 = 4
  2. Alternative velocity = 180,000 / 90,000 = 2
  3. Average holding period = 1 / velocity, so 0.25 years against 0.5 years
Velocity 4, implying an average holding period of about three months

Illustrative units, no as-of date. The relationship is an identity; it does not show that lower velocity caused the higher supply value.

case

A protocol adds a staking lock that removes part of the supply from circulation. Measured velocity rises because the denominator chosen was free-float supply, and the same change would have lowered velocity had total supply been used instead.

Mathematical foundation

V = \frac{TV_{period}}{\overline{M}}, \qquad \overline{M} \times V = TV_{period}

Variables

TV_{period}Value of transactions settled in the token over the period, on a stated basiscurrency per period
\overline{M}Average market value of the token supply over the period, on a stated supply definitioncurrency
VVelocity: turnovers of the supply per periodtimes per period

Procedure

State which transaction-value series and which supply definition you are using, measure both over the identical window and currency, and divide. Report the inverse as well, because one over velocity is the implied average holding period and is easier to sanity-check.

Worked example

Given
  • TV over the year = 180,000 illustrative units
  • Average supply market value = 45,000
Steps
  1. V = 180,000 / 45,000 = 4
  2. Implied average holding period = 1 / 4 = 0.25 years
V = 4 turnovers per year, about a three-month average holding period

Interpretation

A higher figure means the supply changed hands more often, so less value was being held at any moment. It is a description of turnover, not a forecast, and it carries no implication about what the token should be worth.

Assumptions

  • No agreed construction exists. Numerator options include raw transfer value, entity-adjusted transfer value and exchange volume; denominator options include circulating, free-float and total supply, and each pairing gives a different number.
  • The relation supply times velocity equals transaction value is an identity by definition, not an empirical law, so it cannot be tested or used to infer causation.
  • Both inputs include transfers with no economic counterparty, so the measured figure overstates genuine turnover by an unknown amount.
Limits of the formula
  • Not comparable across networks: layer-two settlement, batching and custodial trading move value without on-chain transfers, and the extent differs by chain.
  • Often invoked to argue that high velocity caps a token's price. The identity alone cannot support that claim, and no source in this registry establishes it empirically.

Commonly confused with

Network Value to Transactions

The two are near reciprocals built from the same raw inputs: one divides value by transfers, the other transfers by value. They are not independent evidence, so quoting both as confirmation is double-counting.
