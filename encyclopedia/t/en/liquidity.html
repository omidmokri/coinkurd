Liquidity

Simple definition

How easily you can buy or sell something without moving its price. Cash is the most liquid thing you own; a small token on a quiet exchange is among the least.

Professional definition

The capacity of a market to absorb order flow at prices close to the prevailing quote. Measured by bid-ask spread, visible depth of the order book, and the price impact of a given order size — not by turnover alone.

Technical definition

A multi-dimensional property with three largely separable components: tightness, the cost of an immediate round trip, which is the spread; depth, the quantity available at or near the quote; and resiliency, how fast depth refills after a shock. High turnover on thin depth is not liquidity, it is churn.

Academic definition

The degree to which an asset can be transacted in size, at short notice, without material concession from its prevailing market price.

Mechanism

Liquidity is supplied by participants willing to quote both sides and carry inventory. The spread pays them for that service, and they withdraw when the risk of being adversely selected rises — which is why liquidity thins out exactly when volatility spikes and traders need it most.

In practice

Who uses it
Market makers, execution desks, treasury teams, and any trader sizing an order against a visible book.
Why it matters
It sets the gap between the price you see and the price you get, and therefore the real cost of every entry and exit.
Limitations
Visible depth understates hidden liquidity and overstates committed liquidity: quotes can be pulled in milliseconds.

Common mistakes

  • Reading high 24-hour volume as a guarantee of a clean exit.
  • Sizing a position from the chart rather than from order-book depth.
  • Assuming a weekend or holiday book behaves like a weekday book.

Examples

everyday

A flat in a popular district sells near its asking price within weeks. A remote plot of land may need a year and a discount. Same value on paper, very different liquidity.

crypto

A $200,000 market order in a major BTC pair may move the price a few basis points. The same order in a small-cap token can move it tens of percent, and the exit can cost more than the entry.

Commonly confused with

Trading Volume

Volume says how much changed hands over a period. Liquidity says at what cost you could change hands right now.
