Current Ratio

ENGLISHCurrent Ratioreference
فارسینسبت جاریEstablished equivalent
کوردیڕێژەی جاریProposed equivalent — not standard
Category
Fundamental Analysis
Level
Foundation

Simple definition

Short-term assets divided by short-term debts. Above 1 means the company has more due to it within a year than it owes within a year, at least on paper.

Professional definition

Current assets over current liabilities, both as classified in the balance sheet. A crude test of whether obligations falling due within the operating cycle can be met from assets expected to convert to cash in the same window.

Technical definition

A static ratio of two classified totals at one date, so it says nothing about the timing inside the year or the quality of what it counts. Inventory and prepayments sit in the numerator although neither is cash, which is why the quick ratio excludes them. A figure well above one can mean idle working capital rather than safety, and a figure below one is normal in businesses that collect from customers before paying suppliers.

Academic definition

The ratio of current assets to current liabilities at a reporting date, used as a static indicator of short-term solvency subject to the classification conventions of the reporting framework.

Mechanism

The ratio works by sorting the balance sheet into what moves within a year and what does not, then comparing the two piles. Its weakness is in that sorting: a receivable from a failing customer and cash in the bank are counted alike, and a liability due next week is counted the same as one due in eleven months.

In practice

Who uses it
Lenders and trade creditors, covenant monitors, auditors assessing going concern, and working-capital managers.
Why it matters
It is computable from any balance sheet in seconds and gives a first signal of whether near-term obligations are covered by near-term assets.
Limitations
It is a snapshot at one date, treats unequal assets as equal, and can be dressed up at the reporting date by delaying payments or drawing a facility.

Common mistakes

  • Applying a single rule-of-thumb threshold across different industries.
  • Counting slow-moving inventory as near-cash.
  • Reading a high ratio as strength when it reflects uncollected receivables.

Examples

numeric

Current assets of 600 against current liabilities of 400 give a current ratio of 1.5. Strip out 250 of inventory and the quick ratio is 0.875, so the apparent cushion depends almost entirely on selling stock.

Given
  • Current assets = 600, of which inventory = 250
  • Current liabilities = 400
Steps
  1. Current ratio = 600 / 400 = 1.5
  2. Quick assets = 600 - 250 = 350
  3. Quick ratio = 350 / 400 = 0.875
Current ratio 1.5; quick ratio 0.875

The same balance sheet looks comfortable on one measure and tight on the other.

market

A supermarket chain operates for years with a current ratio below one and no distress, because customers pay at the till while suppliers are paid weeks later. The same ratio in a shipbuilder would be read as a warning.

Mathematical foundation

CR = \frac{CA}{CL}, \qquad QR = \frac{CA - Inv - Prepaid}{CL}

Procedure

Take the current assets and current liabilities subtotals from the balance sheet at the same date and divide. Then recompute excluding inventory and prepayments to obtain the quick ratio, and compare both with the same company's own history and with firms on the same operating cycle.

Worked example

Given
  • Current assets = 600
  • Inventory = 250
  • Current liabilities = 400
Steps
  1. 600 / 400 = 1.5
  2. (600 - 250) / 400 = 350 / 400 = 0.875
Current ratio 1.5; quick ratio 0.875

Interpretation

A value above one means recorded short-term assets exceed recorded short-term obligations at that date. It is a coverage statement about balance-sheet classifications, not a statement about whether cash will actually arrive before the payments fall due.

Assumptions

  • Current and non-current classification follows the reporting framework and the entity's stated operating cycle, which can exceed twelve months in some industries.
  • The quick ratio variant, and whether prepayments are excluded as well as inventory, are conventions that must be stated with the figure.
  • Both totals are measured at one date, so the ratio assumes nothing material happens between the reporting date and settlement.
Limits of the formula
  • Window-dressable: paying down payables or drawing a revolving facility just before the reporting date changes the ratio without changing liquidity.
  • No universal threshold exists. A ratio that signals distress in one operating cycle is routine in another, so comparison must be within an industry.

Commonly confused with

Liquidity

Market liquidity is how easily an asset can be sold without moving its price. The current ratio is an accounting coverage test on a balance sheet. Both are called liquidity and they measure unrelated things.
