Simple definition

A hot wallet keeps its keys on a device that is online, so it is convenient and more exposed. A cold wallet keeps them on a device that never goes online.

Professional definition

A classification by key exposure, not by brand. Hot keys sit in software on an internet-connected machine and can sign at any moment. Cold keys sit on dedicated or air-gapped hardware; a transaction is built online, signed offline, and only the signed result is broadcast.

Technical definition

The distinction is where the signing operation happens and what else runs on that machine. Cold storage reduces the attack surface to physical access and to the data crossing the gap, which is why signed-payload review matters: an air gap does not stop a user approving a malicious payload shown on a compromised screen. Multi-signature and threshold setups are orthogonal to it and address a different failure, namely a single point of compromise or loss.

Academic definition

A taxonomy of custody arrangements by the network exposure of the signing environment, trading transaction latency against the size of the attack surface.

Mechanism

Risk here is a product of exposure and time. A key that can sign on demand must live where code runs, and any code on that machine is a candidate attacker. Moving the key off the network removes that whole class of threat and replaces it with slower operations, physical custody problems, and dependence on the user actually reading what the device asks them to sign.

In practice

Who uses it
Long-term holders, exchange and fund operations teams, DAO treasuries, and anyone whose balance would hurt to lose.
Why it matters
It lets a holder keep working capital available while putting the bulk of the balance beyond reach of any online compromise.
Limitations
Cold storage protects against remote attackers, not against coercion, loss of the backup, a counterfeit device, or an approval the user granted themselves.

Common mistakes

  • Keeping an operational float and long-term savings on the same keys.
  • Buying a signing device second-hand or from an unofficial reseller.
  • Approving a signature request without checking the address on the device screen.

Examples

numeric

A desk keeps 2% of its balance hot for daily settlement and 98% cold. A full compromise of the hot machine costs 2% of the balance, not the desk. The split is a policy choice, not a safe number.

Given
  • Total balance = 100 units
  • Hot allocation = 2 units
  • Cold allocation = 98 units
  • Scenario: hot device fully compromised
Steps
  1. Loss = hot allocation = 2 units
  2. Loss as a share of total = 2 / 100
Maximum remote loss limited to 2% of the balance

The ceiling holds only if the two sets of keys are genuinely independent. A cold key backed up on the hot machine gives a 100% loss, whatever the split on paper.

everyday

A shop keeps a small till for the day's trade and the rest in a safe. Neither choice is wrong; keeping the whole month's takings in the till is.
