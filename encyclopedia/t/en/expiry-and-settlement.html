Expiry and Settlement

Foundation

Simple definition

The date a dated contract stops trading, and the process that closes it out at an official final price. After that the contract no longer exists.

Professional definition

The end of a contract's life: a last trading day, a final settlement price fixed by a published rule, and either cash settlement against that price or delivery of the underlying. Open positions are closed by the venue at the settlement price whether or not the holder acts.

Technical definition

Two separate events that are often conflated. Trading ceases at the last trading time; final settlement happens afterwards against a settlement price determined by a stated procedure — frequently an average or volume-weighted window rather than a last trade, specifically so the print is hard to manipulate. Between the two, positions may be unhedgeable but still marked and margined. Dates, windows and delivery procedures are set per product by the venue.

Academic definition

The terminal phase of a dated derivative contract, comprising the cessation of trading and the determination of a final settlement price under a specified procedure, against which remaining open positions are discharged.

Mechanism

Expiry is what forces a futures price to meet spot: on the final day the contract pays against the same reference the spot market trades on, so any remaining gap is a free arbitrage. That is why basis decays toward zero on a schedule rather than at random, and why a perpetual contract, having no expiry, needs funding to do the same job.

In practice

Who uses it
Hedgers who must match a real exposure date, spread and calendar traders, and anyone holding a dated contract into its final week.
Why it matters
Knowing the last trading day and the settlement procedure is what lets a position be rolled, closed or delivered deliberately rather than by default.
Limitations
Liquidity migrates to the next contract before expiry, so the final days can be thin; the settlement window itself may trade differently from the rest of the session.

Common mistakes

  • Holding a physically settled contract past the last trading day by accident.
  • Assuming the settlement price is the last traded price.
  • Rolling on expiry day instead of when liquidity has already moved.

Examples

market

A hedger needs cover until the end of a quarter but the listed contract expires mid-month. Either the hedge ends early or the next contract is bought at a different price — the gap between the two is a cost the calendar created, not the market.

numeric

A trader is long three contracts of 5 units each, entered at 2,950, and holds to final settlement at 3,010. The settled gain is 900: no exit trade is needed, because the venue discharges the position at the official price.

Given
  • n = 3 contracts
  • Q_c = 5 units per contract
  • Entry price = 2,950
  • Final settlement price = 3,010
Steps
  1. Price difference = 3,010 - 2,950 = 60
  2. Units = 3 x 5 = 15
  3. Settled PnL = 60 x 15 = 900
Settled gain of 900, realised without an exit trade

Only true for a cash-settled contract. A physically settled one would oblige delivery or receipt of 15 units instead.

Commonly confused with

Settlement

Settlement in the general sense is how any trade is completed, asset against payment. Expiry and settlement is the end of a dated contract's whole life, with a final price fixed by rule and open positions discharged.
