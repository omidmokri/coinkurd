Fear and Greed Index

Foundation

Simple definition

A 0 to 100 sentiment gauge built by a data vendor. Low readings are labelled fear, high readings greed. It describes mood, not whether an asset is cheap or expensive.

Professional definition

A composite sentiment index assembled by a vendor from inputs such as volatility, price momentum, social-media activity, market dominance and survey responses, each scored and combined into a single 0 to 100 figure with named bands.

Technical definition

A vendor-defined composite: several heterogeneous series are normalised, weighted and summed onto a bounded scale. The input list and the weights are editorial choices by the publisher and have been changed over time, so the series is not a stable measurement and historical readings are not strictly comparable with current ones. It carries no valuation content and no forecasting claim; it summarises conditions already visible in price, volatility and chatter.

Academic definition

A bounded composite indicator of investor sentiment constructed by a commercial publisher from weighted market and social-activity proxies, with weights set by editorial judgement rather than estimation.

Mechanism

The index is a weighted summary of things already observable, so it lags or coincides with price rather than leading it. Its appeal is compression: one number instead of five noisy series. Its weakness is the same compression, because the weighting that produced the number is a vendor's judgement and is not independently estimated.

In practice

Who uses it
Retail traders watching sentiment, commentators, and analysts cross-checking their own positioning against the crowd's mood.
Why it matters
It gives a quick, shared reference point for how one-sided sentiment has become, which is useful context when a market feels crowded.
Limitations
It is sentiment, not valuation. Weights are the vendor's choice and have changed, bands are labels rather than thresholds, and extremes can persist for weeks.

Common mistakes

  • Buying only because the gauge reads extreme fear.
  • Treating the bands as statistically validated thresholds.
  • Comparing today's reading with an old one after a methodology change.

Examples

market

A gauge can sit in extreme greed through an entire advance. Traders who sold the first greed reading missed the move; the reading described how the crowd felt, not what price would do.

numeric

With five inputs scored 20, 30, 45, 10 and 15 under equal weights, the composite is 24 — labelled fear. Change one weight to 0.5 on the first input and the same day reads 22.5.

Given
  • five illustrative sub-scores: 20, 30, 45, 10, 15
  • case A: equal weights of 0.2 each
  • case B: weights 0.5, 0.125, 0.125, 0.125, 0.125
Steps
  1. case A: (20 + 30 + 45 + 10 + 15) / 5 = 120 / 5 = 24
  2. case B: 0.5 x 20 = 10; 0.125 x (30 + 45 + 10 + 15) = 0.125 x 100 = 12.5
  3. case B total = 10 + 12.5 = 22.5
24 under equal weights, 22.5 under the reweighted scheme

Invented sub-scores used only to show that the published number depends on weights the vendor chooses. The entry states no vendor's actual weights because none is documented in this registry.

Commonly confused with

MVRV

A sentiment composite scores mood from volatility, momentum and chatter. MVRV compares market value with an on-chain cost basis. One is a mood summary, the other a valuation ratio.

That the weights are editorial and have been revised is stated as a property of vendor composites generally.
