Category
Technical Analysis
Level
Foundation

Simple definition

How much time each candle on a chart covers — a minute, an hour, a day. It decides how much detail you see and how much of it is noise.

Professional definition

The sampling interval of a price series. It fixes what counts as one bar, and therefore what counts as a high, a low, a trend, a pattern or a signal. No indicator reading or backtest statistic means anything except with respect to one stated interval.

Technical definition

Aggregation of a finer series: the open of the first sub-bar, the maximum of the highs, the minimum of the lows, the close of the last, volumes summed. The operation is lossy and not invertible, and it is not neutral — shifting the session boundary by an hour yields a different set of highs and lows from identical ticks. Multi-timeframe analysis therefore compares two samplings of one process, not two independent pieces of evidence.

Academic definition

The fixed interval by which a continuous transaction stream is discretised into bars, and relative to which every derived statistic is defined.

Mechanism

A longer interval averages more trades into each bar, so idiosyncratic ticks cancel and what survives is the variation shared by many participants. A shorter interval keeps that detail, including the part that reverses at once. Nothing is free: the same averaging that removes noise also delays every reading derived from the bars.

In practice

Who uses it
Everyone who reads a chart; strategy developers choosing a bar size; risk teams matching holding period to sampling interval.
Why it matters
Matching the interval to the intended holding period keeps the signal, the stop distance and the expected trade duration on one consistent scale.
Limitations
No interval is the correct one. Scanning several and keeping whichever worked is overfitting, and a 24-hour crypto clock makes the daily boundary venue-dependent.

Common mistakes

  • Picking the timeframe after seeing which one made the backtest profitable.
  • Comparing indicator levels across intervals as if one scale.
  • Assuming the daily candle closes at the same hour on every venue.

Examples

chart

One sharp afternoon fall is twelve red candles and a clear downtrend on a five-minute chart, and a single candle with a long lower wick on the daily. Both are correct descriptions of the same trading.

numeric

Four quarter-hour bars make one hourly bar. Its open is the first open, its high the largest high, its low the smallest low, its close the last close, and its volume the sum of the four.

Given
  • bar 1: O 100, H 103, L 99, C 102, V 500
  • bar 2: O 102, H 106, L 101, C 105, V 700
  • bar 3: O 105, H 105, L 100, C 101, V 900
  • bar 4: O 101, H 104, L 100, C 103, V 400
Steps
  1. O = 100 (first open)
  2. H = max(103, 106, 105, 104) = 106
  3. L = min(99, 101, 100, 100) = 99
  4. C = 103 (last close)
  5. V = 500 + 700 + 900 + 400 = 2500
hourly bar: O 100, H 106, L 99, C 103, V 2500

Volume adds, but an average price does not: VWAP must be recomputed from price times volume, never averaged across bars.

Mathematical foundation

O_T = o_1, \quad H_T = \max_{1 \le i \le n} h_i, \quad L_T = \min_{1 \le i \le n} l_i, \quad C_T = c_n, \quad V_T = \sum_{i=1}^{n} v_i

Variables

o_i, h_i, l_i, c_i, v_iOpen, high, low, close and volume of the i-th sub-barprice units; volume units
nNumber of sub-bars inside the longer intervalcount
O_T, H_T, L_T, C_T, V_TThe aggregated bar on the longer timeframeprice units; volume units

Procedure

Group the sub-bars by the boundaries of the target interval, in a declared timezone. Take the first open, the extreme high and low, the last close, and the sum of volumes. Never average opens or closes, and never aggregate across a gap as though it were continuous trading.

Worked example

Given
  • four 15-minute bars with highs 103, 106, 105, 104 and lows 99, 101, 100, 100
  • first open 100, last close 103
  • volumes 500, 700, 900, 400
Steps
  1. H_T = 106
  2. L_T = 99
  3. O_T = 100, C_T = 103
  4. V_T = 2500
one 1-hour bar: 100 / 106 / 99 / 103 on volume 2500

Interpretation

The aggregated bar keeps the extremes and the endpoints and throws away the order in which they occurred. That discarded ordering is exactly the information a lower timeframe retains.

Assumptions

  • Sub-bars tile the target interval exactly, with a declared session boundary and timezone.
  • Volume is additive and from one venue. Summing volume across venues double counts the same economic trade in many crypto datasets.
  • Gaps and halted sessions are handled explicitly; silently closing them changes highs, lows and every derived indicator.
Limits of the formula
  • Aggregation is one-way: the hourly bar cannot be used to recover the path inside the hour.
  • The rule says nothing about which interval should be used; that is a modelling choice and a common source of overfitting.
