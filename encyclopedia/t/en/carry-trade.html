Carry trade

Simple definition

Borrowing where interest is low and investing where it is high, keeping the difference. It works until the exchange rate moves against you, and then it can erase years of gains.

Professional definition

A position funded in a low-yielding currency and invested in a higher-yielding one. The return has two parts: the interest differential, which accrues steadily, and the change in the exchange rate, which does not. The same structure appears in crypto as funding-rate and basis positions.

Technical definition

The payoff profile is short volatility: many small positive carry accruals punctuated by rare, large losses when the funding currency appreciates and positions unwind together. Crowding makes the unwind worse, because the exit is correlated across holders. Covered interest parity removes the differential when the currency risk is hedged, so an uncovered carry position is compensation for bearing that risk, not an arbitrage.

Academic definition

An uncovered position in an interest-rate differential across currencies, whose excess return is empirically associated with negatively skewed payoff distributions and with funding-liquidity and volatility risk.

Mechanism

The differential is paid because someone must bear the risk that the high-yield currency falls. In calm periods it does not, so the position earns steadily and attracts more capital, which compresses the differential and raises crowding. A volatility shock then forces leveraged holders to exit at once, and the exchange-rate loss arrives in days rather than in proportion to the time the carry took to accrue.

In practice

Who uses it
Macro and currency funds, corporate treasuries with cross-currency funding, and crypto traders running funding-rate and basis positions.
Why it matters
It harvests a persistent differential and can produce long, smooth return streams, which is precisely why it attracts leverage.
Limitations
The payoff is negatively skewed, so backtest statistics computed on calm periods overstate it badly, and position sizing based on realised volatility understates the tail.

Common mistakes

  • Sizing a carry position from its calm-period volatility.
  • Treating hedged and unhedged carry as the same trade.
  • Adding leverage because the strategy has been quiet.

Examples

numeric

Fund at 2 percent, invest at 8 percent, exchange rate unchanged: the year returns 6 percent. If the high-yield currency then falls 10 percent, the same position loses about 4.8 percent despite the full carry.

Given
  • home funding rate i_h = 2% = 0.02
  • foreign investment rate i_f = 8% = 0.08
  • case A: spot unchanged, S1/S0 = 1.00
  • case B: foreign currency falls 10%, S1/S0 = 0.90
Steps
  1. case A: 1.08 x 1.00 - 1.02 = 0.06
  2. case B: 1.08 x 0.90 = 0.972
  3. case B: 0.972 - 1.02 = -0.048
case A: +6.0%; case B: -4.8%

Rates and the exchange-rate move are invented. A 10 percent currency move wipes out nearly two years of a 6 percent differential, which is the asymmetry at the centre of the trade.

crypto

A trader is long spot and short the perpetual to collect positive funding. The position pays small amounts for weeks, then a sharp move widens the basis and forces a deleveraging — the same short-volatility shape in a different market.

Mathematical foundation

R = (1 + i_{f}) \frac{S_{1}}{S_{0}} - (1 + i_{h})

Variables

RTotal return on the funded position over the period, in home currencydecimal
i_{f}Interest rate earned in the foreign (investment) currencydecimal
i_{h}Interest rate paid in the home (funding) currencydecimal
S_{0}, S_{1}Spot rate at entry and exit, quoted as home currency per unit of foreign currencyprice

Procedure

Grow one unit of home currency at the foreign rate after converting at entry, convert back at the exit spot, and subtract the cost of the funding leg over the same period. State the quote direction explicitly, because reversing it flips the sign of the exchange-rate term.

Worked example

Given
  • i_f = 0.08
  • i_h = 0.02
  • S1 / S0 = 0.90 (foreign currency depreciates 10%)
Steps
  1. (1 + 0.08) = 1.08
  2. 1.08 x 0.90 = 0.972
  3. R = 0.972 - 1.02
R = -0.048, a 4.8% loss despite a 6 percentage point interest differential

Interpretation

The differential is the part you can see in advance; the exchange-rate term is the part that decides the outcome. A positive carry is a rent for holding currency risk, so the expected return is not the differential.

Assumptions

  • Both rates apply over the same horizon and the position is unhedged; with a forward hedge, covered interest parity removes the differential up to transaction costs.
  • No leverage is included in this form. Any leverage multiplies both the carry and the currency loss, and introduces margin calls that the formula does not show.
  • The quote convention must be fixed in advance; the same trade written with the inverse quote changes the sign of the spot term.
Limits of the formula
  • The formula is a single-period accounting identity and says nothing about the distribution of outcomes, which is the actual risk of the strategy.
  • Realised volatility measured in calm periods systematically understates the tail of a crowded carry position.

Commonly confused with

Arbitrage

Arbitrage is a riskless price inconsistency. An uncovered carry position is paid precisely because it carries currency risk; hedging that risk removes the differential, which is what covered interest parity says.

Flagged by the editor as uncertain on a linguistic, mathematical or regulatory point.

The return decomposition is an accounting identity for a single period and is presented as such. That carry payoffs are negatively skewed — small steady gains punctuated by large losses — is widely documented and stated qualitatively; no skewness, Sharpe ratio or crash frequency is quoted because this registry contains no estimate. Covered interest parity is described as the reason hedged carry is not an arbitrage, without claiming it holds exactly in practice. All rates and the currency move in the examples are invented. Nothing here is a recommendation to run the strategy. The Kurdish headword is a proposal by this project and needs native-expert review.
