Simple definition

How much variation in your results you can live with. Not how much you say you can take, but how much you turn out to accept when the account is actually falling.

Professional definition

The willingness to accept variability in outcomes, conventionally separated from risk capacity, which is the financial ability to absorb a loss. Industry practice elicits it with questionnaires; the stated answer and the behaviour observed during a drawdown are different quantities and often disagree.

Technical definition

Two measures exist and should not be conflated. The stated preference is an answer to a hypothetical, given before any loss has been felt and known to be sensitive to how the question is framed. The revealed preference is inferred from what was actually done — whether the plan was followed, whether size was cut, whether the position was abandoned — and is only observable once a drawdown has occurred. Most people therefore learn their revealed tolerance after it has already mattered.

Academic definition

An individual's willingness to accept dispersion in outcomes, distinguished from risk capacity and separable into a stated component elicited ex ante and a revealed component inferred from behaviour under loss.

Mechanism

A questionnaire asks about a loss in the abstract, where it is a number on a page. A drawdown presents the same loss as a falling balance, in sequence, with no known end point. The two situations differ in what the reference point is and in how long the discomfort lasts, so answers given in one do not predict behaviour in the other particularly well.

In practice

Who uses it
Investors completing a suitability questionnaire, advisers reading one, and traders choosing a risk fraction per position.
Why it matters
Separating willingness from capacity keeps two distinct constraints visible: what you are prepared to sit through, and what your finances can absorb regardless.
Limitations
This entry describes how tolerance is measured and where the measures disagree. What any particular person's tolerance should be is not a question an encyclopedia can answer.

Common mistakes

  • Treating a questionnaire score as a measurement rather than a starting point.
  • Setting position size from willingness while ignoring capacity.
  • Revising the stated tolerance downward mid-drawdown and calling it a new plan.

Examples

numeric

Someone states a tolerance for a 30 percent fall. From 100 that is a balance of 70, and returning to 100 requires a gain of about 43 percent on the reduced base. The stated number and its recovery cost are the same fact.

Given
  • starting equity = 100
  • stated tolerable drawdown = 30 percent
  • no withdrawals or additions
Steps
  1. equity after the drawdown = 100 x (1 - 0.30) = 70
  2. gain required to return to 100 = (100 - 70) / 70
  3. = 0.4286
a 30 percent drawdown requires about a 43 percent gain to recover

Arithmetic only. It says nothing about how long recovery takes or how likely it is, and nothing about what level of drawdown anyone should accept.

market

A questionnaire answer of high tolerance, given in a calm month. Six weeks into a fall the same person halves every position. The questionnaire recorded a stated preference; the halving revealed a different one.

Commonly confused with

Drawdown

A drawdown is a measured fall from a peak, a number about the account. Risk tolerance is about the person: how much of that number they accept before they change what they are doing.

Flagged by the editor as uncertain on a linguistic, mathematical or regulatory point.

The split between tolerance and capacity, and the use of questionnaires to elicit the former, are industry convention rather than a research result, hence the claim type. That stated choices are sensitive to framing is cited to the 1979 paper; the specific claim that questionnaire scores predict drawdown behaviour poorly is reasoned from that framing sensitivity and is not a measured result in the cited sources. Suitability requirements differ by jurisdiction. The entry gives no view on what tolerance anyone should have.
