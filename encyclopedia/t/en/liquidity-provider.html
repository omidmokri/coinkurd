Liquidity Provider

Simple definition

Someone who deposits assets into a pool so that others can trade against them, and who collects a share of the trading fees for doing it.

Professional definition

A depositor whose capital becomes the inventory of an automated market maker. In exchange for a pro-rata claim on fees, the provider accepts a position that is continuously rebalanced against the price move, so the payoff is short volatility of the price ratio.

Technical definition

Economically a passive market maker who quotes by rule instead of by judgement. The return decomposes into fee income, divergence loss on the invariant, any incentive emissions, and the price change of the underlying basket. Unlike a book market maker, the provider cannot widen a spread, pull a quote, or decline to trade with better-informed flow, so adverse selection is absorbed in full and only fee volume compensates for it.

Academic definition

An agent supplying inventory to a constant-function market maker in return for a pro-rata fee claim, bearing in exchange the negative convexity of the pool position relative to a static holding of the same assets.

Mechanism

The provider is paid for immediacy and charged for information. Flow that is merely impatient leaves the reserve ratio roughly where it was and the fee is kept; flow that knows something moves the ratio and does not come back, and that move is the loss. Fee income therefore has to outrun informed flow, which is why wide spreads, high turnover and correlated pairs are the pools that work.

In practice

Who uses it
Individual depositors, professional on-chain market makers, protocol treasuries seeding their own pair, and funds running delta-hedged pool positions.
Why it matters
It converts idle inventory into fee income and, for a protocol, buys a tradable market for a token that has none.
Limitations
The position cannot be quoted selectively, the fee rate is fixed by the pool, and all four return components move together in a stress episode.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing a pool's quoted yield with a deposit rate as if the capital were principal-protected.
  • Providing liquidity in a volatile pair while expecting fees alone to decide the outcome.
  • Ignoring that the exit basket's composition is not what was deposited.

Examples

numeric

A provider holds pool tokens worth two percent of the pool. The reserves end the quarter at 4,000 of asset A and 410,000 of asset B, so the claim is 80 A and 8,200 B — not the amounts first deposited.

Given
  • provider's pool tokens / total pool tokens = 0.02
  • reserves at exit: 4,000 A and 410,000 B
  • no further deposits or withdrawals by others during the period
Steps
  1. claim on A = 0.02 x 4,000 = 80
  2. claim on B = 0.02 x 410,000 = 8,200
80 A and 8,200 B, whatever the opening mix was.

Fees are already inside the reserves in a fee-accruing pool, so the share is taken on the post-fee balances.

market

Two providers deposit the same value on the same day, one into a pegged-asset pool and one into a volatile pair. Both earn fees, but only the second one's result is decided mainly by where the price ratio ended.

Mathematical foundation

Procedure

Divide the provider's pool tokens by the total in issue to get the share, then multiply that share by each current reserve to get the redeemable amounts. Redemption is on today's reserves, so both the composition and the fees accrued since deposit are included automatically.

Worked example

Given
  • L_i = 2,000 pool tokens
  • L_total = 100,000 pool tokens
  • X = 4,000 A
  • Y = 410,000 B
Steps
  1. s = 2,000 / 100,000 = 0.02
  2. x_i = 0.02 x 4,000 = 80 A
  3. y_i = 0.02 x 410,000 = 8,200 B
The position redeems 80 A and 8,200 B.

Interpretation

A pool token is a claim on a fraction of whatever the reserves are at redemption, not a receipt for specific assets. That single fact is the source of both fee accrual and divergence loss: the same share is applied to a basket the pool has been rebalancing all along.

Assumptions

  • A fee-accruing pool where swap fees are added to the reserves. Protocols that pay fees out separately need the fee stream added as its own term.
  • A full-range position. In a concentrated-liquidity design the claim is on a range-specific position, not on a constant fraction of total reserves.
Limits of the formula
  • The share formula gives the quantities redeemable, not the result of the investment: it says nothing about value against a static holding of the same assets.
  • Incentive emissions, gas costs and any hedge sit outside this identity and must be accounted separately.

Market Making
