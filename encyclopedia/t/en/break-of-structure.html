Simple definition

When price closes beyond the last swing high or low that defined the trend, price-action traders call the structure broken and read it as the trend continuing in that direction.

Professional definition

In retail smart-money teaching, a close beyond the most recent reference swing point — the swing high in an uptrend, the swing low in a downtrend — taken as confirmation that the existing trend continues. A break in the opposite direction is usually given a different name, such as change of character or market-structure shift.

Technical definition

Codeable as follows: label swings with a fractal rule, for example a high with k lower highs on each side; in an uptrend the reference is the latest such swing high that was followed by a higher low; a break is recorded when a candle closes beyond that level. Most teachings require a body close, some accept a wick. The rule is therefore parameter-dependent: k, body versus wick, and the timeframe each change which breaks exist on the same chart.

Academic definition

A rule-based annotation of trend continuation defined as the violation of the most recent reference extreme in a labelled swing sequence, used in practitioner price-action frameworks without an established statistical validation.

Mechanism

Nothing in the market enforces a break of structure; it is a bookkeeping rule laid over a swing sequence. What gives it content is definitional: a trend of rising highs and lows must keep exceeding prior highs, so the annotation tracks trend persistence. The institutional-order-flow story told around it is an interpretation of the same candles, not an observation of anyone's orders.

In practice

Who uses it
Price-action and smart-money-concepts traders, and anyone coding swing-structure logic into an indicator or a strategy.
Why it matters
It turns the statement the trend is still intact into one explicit condition, which makes it codeable, auditable and testable instead of a judgement call.
Limitations
Definitions differ between teachers, so two traders reading one chart disagree about where a break occurred. No peer-reviewed evidence base establishes it as an edge.

Common mistakes

  • Calling a break from a wick that closed back inside the level.
  • Changing the swing rule after the fact so a break appears.
  • Reading a low-timeframe break as a change of trend.

Examples

market

An uptrend prints a swing high, pulls back to a higher low, then pushes up again. A close above the swing high records the break and the trend label survives; a failure to close above it leaves the structure unbroken, however far the wick reached.

numeric

The reference swing high is 42,500. One candle spikes to 42,800 but closes at 42,410; the next closes at 42,620. A wick rule records the break on the first candle, a body rule on the second — the same chart, two different entries.

Given
  • reference swing high = 42,500
  • candle A: wick high 42,800, close 42,410
  • candle B: close 42,620
Steps
  1. wick rule: 42,800 > 42,500, so the break is recorded on candle A
  2. body-close rule: 42,410 < 42,500, so candle A does not break
  3. body-close rule: 42,620 > 42,500, so the break is recorded on candle B
The break is dated one candle apart under the two rules.

Same data, two legitimate definitions, different trade. The rule must be fixed in writing before any backtest, or the result is not reproducible.

Commonly confused with

Breakout

A breakout is a move beyond any level: a range edge, a trendline, a round number. A break of structure is specifically the violation of a reference swing point inside a labelled swing sequence.
