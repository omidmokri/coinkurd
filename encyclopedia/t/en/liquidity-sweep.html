Liquidity Sweep

Simple definition

A quick push beyond an obvious high or low, just far enough to trigger the orders waiting there, followed by a sharp move back the other way.

Professional definition

A brief excursion through a level where stop and breakout orders are expected to rest — a prior swing extreme, a round number, a session high or low — which trades through them and then reverses, leaving a long wick and no acceptance beyond the level.

Technical definition

Codeable as: price exceeds a reference extreme by at least a threshold in ticks, percent or a fraction of ATR, then closes back inside the level within m bars, with the excursion's wick exceeding its body by some ratio. Every one of those parameters is a free choice. Note the narrow sense of liquidity here: resting orders inferred from a chart level, not the cost of trading size, which is what the word means in microstructure.

Academic definition

A practitioner label for a short-lived violation of a salient price extreme followed by rapid reversion, interpreted as the execution of orders clustered at that level, with no established evidence base in the reviewed literature.

Mechanism

Stops and breakout entries do cluster just beyond visible extremes, which follows from how traders place orders. A move that reaches them removes that resting supply or demand from the book, and with it gone price can travel back quickly. Whether anyone aimed at them is not observable from price: a breakout attempt that simply failed prints exactly the same wick.

In practice

Who uses it
Price-action and smart-money-concepts traders, and anyone deciding where not to place a stop around an obvious level.
Why it matters
It encodes a real and useful warning: the most obvious place to put a stop is also the place a thin book is most likely to reach.
Limitations
Thresholds and the reversal window are arbitrary, so sweeps are counted differently by different traders. Attributed intent is untestable from chart data, and failed breakouts look identical.

Common mistakes

  • Calling every long wick a sweep after the reversal happened.
  • Placing stops exactly at the obvious swing extreme.
  • Assuming intent where a failed breakout explains the bar.

Examples

market

A pair sits under an obvious triple top for days. A thin weekend session trades a few ticks through it, fills the stops of everyone short from the range, then closes back inside and resumes the range for another week.

numeric

The reference swing low is 1,000 and ATR is 20, so a quarter-ATR threshold is 5. A print at 992 with a close back at 1,008 two bars later qualifies; a dip to 998 does not, because it never cleared the threshold.

Given
  • reference swing low = 1,000
  • ATR = 20
  • excursion threshold = 0.25 x ATR
  • reversal window m = 2 bars
Steps
  1. threshold = 0.25 x 20 = 5, so the trigger level is 995
  2. low printed 992, which is 8 below the reference and past the trigger
  3. close two bars later = 1,008, back inside the level
A sweep is recorded under this parameter set; a 998 low would not have been.

Tighten the threshold to 2 and the 998 case also counts as a sweep. The pattern's frequency is a function of its parameters.

Commonly confused with

Liquidity

In microstructure, liquidity is the cost of trading size. In sweep terminology it means orders presumed to rest at a chart level. The two senses are not interchangeable, and only the first is measurable from the order book.
