Bitcoin Halving

Simple definition

A rule built into Bitcoin that cuts the new coins paid for each block in half every 210,000 blocks. It is why bitcoin issuance slows down over time and eventually stops.

Professional definition

A consensus-enforced halving of the block subsidy every 210,000 blocks, roughly four years at a ten-minute block target. The fourth halving occurred at block 840,000 in April 2024 and cut the subsidy to 3.125 BTC; the fifth is expected around April 2028.

Technical definition

A step reduction in per-block issuance applied by block height, not by date, so the calendar timing drifts with realised block intervals. Subsidies are computed in satoshis with integer arithmetic, so after enough halvings the subsidy rounds to zero and transaction fees become the only block reward. Because the schedule is public and years ahead, any claim that a halving is itself price-moving news must explain why a fully known schedule is not already discounted.

Academic definition

A predetermined, consensus-enforced reduction by one half of per-block coin issuance at fixed block-height intervals of 210,000, producing a geometrically declining supply schedule that converges on a finite limit.

Mechanism

Difficulty retargeting holds the average block interval near ten minutes, so counting blocks is also a rough clock. Fixing issuance to that block count makes new supply a published function of height rather than a policy decision. Miner revenue per block therefore halves overnight while costs do not, which forces the least efficient capacity out until difficulty adjusts.

In practice

Who uses it
Miners planning capacity and hedging revenue, analysts modelling issuance, and anyone reading supply-schedule arguments about bitcoin.
Why it matters
It fixes the only source of new supply on a schedule nobody can discretionarily change, which is the core of bitcoin's monetary design claim.
Limitations
The halving constrains new supply only. It says nothing about demand, about coins already held, or about price, and its dates are estimates.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the halving date as a surprise when the schedule is public years ahead.
  • Confusing a cut in new issuance with a cut in total supply.
  • Quoting a halving date as fixed rather than block-height dependent.

Examples

numeric

At block 840,000 the chain entered its fifth subsidy era. Dividing the original 50 BTC by two four times gives 3.125 BTC per block, which is the subsidy that halving set.

Given
  • initial subsidy = 50 BTC per block
  • halving interval = 210,000 blocks
  • block height h = 840,000
Steps
  1. era index = floor(840,000 / 210,000) = 4
  2. subsidy = 50 / 2^4 = 50 / 16
3.125 BTC per block

Height determines the era. Calendar dates for future halvings are projections from an average block interval, not protocol values.

market

Every halving date is derivable years in advance, so a halving is not new information in the way an unexpected policy change is. Whether realised returns around halvings reflect anything beyond that is contested and the sample is tiny.

Procedure

Divide the block height by 210,000 and take the floor to get the era index. Halve the original 50 BTC subsidy that many times. Protocol implementations do this as an integer right-shift on a satoshi amount, so the result is exact rather than rounded.

Worked example

Given
  • h = 840,000
  • halving interval = 210,000 blocks
  • initial subsidy = 50 BTC
Steps
  1. e = floor(840,000 / 210,000) = 4
  2. 2^4 = 16
  3. 50 / 16 = 3.125
s = 3.125 BTC per block

Interpretation

The output is the new coins created per block in the current era. Summing the schedule over all eras gives a finite total, which is where the commonly quoted 21 million cap comes from.

Assumptions

  • The ten-minute block interval is an average enforced by difficulty retargeting, so halving dates are projections while heights are exact.
  • Subsidies are stored and halved as integer satoshi amounts, which is why the sequence terminates at zero rather than continuing indefinitely.
  • The schedule holds only while consensus rules are unchanged; it is a social and economic commitment enforced by node operators, not a law of nature.
Limits of the formula
  • The formula describes issuance only. It implies nothing about price, demand, or the stock of coins already mined.
  • Other chains labelled as having halvings use different intervals and different initial subsidies, so the constants are Bitcoin-specific.

Commonly confused with

Token Unlock

A halving reduces the rate at which new units are created. A token unlock releases units that already exist into tradable supply. One slows issuance; the other raises float.

Block heights, the 210,000-block interval and the 3.125 BTC subsidy after block 840,000 are protocol facts. The April 2028 date is a projection from the ten-minute average block interval and will move with realised hash rate. Any price effect of halvings is explicitly treated as contested here: the sample is four events and no registry source supports a causal claim. Kurdish wording follows the project lexicon and is descriptive rather than a settled term.
