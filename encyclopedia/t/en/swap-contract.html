Swap Contract

Simple definition

An agreement to exchange one stream of payments for another over time, on an agreed amount. Neither side hands over the amount itself; only the payments are swapped.

Professional definition

A derivative in which two parties exchange cash flows calculated on a notional amount at set dates until the contract matures. The notional never changes hands; it is only the measuring stick for each payment. Interest rates, currencies, commodities and total returns are all swapped this way.

Technical definition

Economically a portfolio of forward contracts sharing one schedule: each payment date is a forward on the underlying reference, and the swap's value is the net present value of the two legs' remaining cash flows. Value at inception is set to zero by choosing the fixed leg, so the contract is an exchange of risk, not of capital. Most interest-rate and credit swaps are now centrally cleared and margined; bespoke ones remain bilateral.

Academic definition

A bilateral derivative contract specifying the exchange of two payment streams, each computed by reference to a stated notional principal and a defined rate or index, over a sequence of determination dates.

Mechanism

Because only the difference between the two legs is paid, a swap transfers a specific risk without moving principal, which is why it needs far less capital than selling the exposure outright. The notional exists to size the payments, so quoting a swap market by notional overstates how much money is actually at stake.

In practice

Who uses it
Corporate treasuries, banks, funds and sovereign borrowers restructuring interest-rate, currency or commodity exposure without refinancing the underlying debt.
Why it matters
It separates the risk a borrower wants from the funding it can actually raise: issue at whatever rate the market offers, then swap into the profile you prefer.
Limitations
A swap is a long-dated obligation whose value swings with rates and curves; unwinding early means paying its market value, and bilateral swaps carry counterparty and documentation risk.

Common mistakes

  • Reading notional as money at risk.
  • Assuming a swap can be exited free of charge before maturity.
  • Hedging a floating exposure with a mismatched payment schedule.

Examples

market

A company can only borrow at a floating rate but wants certainty. It keeps the loan and enters a swap in which it pays fixed and receives floating, so the floating payments cancel and its net cost is fixed.

numeric

On a notional of 10 million, one leg owes 3.0% and the other 3.4% for a half-year period. Only the 0.4% difference moves: about 20,000 for the period. The 10 million itself is never paid by anyone.

Given
  • Notional = 10,000,000
  • Leg A rate = 3.0% a year
  • Leg B rate = 3.4% a year
  • Period = 0.5 year, simple accrual
Steps
  1. Leg A accrual = 10,000,000 x 0.030 x 0.5 = 150,000
  2. Leg B accrual = 10,000,000 x 0.034 x 0.5 = 170,000
  3. Net payment = 170,000 - 150,000 = 20,000
The payer of leg B pays 20,000 net for the period

Day-count conventions change the accrual factor slightly; 0.5 is used here for clarity, not as a market convention.

Commonly confused with

Swap

On a decentralised exchange a swap is an instant exchange of one token for another, settled on the spot. A swap contract is a dated series of future payments and exchanges no principal at all.
