Intrinsic and Time Value

Simple definition

An option's price splits in two: what exercising would pay right now, and what you pay on top for the time left before expiry.

Professional definition

Intrinsic value is the exercise payoff floored at zero, max(S−K,0) for a call and max(K−S,0) for a put. Time value is whatever the traded premium holds above that. The split is an identity on an observed price, not a valuation.

Technical definition

A decomposition of a quoted premium into an arithmetic floor and a residual. The residual carries everything a model has to price: remaining time, the volatility input, the financing of the strike and any carry. It is largest at the money, falls towards zero as expiry approaches, and for a deep in-the-money European put can be negative when intrinsic is measured against the undiscounted strike, because the correct floor there is the discounted strike.

Academic definition

The partition of an option's market premium into its immediate exercise value, bounded below by zero, and a residual extrinsic component attributable to the remaining optionality.

Mechanism

Intrinsic value is arithmetic: a right is never exercised at a loss, so the floor is zero. Everything else in the premium is payment for what has not happened yet. That residual shrinks as the window of possible moves closes, and it is biggest where a small move changes the outcome, which is at the money.

In practice

Who uses it
Option traders judging what they are paying for, market makers quoting the residual, and anyone deciding whether to exercise or to sell the contract.
Why it matters
It separates the part of the premium that is already earned from the part that still has to be justified by a move before expiry.
Limitations
The split tells you the composition of a price, never whether that price is fair. Fair value needs a model and a volatility input.

Common mistakes

  • Calling an option cheap because its premium is small when it is all time value.
  • Exercising early and discarding remaining time value that could be sold.
  • Expecting time value to decay in equal daily amounts.

Examples

market

Two calls trade at the same premium: one in the money and almost all intrinsic, the other far out of the money and all time value. Identical cost, completely different exposure to a change in the volatility input.

numeric

Underlying 104, call strike 100, premium 6. Intrinsic is 4 and time value 2. The same chain's 110 call at 1.20 has no intrinsic value at all, so its whole premium is time value and expires worthless unless price moves.

Given
  • S = 104
  • call K = 100, premium = 6
  • call K = 110, premium = 1.20
Steps
  1. intrinsic(100) = max(104 - 100, 0) = 4
  2. time value(100) = 6 - 4 = 2
  3. intrinsic(110) = max(104 - 110, 0) = 0
  4. time value(110) = 1.20 - 0 = 1.20
the 100 call is two thirds intrinsic; the 110 call is 100% time value

Premiums here are illustrative figures for the arithmetic, not quotes from any market.

Procedure

Compute intrinsic value from the strike and the current price, flooring at zero. Subtract it from the traded premium; the remainder is time value. Do this per unit of underlying, using the same quotation basis for both numbers.

Worked example

Given
  • S = 104
  • K = 100
  • premium = 6
Steps
  1. 104 - 100 = 4
  2. max(4, 0) = 4
  3. 6 - 4 = 2
intrinsic 4, time value 2

Interpretation

The decomposition says what you are buying: a payoff you could take today, plus a claim on moves that have not happened. Only the second part can be destroyed by the mere passage of time.

Assumptions

  • Intrinsic value is defined against the current underlying price and the strike alone; dividends, carry and funding are excluded from it and fall into the residual.
  • The identity decomposes an observed price. It is not a pricing model and says nothing about fair value.
  • For European options the correct no-arbitrage floor uses the discounted strike, so measuring time value against undiscounted intrinsic can make it appear negative for deep in-the-money puts. This is a convention artefact, not an arbitrage.
  • American-style options carry an early-exercise component that most desks leave inside the time-value residual rather than reporting separately.
Limits of the formula
  • Time value is a residual, so every pricing error, stale mark or wide spread in the premium lands in it.
  • The size of the residual cannot be interpreted without the volatility input and the time remaining; a large time value is not evidence of an expensive option on its own.

Commonly confused with

Intrinsic Value

In valuation, intrinsic value means an analyst's estimate of what a business is worth. In options it is pure arithmetic on strike and price, with no estimation in it.
