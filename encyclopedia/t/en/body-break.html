Body Break (not a wick break)

Simple definition

In the Mokri method, a wick poking through a trendline is not a break. Only a candle body crossing the line counts, and until then the line is redrawn from each new high or low.

Professional definition

A confirmation rule: a break exists only when the candle body closes beyond the trendline. A wick excursion leaves the line intact but obliges the analyst to redraw it from the new extreme, and only a body break activates the Body V target.

Technical definition

A filter that trades signal frequency for signal quality. Requiring a body close discards the wick excursions that produce most false breaks, at the cost of later entries. The companion validity rule is that a line with no genuine touch between its two anchor points is rejected in favour of one with more and better touches.

Academic definition

A breakout confirmation criterion defined on the close rather than the intraperiod extreme, combined with an anchor-validity requirement on the number of prior contacts.

Mechanism

The wick records where price went and was rejected; the body records where it was accepted and closed. Conditioning on the close therefore filters for the excursions that the market agreed with, which is why body-based confirmation reduces false breaks at the cost of entering later and further from the level.

In practice

Who uses it
Price-action traders using trendline breaks as entries, on any market where wick noise is common.
Why it matters
It is the single cheapest filter against the fakeout, and it makes the break condition unambiguous enough to code.
Limitations
The author's own framework. On fast markets a body close can arrive far from the line, so the filter improves precision while worsening the entry price and sometimes the risk-reward.

Common mistakes

  • Entering on the wick and calling it a break.
  • Keeping the original line after a new extreme instead of correcting it.
  • Accepting a line whose two anchors have no touch between them.

Examples

scenario

A candle's upper wick pierces a descending resistance line but closes below it. Under this rule nothing happened: the line is redrawn from the new high and no Body V target is activated.

market

Two candles later a body closes clearly above the redrawn line. That is the break, and the Body V measurement is taken from the bodies before this candle, not before the wick.

Commonly confused with

Breakout

A breakout in general usage can be called on any touch beyond a level. Here it is defined strictly on the body close, so many conventional breakouts do not qualify.
