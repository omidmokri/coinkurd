Simple definition

How much money exists in an economy, counted in layers: physical cash first, then current accounts, then savings that are nearly as easy to spend.

Professional definition

A set of nested aggregates. M0 is central-bank money — notes, coin and reserve balances. M1 adds the deposits you can spend immediately. M2 adds close substitutes such as small time deposits and retail money funds. Each layer is wider and slightly less liquid than the last.

Technical definition

The boundaries are administrative choices, not natural facts: each central bank defines its own aggregates, revises them, and the same label can mean different things in different jurisdictions, so M2 is not comparable across countries. Most money is created when banks lend, not when a central bank prints, and the textbook reserve multiplier does not describe operating frameworks in which reserves are ample and the policy rate is set by administered rates.

Academic definition

A family of nested measures of the stock of liquid liabilities held by the non-bank public, whose definitional boundaries are set by the compiling central bank rather than by theory.

Mechanism

Commercial banks create deposits when they extend credit; the central bank supplies the settlement money banks use among themselves and sets its price. So an aggregate can grow because households borrowed, because the state borrowed from banks, or because the central bank swapped bonds for reserves — three different causes with different consequences, invisible in the headline number.

In practice

Who uses it
Monetary economists, bank treasury teams, and macro investors who track aggregates as one input among many.
Why it matters
Aggregates are among the few monthly windows into credit creation and bank balance sheets, and sharp turns in them often accompany turns in credit conditions.
Limitations
Definitions differ by central bank and change over time, velocity is unstable, and no stable short-horizon relation to asset prices has been established.

Common mistakes

  • Comparing one country's M2 with another's as if the label meant the same thing.
  • Reading reserve growth as money in the hands of the public.
  • Expecting a fixed lag from aggregate growth to prices.

Examples

numeric

Currency in circulation is 200, demand and other immediately spendable deposits 1,300, and small time deposits plus retail money funds 2,500. M1 is 1,500 and M2 is 4,000 on these definitions.

Given
  • currency = 200
  • immediately spendable deposits = 1,300
  • small time deposits and retail money funds = 2,500
Steps
  1. M1 = 200 + 1,300 = 1,500
  2. M2 = M1 + 2,500 = 4,000
M1 = 1,500; M2 = 4,000

Component values are invented, and the component list itself is illustrative: each central bank defines which instruments enter which aggregate, and those definitions are revised.

market

Reserve balances rise sharply after central-bank asset purchases while deposits held by households barely move. Commentary that treats the two as one quantity will read a liquidity surge that households never received.

Commonly confused with

Liquidity

Money supply counts a stock of monetary instruments. Market liquidity describes how cheaply an asset can be traded. A large money stock does not guarantee a deep order book in any particular market.

Editorial status

Needs expert reviewcontestedv1.02026-11-05

Flagged by the editor as uncertain on a linguistic, mathematical or regulatory point.

The M0, M1 and M2 descriptions are illustrative of the usual nesting; the actual components of each aggregate are defined by each central bank, differ between jurisdictions and are revised, so no official definition is reproduced and no level or growth rate is quoted. The transmission from monetary aggregates to risk-asset prices is genuinely contested: one reading sees aggregate growth as a leading cause of asset inflation, another sees aggregates as largely endogenous to credit demand and uninformative at short horizons. This registry contains no study settling it, so both readings are stated and neither is endorsed. The Kurdish headword پێشکەشکردنی پارە is a proposal by this project and needs native-expert review.
