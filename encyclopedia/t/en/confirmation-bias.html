Confirmation Bias

Simple definition

Looking for what agrees with the view you already hold and skipping what disagrees. The chart is the same for everyone; the search through it is not.

Professional definition

A tendency to seek, notice and remember evidence that supports a held hypothesis, while subjecting contrary evidence to stricter scrutiny or none at all. In trading it shows up as selective screening, selective recall of past setups, and selective reading of the same chart.

Technical definition

Confirmation bias operates on the search and evaluation stages rather than on the conclusion directly. A trader rarely asserts a false fact; more often the sample of facts examined is non-random with respect to the hypothesis. That makes it a sampling problem, which is why the countermeasures are procedural: a pre-registered rule, a pre-written invalidation level, and a record kept before the outcome is known.

Academic definition

A systematic asymmetry in information search and evaluation favouring hypothesis-consistent evidence, producing overconfident inference from a non-random sample of available data.

Mechanism

The hypothesis arrives first and then shapes the search. Once a direction is held, supporting indicators are added to the chart and contrary timeframes are closed, so the evidence set becomes a function of the belief rather than a test of it. Because each individual observation is genuine, the conclusion feels well supported and the bias is invisible from the inside.

In practice

Who uses it
Analysts, discretionary traders, strategy developers, and anyone reviewing their own past decisions.
Why it matters
It is the bias most directly addressed by method: writing the invalidation condition before entry converts an opinion into a testable claim.
Limitations
Naming the bias does not remove it. Awareness alone has little effect; what helps is a record made in advance and a rule that fires without a further judgement call.

Common mistakes

  • Adding indicators until one agrees with the view.
  • Counting only the setups that worked when reviewing a method.
  • Reading a lower timeframe until it supports the decision already taken.

Examples

market

A trader long an asset reads every bounce as support holding and every break as a liquidity sweep. Both readings may be available on the chart; only one was searched for.

numeric

Reviewing a method, a trader recalls 12 winners and 3 losers and reports an 80 percent win rate. The full log holds 12 winners and 18 losers: 40 percent. Nothing was invented; the sample was selected.

Given
  • remembered sample: 12 wins, 3 losses
  • complete log: 12 wins, 18 losses
  • no trade outcome misreported in either count
Steps
  1. remembered win rate = 12 / (12 + 3) = 0.80
  2. actual win rate = 12 / (12 + 18) = 0.40
  3. difference = 40 percentage points from selection alone
80 percent recalled against 40 percent recorded

Illustrative counts. The mechanism is selective recall of a non-random subsample, which is why a log written before outcomes are known is the practical defence.

Related terminology

related
Loss Aversion Overfitting Backtesting Win Rate Fear and Greed Index
Commonly confused with

Overfitting

Confirmation bias selects evidence for a belief a person holds. Overfitting selects parameters for a model on one dataset. Both are selection problems; one happens in the analyst, the other in the fitting procedure.
