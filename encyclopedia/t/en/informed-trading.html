Informed Trading

Simple definition

Trading on something the market has not priced in yet. The informed trader's problem is that trading reveals the information, so the price moves against them as they go.

Professional definition

Trading motivated by a signal about value that is not yet in the price. In microstructure models the informed trader chooses size to balance the gain from a correct view against the price impact their own orders create, so their flow is detectable in aggregate even when individual orders are not.

Technical definition

In Kyle's framework a single risk-neutral informed trader submits a quantity into a batch auction alongside normally distributed noise flow, and a competitive market maker clears at a price linear in total order flow. The informed trader optimally trades a fixed fraction of their edge, splitting it to disguise it among the noise; the resulting impact coefficient is increasing in value uncertainty and decreasing in noise volume, which makes liquidity the informed trader's camouflage.

Academic definition

Order submission conditioned on private information about asset value, modelled as a strategic choice of quantity under the constraint that the submitted quantity itself determines the execution price.

Mechanism

Information is valuable only while nobody else acts on it, but acting on it is what transmits it. Each order the informed trader sends is evidence, so the quote moves part of the way towards the private signal on every fill. The edge is self-consuming: the trade that captures it destroys it, which is why informed flow is split, timed into liquid windows and hidden behind noise.

In practice

Who uses it
Microstructure researchers, market makers classifying flow, surveillance teams, and execution desks that must measure their own footprint.
Why it matters
It supplies the standard account of why price impact exists at all, and it is the measurable side of what spreads are charging for.
Limitations
Being informed is not a legal category: a legitimate research edge and unlawful inside information are indistinguishable in the data, so impact measures identify neither.

Common mistakes

  • Equating informed trading with insider trading.
  • Treating a large single order as informed and small ones as noise.
  • Assuming impact estimates from one venue transfer to another.

Examples

market

A fund with a genuine research edge wants a large position. Sent as one order it moves the price before it is filled and the edge disappears into its own impact; split over days into liquid sessions, most of the edge survives but some leaks as others infer the flow.

numeric

Value uncertainty has a standard deviation of 20 and noise flow a standard deviation of 500 units. Kyle's impact coefficient is 0.02 per unit, so a net 300-unit buy lifts the clearing price by 6 — the cost of the camouflage running out.

Given
  • sigma_v = 20 (standard deviation of terminal value)
  • sigma_u = 500 (standard deviation of noise order flow)
  • net order flow y = +300 units
Steps
  1. lambda = sigma_v / (2 x sigma_u) = 20 / 1000 = 0.02
  2. price change = lambda x y = 0.02 x 300 = 6
clearing price rises by 6 units

This is Kyle's single-period equilibrium, not an estimate from data. Empirical lambdas are fitted by regression and are not the same quantity, so the two numbers should never be compared directly.

Mathematical foundation

\Delta P = \lambda\, y, \qquad \lambda = \frac{\sigma_v}{2\,\sigma_u}, \qquad y = x + u

Variables

Procedure

State the distribution of value and of noise flow. The market maker prices linearly in total flow; the informed trader picks the quantity maximising expected profit given that rule. Solving the two conditions together fixes the impact coefficient, and the price change for any realised net flow follows from it.

Worked example

Given
  • sigma_v = 20
  • sigma_u = 500
  • y = +300
Steps
  1. lambda = 20 / (2 x 500) = 0.02
  2. Delta P = 0.02 x 300 = 6
lambda = 0.02, price change = +6

Interpretation

Lambda is price impact per unit of net flow, so one over lambda is market depth. Deeper noise trading lowers it and greater value uncertainty raises it: markets are liquid when uninformative flow is plentiful relative to what is at stake.

Assumptions

  • Kyle's one-period model: a single risk-neutral informed trader, normally distributed noise flow, a competitive risk-neutral market maker earning zero expected profit, and batch clearing rather than continuous matching.
  • The closed form for lambda holds in that single-auction setting. The multi-period and continuous-time versions give a different, time-varying coefficient.
  • Empirical price-impact coefficients estimated by regressing returns on signed flow are often also called lambda but are a different, model-free quantity.
Limits of the formula
  • Impact in real markets is concave in size over longer horizons, not linear, so the linear rule is a local approximation at best.
  • The model has exactly one informed trader. With several competing on the same signal the information is revealed faster and the equilibrium changes qualitatively.

Commonly confused with

Insider Trading

Informed trading is an economic description of any order placed on a value signal the price lacks. Insider trading is a legal category defined by the source of the information and the duty attached to it. One is about mechanism, the other about law.
