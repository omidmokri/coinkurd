Interest Rate Swap

Simple definition

A deal where one side pays a fixed interest rate and the other pays a changing one, both on the same agreed amount. Only the difference actually changes hands.

Professional definition

A swap in which one leg accrues at a fixed rate agreed at the start and the other at a floating reference rate reset each period, both on the same notional principal. The notional is never exchanged; on each payment date the two accruals are netted and one party pays the difference.

Technical definition

A sequence of netted exchanges on a fixed schedule, priced so that at inception the present value of the fixed leg equals that of the floating leg; the fixed rate that achieves this is the swap rate. Value afterwards is the difference between the legs' remaining discounted cash flows, so the position has interest-rate duration while carrying almost no principal risk. Floating resets are set in advance and paid in arrears, and day-count and reset conventions are contractual, not universal.

Academic definition

A derivative contract exchanging a fixed-rate payment stream for a floating-rate stream computed on a common notional principal, with the fixed rate determined at inception such that the contract's initial value is zero.

Mechanism

The swap works because the two legs answer different questions about the same money: one locks a cost, the other tracks the market. Netting is what keeps principal out of it — if both sides paid gross on the same notional, the principal flows would cancel anyway. What remains is a pure exposure to the path of the floating reference.

In practice

Who uses it
Corporate borrowers, banks managing asset-liability mismatch, pension funds hedging discount-rate risk, and rates desks taking directional or curve views.
Why it matters
It converts a fixed cost into a floating one or the reverse, without retiring debt, reissuing bonds or moving any principal.
Limitations
The hedge is only as good as the match between the swap's reference, reset dates and day count and those of the exposure; residual basis remains even when the notional is exact.

Common mistakes

  • Calling a swap a hedge when its floating reference differs from the loan's.
  • Ignoring that the position's own market value swings with the curve.
  • Confusing the swap rate with a forecast of future short rates.

Examples

market

A borrower with a floating-rate loan pays fixed and receives floating on a matching notional and schedule. The received floating offsets what the loan charges, leaving a fixed net cost even though the loan itself was never renegotiated.

numeric

On a notional of 50 million, the fixed leg is 3.2% and the floating reference fixed at 3.9% for a 91-day period on a 360-day basis. The fixed payer receives about 88,500 net for that period.

Given
  • N = 50,000,000
  • r_fixed = 3.2% a year
  • r_float for the period = 3.9% a year
  • d = 91 days, basis B = 360
Steps
  1. Accrual factor = 91 / 360 = 0.252778
  2. Rate difference = 0.039 - 0.032 = 0.007
  3. Net = 50,000,000 x 0.007 x 0.252778 = 88,472
Net payment to the fixed payer is about 88,472 for the period

Actual/360 is used here because it is common on floating money-market legs; the fixed leg of a real swap often uses a different day count, which changes the figure.

Mathematical foundation

Procedure

For each payment date, take the floating rate that was fixed at the start of that period, subtract the fixed rate, multiply by the notional and by the period's day-count fraction. A positive result is paid to the fixed payer, a negative one by them. Repeat for every date on the schedule; nobody ever pays the notional.

Worked example

Given
  • N = 50,000,000
  • r_fixed = 0.032
  • r_float = 0.039 for the period
  • d = 91, B = 360
Steps
  1. d / B = 91 / 360 = 0.252778
  2. r_float - r_fixed = 0.007
  3. 50,000,000 x 0.007 = 350,000
  4. 350,000 x 0.252778 = 88,472
Net = +88,472 to the fixed payer for that period

Interpretation

The sign tells you who is right about rates for that one period, not over the contract's life. A fixed payer gains whenever the floating fixing lands above the swap rate, which is also why the position's mark-to-market tracks the whole forward curve rather than today's rate alone.

Assumptions

  • Single-currency, fixed-for-floating swap with matching payment dates on both legs; a basis swap or a cross-currency swap does not net this simply.
  • The floating rate for each period is already fixed, so the formula gives a realised payment, not a valuation.
  • Day-count basis and reset conventions are contractual: Actual/360, Actual/365 and 30/360 all give different accruals from the same rates.
Limits of the formula
  • This is the cash flow on one date, not the value of the swap. Valuation requires discounting every remaining expected net payment on a curve, and discounting conventions themselves changed after the move away from interbank offered rates.
  • It ignores collateral and clearing effects, which alter the economics of a cleared swap relative to an uncollateralised bilateral one.

Swap Contract

The interest-rate swap is one species of swap contract, the fixed-for-floating one on a single currency. Currency, commodity and total-return swaps share the structure but exchange entirely different legs.
