Non-Fungible Token

Simple definition

A token where each unit is distinct and not interchangeable. It records who holds a particular numbered item; what that item entitles you to depends on the contract and any separate agreement.

Professional definition

A token standard in which each identifier is unique, so balances are enumerated rather than summed. The on-chain record proves control of the token, not ownership of whatever it points to: media and metadata usually live off-chain, and legal rights such as copyright or licence terms are not transferred by the transfer itself unless a separate agreement says so.

Technical definition

Uniqueness is implemented as a mapping from token identifier to holder, which makes every unit its own market with its own last trade and no common depth. Price discovery is therefore sparse and floor-based rather than continuous. Two further gaps matter: the asset referenced may be a URL that can change or stop resolving, and the rights implied by holding are governed by contract and copyright law, not by the token standard. Documented fraud in this segment includes fake collections, wash trading between related addresses and promotion of non-existent projects.

Academic definition

A class of distinguishable, non-interchangeable ledger entries whose transfer conveys control of a unique identifier, while the legal and informational content associated with that identifier remains external to the ledger.

Mechanism

Fungible markets aggregate every unit into one book, so a single price serves them all. Enumerated tokens cannot pool that way: each identifier trades on its own, so the market reports a floor and a scatter of individual sales instead of a continuous quote. That is why valuations are fragile and why a thin collection can show a high last price with no bid behind it.

In practice

Who uses it
Collectors and creators, marketplaces and royalty engines, game and ticketing developers, and analysts separating genuine sales from trades between related addresses.
Why it matters
Enumerated tokens let a chain record a specific claim, membership or item, which fungible balances cannot express.
Limitations
Holding the token is not holding the underlying work or any licence to it. Liquidity is per item, metadata may be off-chain and mutable, and reported volume can include wash trading.

Common mistakes

  • Treating the token as transfer of copyright in the artwork.
  • Valuing a holding at a collection floor that no bid supports.
  • Ignoring where the referenced file is actually stored.

Examples

numeric

A collection of 10,000 items shows a floor of 2 units. Multiplying gives an implied 20,000 units, but only a handful are listed and the bid side may be a few items deep. The implied figure is arithmetic, not value.

Given
  • collection size = 10,000 items
  • lowest asking price (floor) = 2 units of account
  • number of live bids unknown
Steps
  1. 10,000 x 2 = 20,000 units of account implied
  2. the floor is an ask, not a bid, so it is a price nobody has paid
implied 20,000; realisable value unknown and almost certainly lower

Hypothetical figures. Floor times supply is a widely quoted convention and a poor estimate of what a collection could be sold for.

everyday

Buying a numbered print does not make you the copyright holder of the image. The token is closer to the numbered certificate than to the rights in the work, unless a licence explicitly comes with it.

Commonly confused with

Token Unlock

An unlock releases fungible units into tradable supply. A non-fungible token has no interchangeable supply at all: each identifier is its own market, so there is nothing to unlock in that sense.
