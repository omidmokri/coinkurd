Head and Shoulders

Technical Analysis

Professional

Simple definition

A three-peak shape in which the middle peak is the highest. A line drawn under the two dips between the peaks is the level traders watch.

Professional definition

A reversal shape: three successive peaks with the middle one highest, and the two intervening troughs joined by a neckline. Confirmation is conventionally a close through the neckline, with a measured target equal to the head-to-neckline height projected from the break. The inverse form mirrors it at a bottom.

Technical definition

Detection needs four independent parameters: a swing rule for peaks and troughs, a tolerance for how equal the shoulders must be, a slope tolerance for the neckline, and a confirmation rule at the break. Any published statistic for the pattern is a statistic about one setting of those four. The measured-move target is a drawing convention taken from the pattern's own height, not an expected value.

Academic definition

A three-extremum reversal configuration in which the central extremum exceeds both flanking ones, with a boundary fitted through the intervening extrema serving as the confirmation level.

Mechanism

The shape summarises a sequence in which each attempt to extend the trend failed sooner than the last, and the final attempt failed before reaching the previous extreme. The neckline collects the lows that held during that deterioration, so stops and resting orders stack just beyond it. Concentrated order flow at one level explains abrupt breaks; it does not establish the reversal claim.

In practice

Who uses it
Discretionary chart analysts, and developers building pattern detectors with explicit swing and tolerance parameters.
Why it matters
It packages a trend-deterioration sequence, one trigger level and an invalidation above the right shoulder into a single structure.
Limitations
Out-of-sample evidence is weak, the drawing is subjective, the neckline can be redrawn after the fact, and the measured move is a convention rather than a measured expectation.

Common mistakes

  • Redrawing the neckline after the break to keep the pattern valid.
  • Quoting a success rate without the detection parameters behind it.
  • Trading the shape with no volume or higher-timeframe context.

Examples

chart

A rally makes a high at 112, a higher high at 120, then a third peak that stalls at 111. The two intervening lows sit near 100, and that line becomes the level every participant is watching.

numeric

With the head at 120, the neckline at 100 and the right shoulder at 112, the conventional measured target on a break below 100 is 80, and the structure is invalidated on a close back above 112.

Given
  • head = 120
  • neckline at the break = 100
  • right shoulder = 112
  • convention: project the head-to-neckline height from the break
Steps
  1. pattern height = 120 - 100 = 20
  2. measured target = 100 - 20 = 80
  3. invalidation on a close back above the right shoulder at 112
Measured target 80, invalidation near 112.

The projection comes from the pattern's own geometry. No source in this registry attaches a hit rate or an expected value to it.

Commonly confused with

Double Top and Double Bottom

A double top has two extremes at a similar level. A head and shoulders has three, with the middle one clearly beyond the other two.
