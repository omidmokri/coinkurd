Non-farm payrolls

Simple definition

A monthly count of how many paid jobs an economy's employers added or cut, excluding farms. It is a job count taken from payroll records, not a survey of people.

Professional definition

Net change in payroll employment from an establishment survey of employers, excluding agriculture and some self-employment categories. Released on a fixed calendar with average hours and earnings, and revised in the following two releases as more employer reports arrive.

Technical definition

A sample-based estimate of employment levels from employer records, benchmarked periodically to administrative counts. Because the unit is the job rather than the person, someone holding two jobs is counted twice, and the series can diverge for months from a household-survey employment measure. Seasonal adjustment is large relative to the headline net change, and the birth-death adjustment for new and closing firms is modelled rather than observed.

Academic definition

An establishment-survey estimate of the net change in dependent employment, excluding the agricultural sector, benchmarked to administrative records and seasonally adjusted.

Mechanism

The number markets react to is a net difference between two large estimated levels, so a small percentage error in either produces a large error in the headline. That is why revisions are routine and why the three-month average is the more honest read of the trend than any single month.

In practice

Who uses it
Rates and currency desks, macro funds, and any trader holding risk through a scheduled labour-market release.
Why it matters
It is one of the highest-impact scheduled macro releases, so the gap between the print and the consensus expectation moves rate expectations within seconds.
Limitations
It is an estimate of a net change with wide confidence intervals, is revised twice, and says nothing about job quality, hours or pay beyond the companion series.

Common mistakes

  • Trading the headline without the revisions to prior months.
  • Ignoring that the figure is seasonally adjusted and modelled.
  • Treating payrolls and household employment as one series.

Examples

numeric

A first estimate reports 185,000 jobs added. Two months later it is revised to 140,000 and the prior month is cut by 30,000. The three-month average, not the original print, is what the trend was.

Given
  • initial print month 3 = +185,000
  • revised month 3 = +140,000
  • months 1 and 2 after revision = +120,000 and +110,000
Steps
  1. three-month sum = 120,000 + 110,000 + 140,000 = 370,000
  2. average = 370,000 / 3
three-month average = about +123,300 jobs per month

All figures are invented to show how revisions change the read. They are not published data.

market

Payrolls print far above expectations while the unemployment rate also rises, because more people entered the labour force than the new jobs absorbed. Rate markets have to decide which half of the report to trade.

Commonly confused with

Unemployment rate

Payrolls are a count of jobs from employer records; the unemployment rate is a share of the labour force from a household survey. They are different instruments and routinely tell different stories.
