Exchange Flows

Simple definition

How much of a coin moves onto and off exchanges. Coins arriving are often read as supply heading towards sale, and coins leaving as holders moving to self-custody.

Professional definition

A family of on-chain metrics counting value transferred into and out of address sets labelled as belonging to exchanges, usually published as inflow, outflow, netflow and exchange balance. Every figure is only as good as the labelling behind it, which no provider can verify completely.

Technical definition

Labels come from clustering heuristics and voluntary disclosure, so they are incomplete and go stale as venues rotate wallets. Internal movements between an exchange's own hot and cold wallets can register as flows unless explicitly netted, and providers differ on whether they are. Custodians, exchange-traded products and over-the-counter desks move coins for reasons unconnected to any intention to trade. Netflow is a difference of two noisy estimates, so small values sit inside measurement error.

Academic definition

On-chain measures of value transferred between labelled exchange address sets and the remainder of the network, whose interpretation as supply-side pressure rests on unverifiable assumptions about the economic intent behind address activity.

Mechanism

The reasoning is behavioural: coins must usually sit at a venue before they can be sold there, so arrivals are treated as a leading indicator of supply. The step that fails is attribution. The chain shows a transfer between address sets; it never shows who owns either set or why the coins moved, and the labels standing in for owners are guesses maintained by hand.

In practice

Who uses it
On-chain research desks, data providers maintaining address labels, and discretionary traders who use flow prints as context rather than as signals.
Why it matters
It is one of the few views of position movement that exists before an order reaches a book, which is why it attracts attention despite its flaws.
Limitations
Attribution is the binding constraint. Exchange-address clustering is incomplete, internal transfers contaminate the series, and intent is never observable.

Common mistakes

  • Reading a single large inflow print as imminent selling.
  • Comparing exchange balance series across providers with different label sets.
  • Treating a small netflow as a signal when it is inside labelling error.

Examples

numeric

Labelled addresses receive 12,500 units in a day and send 9,000. Netflow is positive 3,500, which is 0.7 percent of a labelled balance of 500,000 — well inside the error of an incomplete label set.

Given
  • inflow I = 12,500 units (illustrative)
  • outflow O = 9,000 units
  • labelled exchange balance = 500,000 units
Steps
  1. NF = 12,500 - 9,000 = 3,500
  2. 3,500 / 500,000 = 0.007
netflow +3,500 units, or 0.7% of the labelled balance

Illustrative figures. Both inflow and outflow are estimates over an incomplete address set, so their difference inherits both errors.

crypto

A venue moves coins from cold storage to a new hot wallet. Trackers that have not labelled the new address record a large outflow, and the same event appears as an inflow once the label is added. Nothing left the venue.

Mathematical foundation

NF_t = I_t - O_t, \qquad B_t = B_{t-1} + NF_t

Variables

NF_tNetflow over period t: inflow minus outflowcoin units
I_tValue transferred into labelled exchange addresses during tcoin units
O_tValue transferred out of those addresses during tcoin units
B_tLabelled exchange balance at the end of tcoin units

Procedure

Fix an address set labelled as exchange-controlled and a period. Sum value received by that set and value sent from it, decide explicitly whether transfers inside the set are excluded, and subtract. Carry the netflow onto the previous balance. Publish the labelling and netting rules with the number.

Worked example

Given
  • I = 12,500 units
  • O = 9,000 units
  • B previous = 500,000 units
Steps
  1. NF = 12,500 - 9,000 = 3,500
  2. B = 500,000 + 3,500 = 503,500
NF = +3,500 units; labelled balance = 503,500 units

Interpretation

The result is an accounting identity over a labelled set, not evidence of intent. A positive netflow means more value arrived at those addresses than left them; it does not establish that anyone intends to sell.

Assumptions

  • The address set genuinely corresponds to the venues named, which depends on clustering heuristics and disclosures that are incomplete and age quickly.
  • Providers differ on whether transfers between an exchange's own wallets are netted out and on the aggregation window, so series are not interchangeable.
  • A transfer is assumed to be economically meaningful; custodial, product and administrative movements break that assumption.
Limits of the formula
  • Netflow subtracts two noisy estimates, so small readings are dominated by labelling error.
  • The metric cannot distinguish a deposit intended for sale from one intended for collateral, lending or custody.
  • It measures movement between address sets, not trading, so it says nothing about executed volume or price.

Commonly confused with

Trading Volume

Volume counts executed trades on a venue. Exchange flows count on-chain transfers to and from that venue's addresses. A coin can change hands many times inside a venue without a single on-chain movement.
