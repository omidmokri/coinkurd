Swing Point

Simple definition

A high with lower highs on both sides of it, or a low with higher lows on both sides. It is the turning point that every other structure label is measured from.

Professional definition

A local extreme identified by a fractal rule: a bar whose high exceeds the highs of the k bars before and the k bars after it is a swing high; the mirror condition defines a swing low. Everything in swing-based price action — trend labels, structure breaks, ranges — is built on this one definition.

Technical definition

Formally, bar i is a swing high at lookback k when H_i is greater than H_j for every j in the 2k bars surrounding it. Three choices make the rule precise and are rarely stated: k, whether the inequality is strict (equal highs otherwise produce no pivot), and whether highs or closes are compared. A pivot cannot be confirmed until k bars after it prints, so any live label is provisional and can be withdrawn.

Academic definition

A local extremum of a price series defined by a symmetric fractal condition over a fixed lookback window, used as the reference point for rule-based annotations of market structure.

Mechanism

A swing point is not a market event, it is a filter. Raising k removes small wiggles and leaves fewer, larger turns; lowering it produces many pivots, most of them noise. Because the right-hand side of the window lies in the future, the filter is causal only with a delay of k bars, which is where most repainting in structure indicators comes from.

In practice

Who uses it
Price-action traders, and anyone coding trend, structure-break or zone logic, where the swing rule is the first thing the code must fix.
Why it matters
It converts the eye's sense of a turning point into one explicit, testable condition, so two runs of the same backtest label the same chart identically.
Limitations
The label is parameter-dependent and arrives k bars late. No peer-reviewed evidence shows that any particular k carries predictive value.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing k after looking at the chart.
  • Treating an unconfirmed live pivot as settled.
  • Mixing k values between the indicator and the strategy.

Examples

market

On a daily chart with k set to 3, a cluster of three small reversals inside one week produces no swing points at all, while the single high that ended the move is labelled once three later bars have closed below it.

numeric

Five bars print highs of 104, 107, 110, 106, 105. With k = 2 the third bar is a swing high, confirmed only at the close of the fifth. With k = 3 a sixth bar at 112 would cancel the label entirely.

Given
  • highs: bar1 104, bar2 107, bar3 110, bar4 106, bar5 105
  • k = 2, strict inequality, highs compared
Steps
  1. left side: 110 > 107 and 110 > 104
  2. right side: 110 > 106 and 110 > 105
  3. both sides satisfied, so bar3 is a swing high
  4. confirmation date = bar3 + k = bar5
Bar 3 is a swing high at k = 2, dated two bars late.

With k = 3 the condition also needs bar 6; a higher bar 6 removes the pivot. The same data yields different structure under different k.

Mathematical foundation

Procedure

Fix k, the comparison series (highs and lows, or closes) and whether the inequality is strict. For each bar, compare its high with the highs of the k bars on each side; mark a swing high when it exceeds all of them. Mirror the test on lows for swing lows. Record the confirmation bar as i + k, and use only confirmed pivots in any backtest.

Worked example

Given
  • highs: 104, 107, 110, 106, 105 on bars 1 to 5
  • k = 2
  • strict inequality on highs
Steps
  1. test bar 3: left neighbours 107 and 104, both below 110
  2. right neighbours 106 and 105, both below 110
  3. all 2k = 4 comparisons pass
  4. confirmation bar = 3 + 2 = 5
Bar 3 is a confirmed swing high as of bar 5.

Interpretation

The output is a label, not a forecast: this bar was the local extreme of a window of 2k + 1 bars. Its only content is geometric, and it is known only k bars after the fact.

Assumptions

  • k is fixed in advance and identical everywhere in the system. Different teachers use different k, and no value is standard.
  • The inequality is strict as written, so two bars with identical highs produce no pivot. Non-strict variants exist and give a different pivot set.
  • Highs and lows are compared. Variants comparing closes, or requiring the pivot to be followed by a structure break, are also in use.
  • Right-side confirmation means the label is unavailable in real time for k bars; using it earlier is lookahead.
Limits of the formula
  • Changing k changes the entire downstream structure: trend labels, breaks of structure and zones all move.
  • On a chart with long flat ranges and repeated equal extremes, the strict rule can return almost no pivots.
  • The definition is purely descriptive. It identifies where a turn happened; it contains no claim about where the next one will.

Commonly confused with

Pivot Points

Pivot points are levels computed arithmetically from the previous session's high, low and close. A swing point is a bar selected by a fractal comparison with its neighbours. Same word, unrelated constructions.
