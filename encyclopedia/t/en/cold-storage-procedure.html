Simple definition

The written steps for setting up, storing and recovering an offline wallet: generate the key on the device, write the phrase on paper, keep copies apart, and test recovery before funding it.

Professional definition

The operational discipline around cold keys rather than the device itself. It states how the key is generated, how the recovery phrase is recorded and separated, who may reach each copy, how a transaction is signed offline, and when recovery is rehearsed. Written procedure is what lets cold storage survive illness, travel, staff changes and time.

Technical definition

A workable procedure fixes six things: generation on a device bought from the manufacturer or an authorised seller, with the phrase displayed only on that device; a durable physical record, never a photo, a cloud note, a password manager export or a spreadsheet; geographically separated copies with a written access rule; a check that the phrase restores to the same addresses before any funding; a signing routine in which address and amount are read on the device screen; and a scheduled drill that restores from the backup alone.

Academic definition

A documented control framework governing key generation, backup custody, offline signing and recovery verification for assets held outside any network-connected signing environment.

Mechanism

Cold keys fail through process far more often than through cryptography: a phrase written once and never verified, a device replaced without a drill, a single holder unreachable. Procedure turns a one-off setup into a repeatable control with an owner and a test, which is the only way the design outlives the people who built it.

In practice

Who uses it
Treasury and operations teams, funds, DAOs, and individuals holding a long-term balance they could not replace.
Why it matters
It turns the security property of a device into a property of the organisation, including after staff, hardware and homes change.
Limitations
Procedure cannot cover coercion or a signature the holder approved in error, and a procedure nobody rehearses provides no protection at all.

Common mistakes

  • Photographing the recovery phrase or typing it into any file.
  • Keeping every backup copy in one building.
  • Funding the wallet before a restore from the phrase alone has worked.

Examples

numeric

Three full copies of one recovery phrase tolerate two losses, but each copy alone is total access, so there are three places where one theft is enough. A two-of-three multisig reverses that: one loss tolerated, two keys needed to steal.

Given
  • Design A: one recovery phrase, 3 identical written copies in 3 locations
  • Design B: 2-of-3 multisig, 3 independent keys in 3 locations
Steps
  1. A: losses tolerated = 2; locations where a single theft gives full access = 3
  2. B: losses tolerated = 1; keys an attacker must obtain = 2
A optimises against loss, B against theft; neither does both

Copying a phrase raises redundancy and theft exposure at the same time. Which design fits depends on whether loss or theft is the more credible threat for that holder, and that is a judgement, not a formula.

security

The annual drill: wipe a spare device, restore from the written phrase only, check the first addresses match, sign a 1-unit test transaction, then wipe the spare again. Anything that fails here would have failed in an emergency.

Commonly confused with

Cold and Hot Wallet

Cold and hot classify how exposed a key is. The procedure is what people actually do about it, and it is where most cold setups fail.

The six-point checklist is this encyclopedia's own synthesis of standard practice, not a published standard, which is why the claim type is editorial-recommendation; no custody standard in the source registry prescribes it. Three statements in it are not negotiable and must not be softened: the device is bought from the manufacturer or an authorised seller, the recovery phrase is generated and displayed on the device itself, and the phrase is never photographed or placed in any file, cloud note or spreadsheet. No number of copies or locations is recommended, because that trade-off depends on whether loss or theft is the more credible threat.
